title: "H.R.1834 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.1834](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834), the House bill extending the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits (introduced as the Breaking the Gridlock Act). Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.1834?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026, the bill restores larger Affordable Care Act (ACA) premium tax credits for 2026–2028. These help eligible people pay monthly bills for insurance bought through the ACA marketplaces. “Congress allowed the enhancements to expire on January 1.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/setting-the-record-straight-on-premium-tax-credit-enhancements" title="Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, “Setting the Record Straight on Premium Tax Credit Enhancements | Center on Budget and Policy Priorities”, January 12, 2026">[1]</a> CBO describes an “extension through 2028 of the premium tax credit structure” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a>, applying to “taxable years beginning after December 31, 2025.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[3]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It protects people who would otherwise lack insurance from untreated illness and medical bills.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Assuming enactment in January 2026, CBO projects that the bill would increase the number insured by “0.1 million in 2026, 3.0 million in 2027, 4.0 million in 2028, and 1.1 million in 2029, relative to current law.” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Insurance makes needed care more accessible: “people who are uninsured get far less needed care and live sicker, shorter lives.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/explainer/2025/feb/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-aca-health-plans" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For many women, ACA coverage provides access to “preventive services like mammograms, Pap tests, and prenatal care.” <a href="https://nationalpartnership.org/trumps-health-care-policies-recipe-for-aca-open-enrollment-disaster/" title="nationalpartnership.org, analysis, November 3, 2025">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A randomized study of IRS letters encouraging insurance enrollment found increased coverage and “reduced mortality among middle-aged adults over the same time period.” <a href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/136/1/1/5911132" title="The Quarterly Journal of Economics, Oxford University Press; full author manuscript hosted by Stanford Law School, “Health Insurance and Mortality: Experimental Evidence from Taxpayer Outreach”, 2020-09-24 (first published online; journal issue February 2021)">[6]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Coverage also “protects people against the health and financial risks of accidents or unexpected illnesses, and it helps people with chronic health needs afford care.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/setting-the-record-straight-on-premium-tax-credit-enhancements" title="Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, “Setting the Record Straight on Premium Tax Credit Enhancements | Center on Budget and Policy Priorities”, January 12, 2026">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill also increases public coverage: CBO projects “0.4 million more people would be enrolled in Medicaid and the Children’s Health Insurance Program” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a> combined in 2028.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A randomized Oregon study found that “Medicaid coverage led to a reduction in financial strain from medical costs, according to a number of self-reported measures.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3701298/" title="New England Journal of Medicine; full author manuscript hosted by PubMed Central, “The Oregon Experiment — Effects of Medicaid on Clinical Outcomes”, 2013-05-02">[7]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Bringing more people into coverage protects those exposed to untreated illness, early death and financial hardship. Protecting vulnerable people gives [NAME] a reason to favor the bill.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Access to treatment also supports work and study. One interviewed enrollee received mental health treatment that “kept her on track as she juggled multiple jobs in the gig economy and completed classes to become a certified nursing assistant.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/in-their-own-words-marketplace-enrollees-would-struggle-to-afford-premium-hikes-if-congress" title="cbpp.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Keeping treatment within reach helps stop illness from blocking work and education, advancing the equal opportunities [NAME] values.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Replacement coverage matters when people lose jobs. Earlier enhanced credits “enabled many who lost ESI to afford new coverage.” <a href="https://www.bakerinstitute.org/research/lessons-past-recessions-health-coverage-today" title="bakerinstitute.org, analysis, May 29, 2025">[9]</a> ESI means insurance provided through a job.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Some families have few public alternatives. For one Texas family buying ACA coverage, “In other states, her kids might qualify for free or low-cost health insurance through Medicaid or the Children’s Health Insurance Program, but in Texas, their income is too high.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/in-their-own-words-marketplace-enrollees-would-struggle-to-afford-premium-hikes-if-congress" title="cbpp.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Some veterans also need this alternative: Veterans Affairs “services can be inaccessible for those who do not live near VA facilities.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/Over-600,000-Nonelderly-Veterans-Would-Have-Subsidized-Marketplace-Coverage-in-2026-under-Enhanced-Premium-Tax-Credits.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, September 2025">[10]</a> Urban Institute research found that “the expiration of enhanced PTCs would lead to lower enrollment” <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/Over-600,000-Nonelderly-Veterans-Would-Have-Subsidized-Marketplace-Coverage-in-2026-under-Enhanced-Premium-Tax-Credits.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, September 2025">[10]</a> in subsidized marketplace coverage among veterans.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring affordable coverage protects people exposed by job loss, narrow public insurance rules or gaps in veterans’ care, reinforcing [NAME]’s reason to favor it.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Differences between the studies and this bill do not defeat the PRO’s link from added coverage to protection.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> CBO’s coverage forecast already assumes January 2026 enactment and includes “3.0 million in 2027, 4.0 million in 2028, and 1.1 million in 2029, relative to current law.” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO uses the Oregon study for financial protection under Medicaid, and CBO also forecasts “0.4 million more people would be enrolled in Medicaid and the Children’s Health Insurance Program” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a> combined in 2028.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The mortality study warns that its estimate “may not reflect the effect of coverage for the remainder of the uninsured population.” <a href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/136/1/1/5911132" title="The Quarterly Journal of Economics, Oxford University Press; full author manuscript hosted by Stanford Law School, “Health Insurance and Mortality: Experimental Evidence from Taxpayer Outreach”, 2020-09-24 (first published online; journal issue February 2021)">[6]</a> That limits how far its estimate can be applied; it does not establish that added coverage provides no protection.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut the inference from study differences and delayed enrollment to failure of the protection argument. The work-and-study example supplies no estimate of this bill’s effect, but that does not defeat the separate coverage argument.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It leaves households more money for food, housing and other basic needs.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The credits replace money households must otherwise spend themselves: “PTCs cover premium amounts that people would otherwise be required to pay out of pocket to keep their coverage.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/setting-the-record-straight-on-premium-tax-credit-enhancements" title="Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, “Setting the Record Straight on Premium Tax Credit Enhancements | Center on Budget and Policy Priorities”, January 12, 2026">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The help reaches people with small budgets: “With the enhanced subsidies, most enrollees at the lowest income level — under 150 percent of poverty — pay little or nothing for their plan.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/explainer/2025/feb/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-aca-health-plans" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> An interviewed enrollee explained that higher insurance bills would require him to “cut costs elsewhere and that's now digging into basic necessities, like, food, car, gas, rent.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/in-their-own-words-marketplace-enrollees-would-struggle-to-afford-premium-hikes-if-congress" title="cbpp.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Lower insurance payments leave more of these households’ budgets for basic needs. Protecting financially vulnerable people from those sacrifices gives [NAME] a reason to favor the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Paying credits to insurers and finding some existing zero-premium plans do not disprove savings for households whose payments fall.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The payment passage says the transfer “reduces the premium charged to the household.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48290" title="Bernadette Fernandez, “Enhanced Premium Tax Credit and 2026 Exchange Premiums: Frequently Asked Questions”, 2025-12-10">[11]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The zero-premium source says only that “some enrollees continue to have access to plans that don't have any premiums after their premium subsidy is applied.” <a href="https://www.healthinsurance.org/faqs/whos-getting-zero-premium-health-insurance-plans/" title="Louise Norris, “Who&#x27;s getting zero-premium health insurance plans?”, 2026-01-23">[12]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut this response. Neither premise answers the PRO’s claim about households that must otherwise spend more to keep their coverage. The PRO does not count all new enrollment as money freed from an earlier premium bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> It helps insured people choose plans with lower bills when they need treatment.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Larger credits make such plans easier to buy: “While enhanced PTCs do not directly alter patient cost sharing, they allow people to enroll in or switch to plans that have lower cost-sharing obligations.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/explainer/2025/feb/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-aca-health-plans" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[4]</a> These are the charges patients pay when they receive care.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Treatment costs block needed care. With fewer financial resources, “women are already more likely than men to forego or delay health care due to cost” <a href="https://nwlc.org/resource/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-are-critical-for-women-and-lgbtq-people/" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center, “Enhanced Premium Tax Credits Are Critical for Women and LGBTQ+ People”, 2025-04-10">[13]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Helping people buy plans with lower treatment bills reduces a barrier between having insurance and receiving care. Protecting people with little money and substantial health needs gives [NAME] a reason to favor the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Slow plan switching does not show that the bill’s help with buying plans with lower treatment charges is blocked.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO expressly says the credits “do not directly alter patient cost sharing” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/explainer/2025/feb/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-aca-health-plans" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[4]</a>; its claim concerns making other plans easier to buy.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The California trial found that “The emails led to a statistically significant 3.1–percentage point (95% CI, 2.6-3.6 percentage points) increase in CSR silver 94 enrollment” <a href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2024.0324" title="Marina Lovchikova, Andrew Feher, Langou Lian, “Health Plan Switching and Health Care Utilization: A Randomized Clinical Trial”, 2024-03-29">[14]</a>. These plans covered 94 percent of medical costs on average.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The trial concludes that “with access to more affordable health care, individuals are more likely to visit practitioners.” <a href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2024.0324" title="Marina Lovchikova, Andrew Feher, Langou Lian, “Health Plan Switching and Health Care Utilization: A Randomized Clinical Trial”, 2024-03-29">[14]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut the claim that selection barriers block the gain. The trial shows that eligibility does not ensure switching, but the PRO does not claim automatic switching.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> It reduces coverage barriers tied to discrimination and unequal earnings.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A CBPP analysis identifies an unequal effect of losing the enhanced credits: “Black and Latino people will disproportionately lose coverage, which will increase existing racial disparities in health insurance coverage.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/blog/in-their-own-words-marketplace-enrollees-would-struggle-to-afford-premium-hikes-if-congress" title="cbpp.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Unequal pay makes coverage harder to afford: “women, particularly women of color, and LGBTQ+ people are more likely to have lower incomes due to gender and racial pay discrimination and overrepresentation in low-paid jobs” <a href="https://nwlc.org/resource/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-are-critical-for-women-and-lgbtq-people/" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center, “Enhanced Premium Tax Credits Are Critical for Women and LGBTQ+ People”, 2025-04-10">[13]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The enhanced credits reach these groups. When introduced in 2021, “over 200,000 LGBTQ+ enrollees were eligible for zero-premium plans.” <a href="https://nwlc.org/resource/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-are-critical-for-women-and-lgbtq-people/" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center, “Enhanced Premium Tax Credits Are Critical for Women and LGBTQ+ People”, 2025-04-10">[13]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring help with insurance costs reduces the effect of unequal earnings on access to care and counters coverage losses that widen racial gaps. More equal access for people facing these disadvantages gives [NAME] a reason grounded in equal opportunity and just treatment.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Excluding some people does not disprove gains for eligible members of the groups the PRO names.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited 2026 restriction “eliminates subsidized ACA marketplace coverage eligibility for all lawfully present immigrants with incomes under 100 percent of the federal poverty level.” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/when-do-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-acts-health-care-provisions-go-into-effect/" title="americanprogress.org, analysis, August 18, 2025">[15]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> That restriction concerns a defined group; it does not establish that the eligible women, Black people, Latino people or LGBTQ+ people discussed by the PRO receive no added help.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut the inference from an unchanged exclusion to failure of the claimed reduction in coverage barriers. The 2021 count is not a 2026 count, but the PRO labels it as a 2021 count.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> It replaces an abrupt income cutoff with help tied to income and insurance costs.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Without the extension, eligibility ends at 400 percent of the federal poverty level: “the tax credit falls suddenly to zero when income crosses this line.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> With enhanced credits, help remains limited by income, but “it just phases out gradually rather than dropping off suddenly.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The restored credit caps eligible households’ payments for the benchmark plan—the plan used to set the credit—at “8.5% of income” <a href="https://bipartisanpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Enhanced-PTC-Brief-D4.pdf" title="bipartisanpolicy.org, analysis, October 2025">[17]</a>. Credits limit payments for “a specific plan, called a benchmark” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/aug/health-insurance-tax-credits-unexpected-effectiveness-policies-support" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, August 13, 2025">[18]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> High prices make the cutoff especially harmful. In Urban’s 2026 example, a 60-year-old in Cheyenne earning $65,000 “would face a premium of $26,180—40 percent of their income.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Enhanced%20Premium%20Tax%20Credits%20Direct%20Almost%20All%20of%20Their%20Benefit%20to%20Lower-%20and%20Moderate-Income%20Americans_1218.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, December 2025">[19]</a> At 8.5 percent, the annual payment for benchmark coverage would be $5,525.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cutoff falls especially heavily on “older people, those in high-premium areas (which are heavily rural areas), and small business owners.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring income-based help protects people from high bills tied to age or location. A gradual phaseout also treats people with nearly identical incomes more equally, giving [NAME] a reason grounded in fair treatment.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Credits are paid before final annual income is known: “The credit amounts then need to be reconciled with actual income at year’s end when enrollees file their federal taxes.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/explainer/2025/feb/enhanced-premium-tax-credits-aca-health-plans" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, February 18, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cutoff creates the risk that “a small income change triggers a large tax liability.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Removing that cutoff also protects households from a sudden repayment bill caused by barely crossing the income line.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Differences in assets do not refute the claim that removing an abrupt income cutoff treats people near it more equally.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Under the cutoff, “the tax credit falls suddenly to zero when income crosses this line.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill extends the “Rule To Allow Credit to Taxpayers Whose Household Income Exceeds 400 Percent of Poverty Line” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The draft’s asset objection does not show that this discontinuity remains when the bill extends the credit above the cutoff.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut this response. It substitutes a test of total wealth for the PRO’s comparison of nearly identical incomes. The same objection leaves the sudden repayment risk unanswered.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 6</span> It makes public help fairer for people whose work does not provide affordable insurance.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Many people must arrange coverage themselves: “people who own small businesses or are self-employed typically purchase insurance on their own.” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/congress-failure-to-extend-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-will-greatly-increase-health-insurance-costs-for-small-business-people/" title="americanprogress.org, analysis, October 28, 2025">[20]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> People with insurance from a job get a tax break for it: “the tax exclusion for employer coverage.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The marketplace credit fills a different gap: “With or without the enhancements, the PTC is available only to people without other affordable coverage options.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a> The bill restores the “Rule To Allow Credit to Taxpayers Whose Household Income Exceeds 400 Percent of Poverty Line” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring help above the cutoff reduces the disadvantage of being left to buy insurance yourself while job-based coverage receives tax help. Fairer access to public support across types of work gives [NAME] a reason to favor the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> An existing self-employed deduction does not establish that added credits fail to narrow a gap in public help.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The IRS says, “You may be able to deduct the amount you paid for health insurance” <a href="https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206" title="Internal Revenue Service, “Instructions for Form 7206 (2025)”, 2026-04-30 (publication date returned by search; instructions concern tax year 2025)">[21]</a>. This establishes a conditional deduction, not equal tax help across types of work.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO’s source directly addresses the gap: “For those beyond the cliff, the enhanced PTC is already smaller on average than the tax benefit from the tax exclusion for employer-sponsored coverage.” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/eligibility-cliff-aca-tax-credits-would-make-health-care-unaffordable-middle" title="urban.org, analysis, December 24, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Cut the claim that the deduction or the continued existence of ordinary credits defeats the comparison. <tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> Added debt puts future emergency aid under pressure.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Borrowing adds interest costs.
