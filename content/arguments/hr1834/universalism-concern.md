title: "H.R.1834 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.1834](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834), the House bill extending the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits (introduced as the Breaking the Gridlock Act). Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Concern. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected
- that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life
- that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

Doctors warned that “disruptions in care put these patients at risk of relapse into drug use, a mental health crisis, or having to stop ongoing treatment for chronic illnesses, such as cancer and diabetes” <a href="https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/fraudsters-duping-homeless-people-aca-obamacare-plans/" title="“Fraudsters Are Duping Homeless People Into Signing Up for ACA Plans They Can&#x27;t Afford”, 2023-06-13">[21]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Fraud can also leave victims owing money. Federal auditors list “repayment of APTC if income or other eligibility was misrepresented” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[7]</a>. APTC means tax credits paid to the insurer in advance.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> For excess credits that remain owed, the law starting in 2026 removes repayment caps, requiring even low-income enrollees to “repay the entirety of any excess premium tax credits that were paid to their insurer” <a href="https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/fraud-in-marketplace-enrollment-and-eligibility-five-things-to-know/" title="Kaye Pestaina, Rayna Wallace, Michelle Long, Meghan Salaga, Emma Lee, “Fraud in Marketplace Enrollment and Eligibility: Five Things to Know”, 2025-06-30 (updated 2025-07-11)">[22]</a>. The bill does not restore those caps.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> Existing controls still left gaps in late 2025. A December federal audit found that “as of September 2025 18 of our 20 fictitious applications for plan year 2025 were receiving subsidized coverage” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[7]</a>. H.R.1834's sole section, “EXTENSION OF ENHANCED PREMIUM TAX CREDIT” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[2]</a>, changes the credit's dates and adds no broker safeguards.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Restoring this level of subsidy expands an opening for sellers to profit by disrupting vulnerable people's care and exposing them to debts. Morgan's concern for their protection gives Morgan a reason to oppose that expansion without added safeguards.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Rejecting the bill would not close the opening for dishonest sellers, because free plans and the fraud both exist without the enhanced credits.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Without the bill, almost all the same people still get a free plan: “Of adults who qualified for a free silver plan with enhanced premium tax credits, almost all (98%) would still qualify for a free bronze plan without enhanced premium tax credits.” <a href="https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/higher-premium-payments-or-higher-deductibles-the-tradeoffs-aca-enrollees-face/" title="Emma Wager, Justin Lo, Cynthia Cox, “Higher Premium Payments or Higher Deductibles: The Tradeoffs ACA Enrollees Face”, 2026-01-14">[23]</a> Bronze plans have lower premiums but leave patients more of the bill.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The conviction the PRO cites was for a scheme that ran “from approximately August 2018 through approximately September 2022” <a href="https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-vns/case/united-states-v-cory-lloyd-et-al" title="“United States v. Cory Lloyd et al (Criminal Division case page)”, 2026-04-14 (page last updated)">[24]</a>. It began before the enhanced credits, which cover “tax years 2021 through 2025” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48290" title="“Enhanced Premium Tax Credit and 2026 Exchange Premiums: Frequently Asked Questions (R48290)”, 2025-12-10">[25]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Sellers preyed on homeless people even earlier: “In one case from 2015, a North Carolina agent signed up hundreds of homeless people for ACA coverage.” <a href="https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/fraudsters-duping-homeless-people-aca-obamacare-plans/" title="“Fraudsters Are Duping Homeless People Into Signing Up for ACA Plans They Can&#x27;t Afford”, 2023-06-13">[21]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The weak sign-up checks the GAO audit found also came before the enhanced credits. Its testing “suggests weaknesses have persisted since our tests in plan years 2015 through 2016” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The GAO's late-2024 fake applicants got in “using a special enrollment period for low-income applicants” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[7]</a>, a sign-up window outside the yearly season. For 2026, the 2025 budget law already “Prohibits eligibility for APTCs for Marketplace consumers who qualify for special enrollment periods based solely on their income relative to the poverty level” <a href="https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/fraud-in-marketplace-enrollment-and-eligibility-five-things-to-know/" title="Kaye Pestaina, Rayna Wallace, Michelle Long, Meghan Salaga, Emma Lee, “Fraud in Marketplace Enrollment and Eligibility: Five Things to Know”, 2025-06-30 (updated 2025-07-11)">[22]</a>. APTCs are tax credits paid in advance.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The debt risk also comes from the 2025 law, which “Removes APTC repayment limits” <a href="https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/fraud-in-marketplace-enrollment-and-eligibility-five-things-to-know/" title="Kaye Pestaina, Rayna Wallace, Michelle Long, Meghan Salaga, Emma Lee, “Fraud in Marketplace Enrollment and Eligibility: Five Things to Know”, 2025-06-30 (updated 2025-07-11)">[22]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> H.R.1834 leaves both of those rules in place. It is not an effect of H.R.1834, so Morgan's concern for the vulnerable gives no reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill extends public help to high earners while leaving uninsured adults below the poverty line outside the credit.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill restores eligibility above four times the poverty line: “Extension of Rule To Allow Credit to Taxpayers Whose Household Income Exceeds 400 Percent of Poverty Line” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[2]</a>. A December 2025 Urban Institute model projects 2026 credits for some people earning over ten times the poverty line—$156,500 for a single person or $211,500 for a couple—“averaging $3,410 per person for the year” <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Enhanced%20Premium%20Tax%20Credits%20Direct%20Almost%20All%20of%20Their%20Benefit%20to%20Lower-%20and%20Moderate-Income%20Americans_1218.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, December 2025">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The spending choice is large: “the House-passed bill would increase the deficit by more than $80 billion” <a href="https://www.aei.org/economics/would-senate-passage-of-an-aca-tax-credit-extension-matter/" title="American Enterprise Institute, “Would Senate Passage of an ACA Tax Credit Extension ...”, January 14, 2026">[27]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> At the other end of the income scale, a 2025 study estimated over 1.5 million uninsured adults in the Medicaid coverage gap. Their incomes are “too low to qualify for financial help in the ACA marketplaces” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/closing-medicaid-coverage-gap-would-provide-over-15-million-uninsured-adults-path" title="Elizabeth Zhang, “Closing Medicaid Coverage Gap Would Provide Over 1.5 Million Uninsured Adults Path to Affordable Health Coverage”, 2025-04-10">[28]</a>, and their states' Medicaid rules leave them ineligible for that public insurance too.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> This exclusion falls on people with serious needs: “Some 15 percent of adults in the coverage gap have a disability, compared to 11 percent of other adults.” <a href="https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/closing-medicaid-coverage-gap-would-provide-over-15-million-uninsured-adults-path" title="Elizabeth Zhang, “Closing Medicaid Coverage Gap Would Provide Over 1.5 Million Uninsured Adults Path to Affordable Health Coverage”, 2025-04-10">[28]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> By extending the credit's amounts and upper-income rules while keeping its lower-income floor, the bill expands public help for high earners and leaves these poorer adults excluded. Above ten times the poverty line, the PRO's own source estimates “fewer than 13,000 people nationwide” would get it <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Enhanced%20Premium%20Tax%20Credits%20Direct%20Almost%20All%20of%20Their%20Benefit%20to%20Lower-%20and%20Moderate-Income%20Americans_1218.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, December 2025">[12]</a>. Their $3,410 average is less than half what lower earners get, “compared with an average of $7,470 for those with incomes below four times the FPL” <a href="https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/Enhanced%20Premium%20Tax%20Credits%20Direct%20Almost%20All%20of%20Their%20Benefit%20to%20Lower-%20and%20Moderate-Income%20Americans_1218.pdf" title="urban.org, analysis, December 2025">[12]</a>.
