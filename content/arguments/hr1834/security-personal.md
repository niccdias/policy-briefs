**Bill:** [H.R.1834](https://web.archive.org/web/20261020233134/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834)), the House bill extending the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits (introduced as the Breaking the Gridlock Act). Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?

CBO explains that “eligibility for the advance credit is limited to people who are ineligible for affordable employment-based insurance” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029170902/https://waysandmeans.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/250902-Letter-CBO-Responds.pdf" title="“Clarifications of Marketplace Coverage and Eligibility Under Public Law 119-21 (H.R. 1) and the 2025 Marketplace Integrity and Affordability Rule”, 2025-08-25">[28]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The comparison leaves out other plans the credits can buy: “Alternatively, applying the benchmark tax credit to a gold plan can make insurance with more benefits relatively inexpensive.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261011012310/https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/mar/low-marketplace-premiums-often-reflect-high-deductibles" title="Authors John Holahan, Michael Simpson, Erik Wengle, “Low Marketplace Premiums Often Reflect High Deductibles”, 2025-03-19">[29]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The deductible experiment studied an employer that “required all of its employees to switch from an insurance plan that provided free health care to a non-linear, high deductible plan.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201352/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21632/w21632.pdf" title="Zarek C. Brot-Goldberg, Amitabh Chandra, Benjamin R. Handel, Jonathan T. Kolstad, “What Does a Deductible Do? The Impact of Cost-Sharing on Health Care Prices, Quantities, and Spending Dynamics”, 2015-10; revised 2015-11">[30]</a> It does not identify the plans workers would enter or leave because of H.R.1834.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The missing step is evidence that the extension moves workers into plans that leave them paying more for treatment than the job plans they would otherwise have. A claim about what happens if workers enter particular plans does not establish that the bill causes that change.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> The coverage shift also risks leaving people without protection against dental disease.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Employer benefits commonly include access to dental insurance. KFF reports: “37% of Marketplace Enrollees Reported Delaying or Forgoing Dental Care Due To Cost Barriers” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261003080218/https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/access-to-adult-dental-care-gets-renewed-focus-in-aca-marketplace-proposal/" title="Rayna Wallace, Michelle Long, Kaye Pestaina, “Access to Adult Dental Care Gets Renewed Focus in ACA Marketplace Proposal”, 2024-03-08; updated 2025-02-20">[31]</a>. The bill’s shift away from employer coverage therefore creates a risk of gaps in dental care. Avoiding those gaps serves [NAME]’s wish to avoid disease.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> A fall in employer medical coverage does not establish the loss of employer dental benefits needed for this path to dental disease.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Most private dental benefits are separate from medical insurance: “only about 1.2% of commercial dental benefits are integrated into medical policies.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260606154336/https://www.nadp.org/nadp-report-shows-continued-decline-in-dental-benefits-enrollment/" title="Stefanie Harris, Lauren Oakley, “NADP Report Shows Continued Decline in Dental Benefits Enrollment”, 2025-12-04">[33]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal rules allow dental benefits without employer medical coverage: “limited-scope vision or dental benefits can be provided without connection to a primary plan” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20250418053945/https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/10/01/2014-23323/amendments-to-excepted-benefits" title="“Amendments to Excepted Benefits”, 2014-10-01">[34]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Those separate benefits can coexist with Marketplace tax credits: “eligibility for excepted benefits does not preclude an individual from eligibility for a premium tax credit under section 36B of the Code” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20250418053945/https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/10/01/2014-23323/amendments-to-excepted-benefits" title="“Amendments to Excepted Benefits”, 2014-10-01">[34]</a>. Here, excepted benefits include separate dental coverage.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The 37% figure is not a measure of dental benefits lost through a change in medical insurance: “Consumers having insurance coverage does not mean that all consumers surveyed had dental coverage.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261003080218/https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/access-to-adult-dental-care-gets-renewed-focus-in-aca-marketplace-proposal/" title="Rayna Wallace, Michelle Long, Kaye Pestaina, “Access to Adult Dental Care Gets Renewed Focus in ACA Marketplace Proposal”, 2024-03-08; updated 2025-02-20">[31]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO never connects the medical coverage shift to lost dental eligibility or employer dental payments. Without that link, it has not shown a new dental bill or an added risk of dental disease caused by the extension.</li></ul></td></tr>
