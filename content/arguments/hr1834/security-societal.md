title: "H.R.1834 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.1834](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834), the House bill extending the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits (introduced as the Breaking the Gridlock Act). Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Security – Societal. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- that their country is secure and stable
- to have a strong state that can defend its citizens
- that their country protect itself against all threats

*Read "their country" as the United States.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

That moves the country closer to a debt crisis that could shake banks and pensions nationwide, which runs against Morgan's wish for a stable country.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Moody’s downgrade and CBO’s general debt warning do not establish that this extension would destabilize banks or pensions.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> CBO ties crisis risk to borrowing costs and the ability to renew loans: “Ultimately, it is the government’s cost of servicing the debt and its ability to refinance that debt that matter.” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61270" title="Congressional Budget Office, “The Long-Term Budget Outlook: 2025 to 2055”, 2025-03-27">[15]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The same CBO report says the crisis risk “appears to be low in the near term despite the large amount of federal debt” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61270" title="Congressional Budget Office, “The Long-Term Budget Outlook: 2025 to 2055”, 2025-03-27">[15]</a>. Its description of possible bank failures explains what a crisis could cause; it does not forecast those failures under H.R.1834.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Moody’s downgrade came with a stable outlook: “The stable outlook reflects balanced risks” <a href="https://ratings.moodys.com/ratings-news/443154" title="Moody&#x27;s Ratings (Moody&#x27;s Investors Service, Inc.), “Moody&#x27;s Ratings downgrades United States ratings to Aa1 from Aaa; changes outlook to stable”, 2025-05-16">[19]</a>. Moody’s also says the dollar’s global role helps the government “finance large annual fiscal deficits and refinance its large debt burden at moderate and relatively predictable costs” <a href="https://ratings.moodys.com/ratings-news/443154" title="Moody&#x27;s Ratings (Moody&#x27;s Investors Service, Inc.), “Moody&#x27;s Ratings downgrades United States ratings to Aa1 from Aaa; changes outlook to stable”, 2025-05-16">[19]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> These sources assess the whole country’s debt and ability to borrow. The PRO supplies no further link showing that this health-coverage extension would undermine that ability or threaten the financial system.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> The bill sends more money through an enrollment system that fake identities and dishonest insurance brokers already exploit.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill extends the larger-credit schedule. Under it, people earning less than 1.5 times the poverty line “became eligible for zero-premium marketplace plans under the enhanced tax credit schedule” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/aug/health-insurance-tax-credits-unexpected-effectiveness-policies-support" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, August 13, 2025">[20]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Even the Commonwealth Fund, which backs the larger credits, says $0 plans make fraud easier: “zero-premium plans do make it easier for agents and brokers to fraudulently enroll eligible individuals” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/aug/health-insurance-tax-credits-unexpected-effectiveness-policies-support" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, August 13, 2025">[20]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress's auditor, tested the system with made-up people, and nearly all got coverage: “as of September 2025 18 of our 20 fictitious applications for plan year 2025 were receiving subsidized coverage.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[21]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Ordinary people are already being hurt. The federal agency that runs the system “received approximately 275,000 complaints between January and August 2024 that consumers were enrolled in a plan or had their plan changed without their consent” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[21]</a>, and “such practices can result in harm and unexpected costs for consumers.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[21]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill would grow this system. By 2028, “6.2 million more people would be enrolled through the health insurance marketplaces” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill puts more public money behind a weak spot that criminals already use against the government and against ordinary people. A strong state that protects itself and its citizens would close that gap, not feed it.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Letting the larger credits lapse leaves $0-premium plans available; consent and eligibility checks address the fraud the PRO describes.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> KFF’s analysis of 2026 plans finds that, among adults who qualified for a free silver plan with the larger credits, “almost all (98%) would still qualify for a free bronze plan without enhanced premium tax credits” <a href="https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/higher-premium-payments-or-higher-deductibles-the-tradeoffs-aca-enrollees-face/" title="Emma Wager, Justin Lo, and Cynthia Cox; Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, “Higher Premium Payments or Higher Deductibles: The Tradeoffs ACA Enrollees Face”, 2026-01-14">[22]</a>. Rejecting the extension therefore leaves a different type of $0-premium plan available to this group.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> GAO, Congress’s auditor, warns that its fake-applicant test results “cannot be generalized to the overall enrollment population” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[21]</a>. Those approvals cannot stand in for the additional enrollment CBO expects under this bill.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing law already restricts a route used in GAO’s tests. GAO’s four 2024 applicants used “a special enrollment period for low-income applicants” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH213.htm" title="Government Accountability Office, report, December 3, 2025">[21]</a>. Since January 1, 2026, the 2025 tax-and-spending law bars credits through that route: “The OBBBA prevents individuals who enroll under the low-income special enrollment period from qualifying for premium tax credits” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/when-do-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-acts-health-care-provisions-go-into-effect/" title="americanprogress.org, analysis, August 18, 2025">[23]</a>. H.R.1834 leaves that restriction in place.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The same law requires checks before enrollment starting in 2028, within the extension period: “The OBBBA requires health insurance exchanges to verify eligibility for premium tax credits prior to enrollment, rather than allowing for retrospective determinations” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/when-do-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-acts-health-care-provisions-go-into-effect/" title="americanprogress.org, analysis, August 18, 2025">[23]</a>. H.R.1834 leaves that requirement in place too.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> CMS, the agency running the federal marketplace, reported less casework about unauthorized changes after restricting broker access in July 2024: “Casework associated with consumer reports of unauthorized plan changes has dropped by approximately 30% since the changes went into effect” <a href="https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-update-actions-prevent-unauthorized-agent-broker-marketplace-activity" title="Centers for Medicare &amp; Medicaid Services (CMS), “CMS Update on Actions to Prevent Unauthorized Agent and Broker Marketplace Activity”, 2024-10-17">[24]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The evidence supports enforcing consent and eligibility while keeping lawful coverage affordable. The PRO does not show that withholding the larger credits would close the fraud routes it identifies, or that restoring them would undo those protections.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The bill supports hospitals that communities need to withstand health emergencies.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> CBO’s estimate assumes enactment in January 2026 and projects added insurance coverage of “3.0 million in 2027, 4.0 million in 2028” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A study of rural hospital finances explains the link between lost coverage and lost services: “further reductions in revenue and increases in uncompensated care could force hospitals to reduce costs by eliminating services that would affect all members of their community” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2025/without-renewal-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-rural-hospital-revenues-will-drop-16-billion" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, November 13, 2025">[7]</a>.
