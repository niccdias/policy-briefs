title: "H.R.1834 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.1834](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1834), the House bill extending the enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits (introduced as the Breaking the Gridlock Act). Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.1834?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.1834?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Restore the larger tax credits for buying Affordable Care Act (ACA) Marketplace insurance for 2026–2028, compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026. The bill extends the rules “through 2028” and applies them to “taxable years beginning after December 31, 2025.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[1]</a> Assuming passage in January 2026, CBO estimates additional people insured: “0.1 million in 2026, 3.0 million in 2027, 4.0 million in 2028, and 1.1 million in 2029, relative to current law.” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> More affordable coverage strengthens the country’s defense against contagious disease.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Coverage helps people get care. A study of previously uninsured adults eligible for Marketplace subsidies found “decreased barriers to medical care, increased the use of outpatient services and prescription drugs”. <a href="https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2017.1390" title="Health Affairs; full author manuscript in Harvard DASH, “Effects Of The ACA’s Health Insurance Marketplaces On The Previously Uninsured: A Quasi-Experimental Analysis”, 2018-04-02 (publication); 2017-12-19 (author manuscript)">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Cost barriers impede outbreak control. Research on COVID-19 found that “financial barriers to COVID-19 care delayed diagnosis and exacerbated transmission.” <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2200536119" title="Alison P. Galvani, Alyssa S. Parpia, Abhishek Pandey, Pratha Sah, Kenneth Colón, “Universal healthcare as pandemic preparedness: The lives and costs that could have been saved during the COVID-19 pandemic”, 2022-06-13 (online); 2022-06-21 (issue)">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Finding cases helps protect others: “COVID-19 testing allows case identification, initiation of isolation, contact tracing, and prompt treatment.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971243/" title="Journal of General Internal Medicine, “COVID-19 Testing and Incidence Among Uninsured and Insured Individuals in 2020: a National Study”, 2022-02-09 (online); 2022-04 (issue)">[5]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> By making coverage affordable for more people, the bill reduces a barrier to diagnosis and care. That strengthens outbreak control and protects people beyond those receiving the credits, advancing [NAME]’s concern for national protection against threats.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The cited studies do not establish the step from added coverage to earlier diagnosis and less spread of infections.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Marketplace study reports more use of “outpatient services and prescription drugs” <a href="https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2017.1390" title="Health Affairs; full author manuscript in Harvard DASH, “Effects Of The ACA’s Health Insurance Marketplaces On The Previously Uninsured: A Quasi-Experimental Analysis”, 2018-04-02 (publication); 2017-12-19 (author manuscript)">[3]</a>. Those outcomes do not measure when contagious people get diagnosed or isolate.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The COVID-19 testing study measures insurance “at the time of interview” and whether people had “had ≥ 1 COVID-19 test” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971243/" title="Journal of General Internal Medicine, “COVID-19 Testing and Incidence Among Uninsured and Insured Individuals in 2020: a National Study”, 2022-02-09 (online); 2022-04 (issue)">[5]</a>. It does not establish that insurance came before testing, how quickly infected people were tested, or whether they isolated. The authors warn that “observational studies cannot prove causality.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8971243/" title="Journal of General Internal Medicine, “COVID-19 Testing and Incidence Among Uninsured and Insured Individuals in 2020: a National Study”, 2022-02-09 (online); 2022-04 (issue)">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The pandemic paper studies “comprehensive universal healthcare that eliminates all costs to the patient.” <a href="https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2200536119" title="Alison P. Galvani, Alyssa S. Parpia, Abhishek Pandey, Pratha Sah, Kenneth Colón, “Universal healthcare as pandemic preparedness: The lives and costs that could have been saved during the COVID-19 pandemic”, 2022-06-13 (online); 2022-06-21 (issue)">[4]</a> H.R.1834 extends the “Rules To Increase Premium Assistance Amounts” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[1]</a>. The paper does not separate the effect of premium help from removing charges for care.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> More routine care, a testing association, and a broader reform’s projected benefits do not establish that this bill changes the diagnosis and isolation behavior needed for the claimed outbreak protection.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Supporting hospital finances preserves the country’s ability to respond to disasters and attacks.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A comparison of keeping and losing the larger credits links their loss to “lower revenue for health care providers and fewer services rendered” and an “increase in uncompensated care sought by the uninsured”. Uncompensated care means care for which providers are not paid. <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/changes-health-care-spending-and-uncompensated-care-under-enhanced-tax-credit" title="urban.org, analysis, September 25, 2025">[6]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Revenue losses at rural hospitals “could force hospitals to reduce costs by eliminating services that would affect all members of their community.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2025/without-renewal-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-rural-hospital-revenues-will-drop-16-billion" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, November 13, 2025">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Small rural hospitals “are called upon to respond to a wide range of emergency situations in their communities”. Their emergency plans cover natural disasters and “Terrorism and bioterrorism events – bombings, use of biological agents such as anthrax”. <a href="https://www.flexmonitoring.org/sites/flexmonitoring.umn.edu/files/media/health_care_coalitions_cah_emergency_preparedness_2025.pdf" title="Flex Monitoring Team, “Health Care Coalitions: A Resource to Support Critical Access Hospital Emergency Preparedness Planning”, April 2024 (printed in the report)">[8]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Supporting payments for care helps preserve hospitals and services that emergency responders need. That protects the country’s response capacity during disasters and attacks, including care for residents with other insurance.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The hospital forecasts do not establish that this January bill would preserve services needed during disasters or attacks.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The rural-hospital forecast assumes a nationwide loss of coverage: “4.8 million more people uninsured in 2026.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2025/without-renewal-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-rural-hospital-revenues-will-drop-16-billion" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, November 13, 2025">[7]</a> CBO’s estimate for January enactment instead projects insurance gains of “0.1 million in 2026, 3.0 million in 2027, 4.0 million in 2028” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61999" title="Congressional Budget Office, cost estimate, January 8, 2026">[2]</a>. The rural forecast therefore cannot stand in for this bill’s hospital effects in 2026.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The spending study links lower revenue to “fewer services rendered” <a href="https://www.urban.org/research/publication/changes-health-care-spending-and-uncompensated-care-under-enhanced-tax-credit" title="urban.org, analysis, September 25, 2025">[6]</a>. Fewer services used by patients and less capacity available for an emergency are different outcomes. Its revenue estimates do not identify which emergency services would be lost or preserved.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The preparedness paper describes coalitions’ “support for emergency preparedness planning” <a href="https://www.flexmonitoring.org/sites/flexmonitoring.umn.edu/files/media/health_care_coalitions_cah_emergency_preparedness_2025.pdf" title="Flex Monitoring Team, “Health Care Coalitions: A Resource to Support Critical Access Hospital Emergency Preparedness Planning”, April 2024 (printed in the report)">[8]</a>. Describing hospitals’ emergency role does not show how the bill’s added patient payments change their readiness.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Neither the earlier financial forecast nor the description of emergency planning establishes the claimed protection of response capacity. CBO’s later coverage gains do not supply that missing link.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> Broader coverage strengthens the country’s response to the opioid crisis.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Marketplace plans must “offer substance use disorders benefits at parity with medical and surgical benefits”, meaning comparable insurance protections for addiction treatment. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8328862/" title="Psychiatric Services, “Effects of the ACA on Health Care Coverage for Adults with Substance Use Disorders”, 2021-05-07 (online); 2021-08-01 (issue)">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Insurance helps people reach treatment. A study identifies “Financial barriers such as lack of insurance coverage” as an obstacle. In its long-term study of Baltimore residents who injected drugs, private insurance “was associated with receipt of buprenorphine and having a regular source of medical care.” Buprenorphine is a medicine for opioid addiction. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6556110/" title="Journal of Substance Abuse Treatment, “Health Insurance Coverage is Associated with Access to Substance Use Treatment Among Individuals with Injection Drug Use: Evidence from a 12-year Prospective Study”, 2018-08-28 (online publication); 2019-01 (issue)">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Medicines for opioid addiction, including buprenorphine, have “proven effectiveness at reducing mortality risks.” <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2831936" title="Sumedha Gupta, Aditya James, Jennifer Miles, Hillary Samples, Stephen Crystal, “Trends in Access to Medications for Opioid Use Disorder”, 2025-04-04">[11]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> An earlier ACA study, covering states with and without Medicaid expansion, found: “Areas with higher uninsurance rates prior to the ACA saw larger reductions in opioid-related ED visits after the ACA took effect.” These are emergency department visits; the study found the effect emerged over several years. <a href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP69059.html" title="Decker, Sandra L., Dworsky, Michael, Gibson, Teresa B., Henke, Rachel M., McDermott, Kimberly Walsh, “The Impact of the Affordable Care Act Insurance Expansions on Opioid-Related Emergency Department Visits (RAND research summary)”, 2022-10-27 (RAND posting)">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The national health-security strategy identifies substance use disorder as a challenge to communities’ ability to withstand crises and states: “Community resilience plays a significant role in national health security.” <a href="https://www.biosecuritycentral.org/static/e7b9be30b22cc3f8c576748364c46fa0/nhss-2023-2026-508..pdf" title="HHS ASPR, “National Health Security Strategy 2023-2026”, 2023 (strategy for 2023-2026)">[13]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Making treatment coverage more affordable strengthens the response to a deadly drug threat and supports communities’ ability to withstand further emergencies. Both serve [NAME]’s concern for a secure country.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO turns benefit rules and treatment associations into a treatment gain that its evidence does not establish.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The paper describing required addiction benefits states that “the analysis did not assess the effects of coverage on substance use treatment or other health care use.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8328862/" title="Psychiatric Services, “Effects of the ACA on Health Care Coverage for Adults with Substance Use Disorders”, 2021-05-07 (online); 2021-08-01 (issue)">[9]</a> A covered benefit and treatment received are different outcomes.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Baltimore study identifies possible “income changes that affect both eligibility for some insurance programs and use of treatment.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6556110/" title="Journal of Substance Abuse Treatment, “Health Insurance Coverage is Associated with Access to Substance Use Treatment Among Individuals with Injection Drug Use: Evidence from a 12-year Prospective Study”, 2018-08-28 (online publication); 2019-01 (issue)">[10]</a> Its private-insurance association does not establish what added insurance caused.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The emergency-department study examines “ACA coverage expansions (including Medicaid expansion and Marketplaces)” and reports results “in both expansion and nonexpansion states” <a href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP69059.html" title="Decker, Sandra L., Dworsky, Michael, Gibson, Teresa B., Henke, Rachel M., McDermott, Kimberly Walsh, “The Impact of the Affordable Care Act Insurance Expansions on Opioid-Related Emergency Department Visits (RAND research summary)”, 2022-10-27 (RAND posting)">[12]</a>. That broader 2014 reform does not separate the effect of larger premium credits from the other changes it studies.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> These studies do not establish more people receiving effective treatment or less opioid harm from these credits. The medicine’s effectiveness and the health-security strategy do not supply that missing link.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> Better access to care helps preserve the workforce the country depends on.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Research on the larger credits describes improved access to care, including “primary and preventive care visits”, which “can both help prevent and manage costly chronic diseases.” Chronic diseases are long-lasting illnesses. <a href="https://bipartisanpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Enhanced-PTC-Brief-D4.pdf" title="bipartisanpolicy.org, analysis, October 2025">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> These illnesses affect the wider economy. CDC researchers identify “lost productivity in the workforce” among their burdens. <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0539.htm" title="Kathleen B. Watson, “Trends in Multiple Chronic Conditions Among US Adults, By Life Stage, Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System, 2013–2023”, 2025-04-17 (date printed in article)">[15]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Supporting care that prevents and manages illness helps preserve people’s ability to work. That supports the country’s ability to produce goods and provide services, strengthening its stability.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not show that the added care would prevent the work limits on which its production argument rests.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The credit report describes more “primary and preventive care visits” that “can both help prevent and manage costly chronic diseases.” <a href="https://bipartisanpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Enhanced-PTC-Brief-D4.pdf" title="bipartisanpolicy.org, analysis, October 2025">[14]</a> That is a claim about access and possible health benefits, not a finding about fewer work limits or missed workdays.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The CDC paper describes its contribution as “national data on the prevalence of selected chronic conditions” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0539.htm" title="Kathleen B. Watson, “Trends in Multiple Chronic Conditions Among US Adults, By Life Stage, Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System, 2013–2023”, 2025-04-17 (date printed in article)">[15]</a>. Its reference to “lost productivity in the workforce” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2025/24_0539.htm" title="Kathleen B. Watson, “Trends in Multiple Chronic Conditions Among US Adults, By Life Stage, Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System, 2013–2023”, 2025-04-17 (date printed in article)">[15]</a> describes the burden of illness, not how much of that burden added coverage prevents.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Showing that illness can hinder work does not show that the care gained through this extension prevents that harm. The step to more reliable national production remains unsupported.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> Maintaining health-care spending supports jobs, economic stability, and public services.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Coverage losses cut provider income: “Health care providers that lose revenue must reduce how much they can spend on their staff and goods purchased from vendors”. <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The effects spread beyond health care: “Reductions in labor expenses mean that some jobs are lost and some staff may be paid less. Thus, staff must restrict their purchases of consumer goods”. <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring the credits supports payments to providers, workers, and suppliers, reducing a source of economic disruption across the country.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Falling incomes also weaken public finances: “The combined reductions in individual and business incomes inevitably lead to lower state and local tax revenues.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The resulting budget pressure hinders public services: “This means that state and local governments will have more difficulties balancing their budgets and paying for health, human service, and other government functions.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Supporting employment and incomes also protects the revenue governments use to serve residents. This chain supports both national stability and the state’s capacity to protect its people.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The spending study does not establish that the bill preserves jobs and tax revenue across the country, rather than supporting a particular flow of spending.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It uses the “IMPLAN economic modeling system” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a>. USDA’s guidance warns: “Results of the analysis, whether output or jobs, should not be reported as net, new, or additional.” <a href="https://efotg.sc.egov.usda.gov/references/public/IL/TechNote_2_IMPLAN.pdf" title="phyllis.watkins, “Guidelines for Economic Impact Analysis with IMPLAN (Technical Note 200-ECN-2)”, 2014-12-05">[17]</a> Jobs supported by spending are not a count of jobs the country would otherwise lose.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Its spending assumptions were aligned with “the cost of extending the ACA credits and reversing other changes in 2026.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a> That comparison does not isolate H.R.1834’s January restoration of the credit rules “through 2028” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1834/BILLS-119hr1834eh.htm" title="H.R.1834, bill text, January 8, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The model’s provider, supplier, and worker spending chains do not establish the claimed national employment and tax gains from this bill. The further claim about protecting public services rests on those unestablished gains.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 6</span> Lower premium bills support household spending and economic stability.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> An analysis of the credits’ expiration links higher insurance bills for people who stay covered to less spending elsewhere: “This reduces their ability to purchase other goods and services, further dampening state economies.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> By lowering premiums for eligible policyholders, the bill preserves money for other purchases. That supports households and the businesses that depend on their spending, strengthening economic stability across the country.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> More purchasing power for policyholders does not by itself establish a more stable national economy.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited household claim concerns an ability to spend: higher insurance bills reduce “their ability to purchase other goods and services” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/issue-briefs/2025/oct/expiring-premium-tax-credits-lead-340000-jobs-lost-2026" title="The Commonwealth Fund, “Expiring Premium Tax Credits Lead to State Job Losses in 2026 | Commonwealth Fund”, October 16, 2025">[16]</a>. That does not measure the bill’s effect on spending across the country.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The model’s own guidance says: “Input-Output models do not account for general equilibrium effects such as offsetting gains or losses in other Industries or geographies or the diversion of funds from other projects.” <a href="https://support.implan.com/hc/en-us/articles/18944187743643-Assumptions-of-I-O" title="Michael Nealy, “Assumptions of I-O”, 2023-09-20 (written 2023-08-30)">[18]</a> These are changes elsewhere when money and work shift between uses.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Following policyholders’ purchasing power alone leaves out the changes needed to establish the claimed national effect. It does not show that the country’s economy becomes more stable.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 7</span> Federal premium help reduces pressure to divert limited state funds to replace it.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Connecticut committed its own money to replace lost federal help before the January vote: “Connecticut allocated an additional $51 million after a November 2025 special legislative session”. <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2026/some-states-blunted-impact-lost-federal-marketplace-subsidies-efforts-will-be-hard" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, April 21, 2026">[19]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Filling that gap competes with other health protection. California and Colorado shifted state subsidies away from reducing deductibles and copayments to reducing premiums: “switching from a program that lowered out-of-pocket cost sharing (by reducing or eliminating deductibles and copayments) to one that lowers monthly premiums to help people purchase and maintain comprehensive coverage.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2026/some-states-blunted-impact-lost-federal-marketplace-subsidies-efforts-will-be-hard" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, April 21, 2026">[19]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Restoring federal help meets more of that premium need and reduces pressure on states to fund replacements. That protects states’ ability to use limited health funds for other needs, serving [NAME]’s concern for a capable state.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The state examples do not establish that this bill frees state funds for other protective work.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The source reports that “Connecticut allocated an additional $51 million after a November 2025 special legislative session” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2026/some-states-blunted-impact-lost-federal-marketplace-subsidies-efforts-will-be-hard" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, April 21, 2026">[19]</a>. That is an allocation in response to expiration, not an estimate of state spending under a January restoration.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> California and Colorado were “switching from a program that lowered out-of-pocket cost sharing (by reducing or eliminating deductibles and copayments) to one that lowers monthly premiums to help people purchase and maintain comprehensive coverage.” <a href="https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2026/some-states-blunted-impact-lost-federal-marketplace-subsidies-efforts-will-be-hard" title="commonwealthfund.org, analysis, April 21, 2026">[19]</a> This documents a change in insurance aid. It does not track how federal restoration would change state spending or the health protection those state dollars buy.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO assumes the state savings and the added protective capacity it needs to establish.
