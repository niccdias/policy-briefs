**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419140125/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131)), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 8, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, July 21, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

**Reference:** Read "their country" as the United States.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.131 eases repayment to help finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit. Existing law requires “35 percent nonfederal repayment over a 50-year period, starting after project completion.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a> The bill keeps that share: “payment in an amount equal to 35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. It counts “funding provided during construction from any entity other than the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a> toward payment. For the unpaid balance, it allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. Simple interest charges no interest on past interest. The Interior Department must find hardship: “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Repayment is a barrier to completion: “the communities that it will serve do not have the financial capacity to meet the traditional repayment obligations.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210625/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Public resources are already committed: “More than $249 million has already been spent on the AVC” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029214321/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202501228/html/DCPD-202501228.htm" title="The President, presidential document, December 29, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Lower interest and more years to repay ease that barrier and help bring existing construction into service.</li></ul></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.131 lowers interest and allows longer repayment on the conduit’s unpaid local construction share. Existing law provides “35 percent nonfederal repayment over a 50-year period” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>. For the balance left after contributions during construction, the bill allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>, “based on a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</p></div>
