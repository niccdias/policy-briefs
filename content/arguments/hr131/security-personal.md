**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419140125/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131)), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 8, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, July 21, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by an average American: ordinary personal health and safety risks in U.S. circumstances, without assuming a specially exposed person.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026, H.R.131 would ease repayment for the Arkansas Valley Conduit. Existing law requires repayment “within fifty years from the time the works are first available for the delivery of water” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187 print of existing law, law in force, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>. After the Interior Secretary finds financial hardship, the bill allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. This addresses a barrier to finishing the pipeline. The local repayment agency explained in 2024 that project revenue could no longer cover the share on the old terms: “miscellaneous revenues were expected to be more than sufficient to repay 35 percent of the AVC’s cost with interest and within 50 years. With the May, 2024, updated cost estimate, this is no longer true. Repayment can now be accomplished only if the interest rate is reduced to at least half the current project rate and the repayment period is at least 75 years, 25 years longer than the currently authorized 50 years.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162131/https://docs.house.gov/meetings/II/II13/20241120/117720/HHRG-118-II13-Wstate-LongB-20241120.pdf" title="“Statement on H.R. 9514, Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act”, November 20, 2024">[3]</a> A House floor manager described the bill's purpose: “This bill would revise certain repayment terms to provide additional flexibility for local stakeholders to help finance the project and begin delivering clean, reliable water to rural communities.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203151/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-21/html/CREC-2025-07-21-pt1-PgH3497.htm" title="Hoyle, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 21, 2025">[4]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.131 eases repayment to help finish the conduit. It allows “based on a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary, repayment of the balance not covered under subparagraph (A) for a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. Current law provides a “50-year period, starting after project completion.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a> The repayment barrier matters to the remaining work: on January 8, Congress heard that “the project is still not completed largely because the communities that it will serve do not have the financial capacity to meet the traditional repayment obligations.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210625/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[5]</a></p></div>
