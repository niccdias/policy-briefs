**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act. Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.131 makes federal financing for this project cheaper and longer. Current law requires “35 percent nonfederal repayment over a 50-year period” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. <tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It uses shared public resources for added financing relief while vulnerable families elsewhere still lack affordable water.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing law bases interest on what the Treasury pays to borrow money: “the computed average interest rate payable by the Treasury upon its outstanding marketable public obligations” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[9]</a> The bill changes repayment of that financing.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The district responsible for repayment says expected project revenues no longer cover the old terms: “When Public Law 111-11 was enacted, miscellaneous revenues were expected to be more than sufficient to repay 35 percent of the AVC’s cost with interest and within 50 years. H.R.131 changes “the contract for the Arkansas Valley Conduit” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[1]</a>; it does not change that program’s grants.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Concern for vulnerable people makes lost protection, fewer life opportunities and unjust treatment reasons for opposition. A lower federal return does not by itself establish any of those losses.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO’s possible alternative use of public money does not establish that this repayment change leaves vulnerable families elsewhere less protected.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It reserves new hardship relief for one project instead of giving other vulnerable communities a chance to qualify by need.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The new terms apply to “the contract for the Arkansas Valley Conduit” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[1]</a>. Hardship outside that project cannot qualify a community for this relief.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Other communities need affordable water infrastructure. EPA reports of Martin County, Kentucky: “Failing and aging drinking water infrastructure is the main contributor to these financial challenges.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-12/water-affordability-needs-assessment.pdf" title="USEPA, OW, OWM, WID, WIRFC, “Water Affordability Needs Assessment: Report to Congress”, December 2024">[19]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Many residents have low incomes and lack local help with bills: “Martin County is not able to support a local Customer Assistance Program because of its small population size and high percentage of low-income customers.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-12/water-affordability-needs-assessment.pdf" title="USEPA, OW, OWM, WID, WIRFC, “Water Affordability Needs Assessment: Report to Congress”, December 2024">[19]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The problem threatens basic access: “In 2023, there were 349 residential water disconnections for nonpayment – about one in ten households.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-12/water-affordability-needs-assessment.pdf" title="USEPA, OW, OWM, WID, WIRFC, “Water Affordability Needs Assessment: Report to Congress”, December 2024">[19]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing water aid does not guarantee affordable projects. EPA reports: “Subsidized DWSRF assistance offered may still not be sufficient to make projects affordable for communities.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/dwsrf_set-asides-for-dacs_508_0.pdf" title="U.S. EPA, OW, Office of Ground Water and Drinking Water, “Targeted Approaches for Using DWSRF Set-Asides to Assist Disadvantaged Communities”, January 2025">[23]</a> DWSRF is the federal program that helps states finance drinking-water projects.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A way to compare needs already exists. That program gives priority to projects that “assist systems most in need, on a per household basis, according to State determined affordability criteria.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-09/fact-sheet-dwsrf-overview-final_1.pdf" title="U.S. EPA, OW, Office of Ground Water and Drinking Water, “Drinking Water State Revolving Fund Fact Sheet”, February 2023 (date printed in EPA 816-F-18-001)">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Just treatment calls for people with the same basic water needs to have a chance at hardship relief based on those needs, wherever they live.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The bill makes access to its added relief depend on being served by a named project. It gives other vulnerable communities no chance to have their need considered for those terms. Equal opportunities and just treatment give [NAME] a reason to oppose that unequal access to help with a basic necessity.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Exclusion from one project’s repayment terms does not by itself show unfair access to help with the same basic water needs.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This bill changes a particular repayment duty. Reclamation explains that rising costs left the project’s beneficiaries “again faced with challenges to repay their share of construction of the project” <a href="https://usbr.gov/newsroom/congressional-testimony/5024?field_story=1" title="Bureau of Reclamation, “Statement of Deputy Commissioner Roque Sánchez on H.R. 9514, H.R. 9515, and H.R. 9969”, November 20, 2024">[8]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill keeps payment of “35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[1]</a> and requires “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[1]</a> for the new repayment terms.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The project boundary identifies the debt being changed; the hardship rule ties the change to difficulty paying that debt. The PRO must show why this targeted response to a repayment barrier gives people with comparable needs an unfair advantage.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Other water aid uses different forms of help. EPA says drinking-water loans “may be offered with principal forgiveness, grants, negative interest rates, or other loan forgiveness.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-09/fact-sheet-dwsrf-overview-final_1.pdf" title="U.S. EPA, OW, Office of Ground Water and Drinking Water, “Drinking Water State Revolving Fund Fact Sheet”, February 2023 (date printed in EPA 816-F-18-001)">[22]</a> Its program gives priority to projects that “assist systems most in need, on a per household basis, according to State determined affordability criteria.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-09/fact-sheet-dwsrf-overview-final_1.pdf" title="U.S. EPA, OW, Office of Ground Water and Drinking Water, “Drinking Water State Revolving Fund Fact Sheet”, February 2023 (date printed in EPA 816-F-18-001)">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Equal opportunities in life and just treatment concern the help people can obtain for their needs and the costs they must still bear. Eligibility for the same loan terms cannot measure that across loans, grants and forgiven debt.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> EPA warns that this aid “may still not be sufficient to make projects affordable for communities.” <a href="https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/dwsrf_set-asides-for-dacs_508_0.pdf" title="U.S. EPA, OW, Office of Ground Water and Drinking Water, “Targeted Approaches for Using DWSRF Set-Asides to Assist Disadvantaged Communities”, January 2025">[23]</a> Its Martin County examples also establish unmet need. They do not compare that need, the aid available and the remaining costs with those of the Conduit communities.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO has not shown that H.R.131 gives an unfair preference among equally needy people. Its evidence of inadequate help elsewhere does not turn relief from this separate repayment duty into unjust treatment.</li></ul></td></tr>
