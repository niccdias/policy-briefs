title: "H.R.131 × Universalism – Tolerance"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act. Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Tolerance. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to be tolerant toward all kinds of people and groups
- to listen to and understand people who are different to them
- to accept people even when they disagree with them

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

The assessment requires compliance with federal or state burial law and allows work to resume “only when compliance has been completed.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit Final Environmental Assessment”, February 2022">[14]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> These steps address respect for sacred sites before and during construction. The PRO does not show why helping communities repay for work under these protections amounts to dismissing tribal beliefs.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill speeds a project whose plan for Native sites was made without hearing from any tribe.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The tribes tied to this land were moved out of Colorado long ago. Colorado's historical society linked Native remains found across the state to “present-day tribes who jointly claim a presence in the region prior to and during the contact period” <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2001/02/20/01-4080/notice-of-inventory-completion-for-native-american-human-remains-and-associated-funerary-objects-in" title="National Park Service, U.S. Department of the Interior (Federal Register, FR Doc. 01-4080), “Notice of Inventory Completion for Native American Human Remains and Associated Funerary Objects in the Possession of the Colorado Historical Society, Denver, CO”, February 20, 2001">[15]</a>. It also noted “Colorado's history of tribal relocation” <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2001/02/20/01-4080/notice-of-inventory-completion-for-native-american-human-remains-and-associated-funerary-objects-in" title="National Park Service, U.S. Department of the Interior (Federal Register, FR Doc. 01-4080), “Notice of Inventory Completion for Native American Human Remains and Associated Funerary Objects in the Possession of the Colorado Historical Society, Denver, CO”, February 20, 2001">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Many of these tribes now live far away. The project's list of tribes includes the “Cheyenne-Arapaho Tribe of Oklahoma” and the “Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit Final Environmental Assessment”, February 2022">[14]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In 2013, Reclamation signed its plan for protecting historic and cultural sites along the route. Before that, it wrote to the tribes: it “consulted with twenty-four Native American tribes” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_ea_app_c.pdf" title="U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer, “Programmatic Agreement between Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and the Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer regarding the Arkansas Valley Conduit Project, Colorado (Agreement R13MU60034; AVC EIS Appendix N.1, posted as Appendix C to the AVC Final Environmental Assessment)”, Signed July 2013 (executed July 31, 2013); posted as EA Appendix C, February 2022">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> When that plan was signed, none of the “Tribes have responded to our letters of June 20, 2011, January 17, 2012, and January 5, 2013” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_ea_app_c.pdf" title="U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer, “Programmatic Agreement between Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and the Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer regarding the Arkansas Valley Conduit Project, Colorado (Agreement R13MU60034; AVC EIS Appendix N.1, posted as Appendix C to the AVC Final Environmental Assessment)”, Signed July 2013 (executed July 31, 2013); posted as EA Appendix C, February 2022">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That 2013 plan still guides the work. The 2022 review says “Reclamation would follow the PA” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit Final Environmental Assessment”, February 2022">[14]</a>, meaning that plan.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Tolerance means listening to and understanding people who are different. Morgan has a reason to oppose a bill that speeds digging under a plan made without the voices of the tribes whose dead may lie in its path.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> Sending letters is not the same as hearing people. When distant tribes do not answer, the plan goes ahead without their views, and that gap is exactly what this value cares about.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The 2013 record of unanswered outreach does not show that H.R.131 shuts tribes out of later decisions about their sites.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The record describes invitations to 24 tribes and follow-up “telephone calls” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_ea_app_c.pdf" title="U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer, “Programmatic Agreement between Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and the Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer regarding the Arkansas Valley Conduit Project, Colorado (Agreement R13MU60034; AVC EIS Appendix N.1, posted as Appendix C to the AVC Final Environmental Assessment)”, Signed July 2013 (executed July 31, 2013); posted as EA Appendix C, February 2022">[13]</a>. Unanswered outreach does not establish that the agency refused to listen.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The 2013 agreement provides for later tribal review of site reports: tribes “will have 30 days to review the report(s) and provide comments” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_ea_app_c.pdf" title="U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer, “Programmatic Agreement between Bureau of Reclamation, Eastern Colorado Area Office, and the Colorado State Historic Preservation Officer regarding the Arkansas Valley Conduit Project, Colorado (Agreement R13MU60034; AVC EIS Appendix N.1, posted as Appendix C to the AVC Final Environmental Assessment)”, Signed July 2013 (executed July 31, 2013); posted as EA Appendix C, February 2022">[13]</a>. The 2022 assessment says that, based on surveys and consultation with tribes and the state's historic preservation officer, “pipeline alignments could be shifted” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. Signing the agreement did not settle every site decision without tribal input.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Federal historic-site review rules also require a real opportunity to raise concerns and help address harm. Consultation is required “regardless of whether the undertaking is located on or off tribal lands.” <a href="https://www.achp.gov/sites/default/files/2021-06/ConsultationwithIndianTribesHandbook6-11-21Final.pdf" title="Valerie, “Consultation with Indian Tribes in the Section 106 Review Process: The Handbook”, June 2021">[18]</a> Living outside Colorado does not remove a tribe's place in the review.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Listening to different people requires opportunities for their views to shape decisions. The bill preserves those routes for tribal input and the duty to consult. A record of nonresponse in 2013 does not turn repayment relief into rejection of tribal voices.</li></ul></td></tr>
