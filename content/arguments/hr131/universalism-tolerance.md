title: "H.R.131 × Universalism – Tolerance"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act. Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be tolerant toward all kinds of people and groups. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to listen to and understand people who are different to them. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to accept people even when they disagree with them. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.131?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026, H.R.131 would ease repayment of the local and state construction share of the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a drinking-water pipeline. Existing law required “35 percent nonfederal repayment over a 50-year period, starting after project completion.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a> With “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>, the bill allows repayment of the unpaid balance “for a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. This would help communities “finance the project and begin delivering clean, reliable water to rural communities” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-21/html/CREC-2025-07-21-pt1-PgH3497.htm" title="Hoyle, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 21, 2025">[3]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It makes rural communities' own account of their needs count in the repayment rules.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Prowers County commissioners describe a funding problem: “Rural water providers in our area often struggle to secure the funding needed to meet the needs of the communities they serve.” <a href="http://boebert.house.gov/media/press-releases/congresswoman-lauren-boeberts-finish-arkansas-valley-conduit-act-passes-0" title="Boebert, press release, July 21, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The local water district helped draft the bill. Boebert said, “I thank the Bureau of Reclamation and the Southeastern Colorado Water Conservancy District for working with us in drafting this critical legislation.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-21/html/CREC-2025-07-21-pt1-PgH3497.htm" title="Boebert, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 21, 2025">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The repayment burden is a barrier to finishing the pipeline: “the project is still not completed largely because the communities that it will serve do not have the financial capacity to meet the traditional repayment obligations” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> [NAME]'s commitment to listening to and understanding different people gives weight to a community's own account of the barriers it faces.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The bill changes the payment terms in response to a barrier these communities describe and helped address. Favoring that response puts understanding of their circumstances into practice.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Understanding the residents' funding problem does not give their proposed remedy extra weight under tolerance.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Local commissioners report a funding problem: “Rural water providers in our area often struggle to secure the funding needed to meet the needs of the communities they serve.” <a href="http://boebert.house.gov/media/press-releases/congresswoman-lauren-boeberts-finish-arkansas-valley-conduit-act-passes-0" title="Boebert, press release, July 21, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Boebert credits the local water district and the Bureau of Reclamation “for working with us in drafting this critical legislation” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-21/html/CREC-2025-07-21-pt1-PgH3497.htm" title="Boebert, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 21, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bases repayment relief on “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Listening gives residents' account a fair hearing. Choosing to ease their payments adds a separate goal: meeting the need they describe. Understanding a need and wanting to meet it are different aims.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO shows a payment change informed by local concerns. Its step from understanding those concerns to favoring relief relies on the added aim of improving water access. Calling relief a response to residents does not make that aim part of tolerance.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It helps residents without requiring agreement with their politics or their representatives.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The relief serves households' water needs. The bill's stated purpose is “To provide domestic water supplies to communities and households that do not have reliable access to domestic water supplies” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Completing the project would meet that need across the communities it serves: “Once completed, the Arkansas Valley Conduit will deliver clean water to over 50,000 people in 39 rural communities across southeastern Colorado.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-12-16/html/CREC-2025-12-16-pt1-PgS8768-4.htm" title="Bennet, floor speech, Congressional Record, December 16, 2025">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> There is an explicit example of support across disagreement. Huffman said House members “looked past their political differences” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[8]</a> to pass this water bill.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Accepting people despite disagreement gives [NAME] a reason to support useful help for them without making shared political views a condition.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Favoring the repayment relief puts that acceptance into practice for these residents, whatever [NAME] thinks of their views or those of the bill's sponsors.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Setting political dislike aside when judging aid does not turn the aid's usefulness into a reason grounded in tolerance.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill states a water-supply goal: “To provide domestic water supplies to communities and households that do not have reliable access to domestic water supplies” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The value framework distinguishes tolerance's “Acceptance and understanding of those who are different” <a href="https://www.zora.uzh.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/7859b586-355b-468f-8488-446062a83d98/content" title="Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, American Psychological Association; accepted version hosted by the University of Zurich, “Refining the Theory of Basic Individual Values”, 2012 (journal issue: October 2012; volume 103, issue 4, pages 663–688)">[9]</a> from concern's “Commitment to equality, justice and protection” <a href="https://www.zora.uzh.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/7859b586-355b-468f-8488-446062a83d98/content" title="Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, American Psychological Association; accepted version hosted by the University of Zurich, “Refining the Theory of Basic Individual Values”, 2012 (journal issue: October 2012; volume 103, issue 4, pages 663–688)">[9]</a>. A benefit to people's welfare alone does not establish a link to accepting different people.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Huffman's example concerns setting aside the sponsors' politics: “could care less about Ms. Boebert's politics or Mr. Hurd's politics” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[8]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Accepting people despite disagreement rules out discounting their needs because of their views. Removing that unfair ground for opposing aid does not supply a positive reason to favor its payment terms.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Huffman's example supports judging the bill without partisan bias. The PRO turns that rule for judging a policy into support for the policy's water benefit. It has not shown why this financial help itself expresses acceptance of residents across disagreement.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> It enacts payment terms shaped by taking opposing views seriously.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Lawmakers objected to the original terms. Huffman explained that “Representative Boebert's bill did raise some concerns about removing interest payments and extending the repayment period” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a>, and that “our colleagues in the Senate worked to address these shared concerns” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The final bill pairs relief with continued repayment: “payment in an amount equal to 35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>, with interest on the unpaid balance at “50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Huffman described the result as “a carefully negotiated and narrowly tailored bipartisan bill to support the completion of this project” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Listening to people with different views includes taking their reasons seriously enough to shape a shared solution.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Enacting these terms turns that listening into payment relief shaped by competing views. [NAME]'s commitment to listening and understanding gives a reason to favor this practical agreement.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Taking opposing reasons seriously does not give tolerance a reason to favor the resulting deal just because it was negotiated.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Huffman describes concerns about “removing interest payments and extending the repayment period” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a> and says senators “worked to address these shared concerns” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[6]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill still requires “payment in an amount equal to 35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. For the unpaid balance, subject to financial hardship, it allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> [NAME]'s value favors hearing and understanding people's reasons. It does not favor a particular financial balance just because that balance reflects a compromise. Accepting people and assessing their financial proposals are separate judgments.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO shows how competing views shaped the deal. Enacting it would carry out the agreed financial terms, but their negotiated origin supplies no bridge from valuing a fair hearing to favoring those terms.</li></ul></td></tr>
</table>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.131?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> On January 8, 2026, existing law required repayment “within fifty years from the time the works are first available for the delivery of water” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187 print of existing law, law in force, July 10, 2025">[10]</a>. H.R.131 eases repayment of the unpaid balance: “based on a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary, repayment of the balance not covered under subparagraph (A) for a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Helping construction continue puts Native cultural ties to ancestral places and burial traditions at risk.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The current financing creates a stopping point. Water district board president Bill Long explains the bill's importance: “Without this legislation there will be a point where we will have to stop.” <a href="https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[11]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The project's environmental review identifies prehistoric sites in the area it could affect: “Archaeological resources also include two prehistoric sites.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The review treats encountering undisturbed historic places as a construction risk. Its route plan aims “to diminish the probability of encountering any undisturbed historic properties.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It does not guarantee avoiding every burial: “All known burials or cemeteries will be avoided to the extent possible.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Easing the financial barrier helps more construction proceed with a remaining risk to ancestral places and graves.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> These places matter to living cultures. The National Association of Tribal Historic Preservation Officers explains: “Connections to cultural heritage sustain the health and vitality of Native peoples.” <a href="https://www.indian.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/NATHPO%20Written%20Testimony%20NAGPRA%20SCIA%201.31.2022.pdf" title="Timothy McKeown, “Written Testimony of Valerie Grussing: The Long Journey Home: Advancing the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act’s Promise After 30 Years of Practice”, 2022-01-31">[13]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Disturbing graves can violate the beliefs expressed through burial traditions. Southern Ute preservation official Cassandra Atencio explains: “When you put somebody in the ground, you expect them to stay there because you made prayers or did something over them, and that meant something so they could go somewhere else. When you uncover that, you're disturbing them, and now you're bringing them back.” <a href="https://www.historycolorado.org/lost-highways/2022/03/21/spirits-place-native-american-graves-protection-and-repatriation-act-and" title="“Spirits of Place: The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act and Its Legacy”, 2022-03-21">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Listening to and accepting people with different beliefs gives weight to their cultural ties and burial practices, even when [NAME] does not share those beliefs.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Respecting those practices gives [NAME] a reason to oppose financing that advances this risk of disturbance.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The objection concerns disturbance itself. The plan requires compliance before work restarts: “only when compliance has been completed.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a> That cannot undo any disturbance that led to discovering a grave.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not connect the later construction helped by repayment relief to the living cultural ties or burial practices it says are at risk.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing funds support construction “for another three to five years” <a href="https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[11]</a>. The source places the repayment problem “when we get to the second half of the project” <a href="https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[11]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Risk from already-funded work cannot establish the added risk from the later work that repayment relief would help advance.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The environmental review says, “Archaeological resources also include two prehistoric sites.” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The original environmental impact statement defines that label by age: “Prehistoric archaeological sites predate written records” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_eis.pdf" title="“Arkansas Valley Conduit and Long-Term Excess Capacity Master Contract: Final Environmental Impact Statement”, 2013-08">[15]</a>. A site’s age alone does not identify a grave or explain which living cultural ties the work would threaten.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The same statement explains the study corridor: “The area of potential effect allows flexibility in pipeline location” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_eis.pdf" title="“Arkansas Valley Conduit and Long-Term Excess Capacity Master Contract: Final Environmental Impact Statement”, 2013-08">[15]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Surveys and consultation can change the route: “Based on inventory results and Tribal and Colorado SHPO consultations, pipeline alignments could be shifted to avoid known sites” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Counting sites in that corridor does not show how the later work puts those sites at greater risk or threatens Native cultural ties. The PRO leaves that link unstated.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The burial provision addresses a condition: “If a burial or cemetery cannot be avoided or is encountered during construction” <a href="https://www.usbr.gov/gp/ecao/avc/docs/avc_final_ea.pdf" title="Stroh, Terence L. (Terry), “Arkansas Valley Conduit: Final Environmental Assessment”, 2022-02">[12]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> A rule for handling a possible discovery does not establish an added risk of one. Saying that stopping afterward cannot undo disturbance does not supply that missing evidence.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The heritage testimony states a general connection: “Connections to cultural heritage sustain the health and vitality of Native peoples.” <a href="https://www.indian.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/NATHPO%20Written%20Testimony%20NAGPRA%20SCIA%201.31.2022.pdf" title="Timothy McKeown, “Written Testimony of Valerie Grussing: The Long Journey Home: Advancing the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act’s Promise After 30 Years of Practice”, 2022-01-31">[13]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Atencio’s account explains the meaning of uncovering a buried person: “When you uncover that, you're disturbing them, and now you're bringing them back.” <a href="https://www.historycolorado.org/lost-highways/2022/03/21/spirits-place-native-american-graves-protection-and-repatriation-act-and" title="“Spirits of Place: The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act and Its Legacy”, 2022-03-21">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Listening to and accepting people gives weight to their beliefs. It does not turn general accounts of heritage and burial beliefs into evidence that this bill puts those ties or practices at greater risk.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Neither the testimony nor the discovery precaution fills the gap between repayment relief and the added cultural risk on which the PRO’s reason depends.</li></ul></td></tr>
