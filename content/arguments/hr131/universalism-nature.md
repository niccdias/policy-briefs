title: "H.R.131 × Universalism – Nature"

**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act. Judged as of January 8, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to care for nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to take part in activities to defend nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Cleaner source water reduces polluted treatment waste that threatens rivers and aquatic life.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Treating local groundwater creates concentrated waste: “When this groundwater is cleaned to drinking water standards, in many cases it produces a reject stream which violates EPA discharge standards.” <a href="https://docs.house.gov/meetings/II/II13/20241120/117720/HHRG-118-II13-Wstate-LongB-20241120.pdf" title="“Statement on H.R. 9514, Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act”, 2024-11-20">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> La Junta and Las Animas use reverse osmosis, a filtering process. Describing their discharge problem does not supply that link.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Clean tap water lets families replace bottled water, avoiding its waste, pollution, and use of resources.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Poor local water quality “forces many families to rely on bottled water or expensive filtration systems” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Huffman, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Helping provide clean tap water removes the water-quality problem driving those bottled-water purchases.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A U.S. study compared water-supply methods for the same amount of drinking water: “The functional unit for the study was 1000 gallons of drinking water” <a href="https://css.umich.edu/sites/default/files/css_doc/CSS09-11.pdf" title="hunscher, “Comparative Life-Cycle Assessment of Bottled vs. Tap Water Systems”, 2009-12-14">[8]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Across production, use, and disposal, tap water served in reusable cups or bottles produced less waste: “With respect to life-cycle solid waste generation, tap water systems are the preferred systems.” <a href="https://css.umich.edu/sites/default/files/css_doc/CSS09-11.pdf" title="hunscher, “Comparative Life-Cycle Assessment of Bottled vs. Tap Water Systems”, 2009-12-14">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The study also found lower emissions of gases that warm the climate: “With respect to life-cycle greenhouse gas emissions, tap water systems are the preferred systems.” <a href="https://css.umich.edu/sites/default/files/css_doc/CSS09-11.pdf" title="hunscher, “Comparative Life-Cycle Assessment of Bottled vs. Tap Water Systems”, 2009-12-14">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Making plastic bottles uses oil and energy.
