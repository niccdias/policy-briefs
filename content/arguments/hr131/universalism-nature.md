**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419140125/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131)), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 8, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, July 21, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to care for nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to take part in activities to defend nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law on January 8, 2026, H.R.131 would ease local repayments for completing the conduit, a water pipeline.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing law requires “35 percent nonfederal repayment over a 50-year period, starting after project completion.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> If the Interior Secretary finds financial hardship, the bill allows repayment of the remaining balance “for a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill also counts construction contributions toward the local share: “funding provided during construction from any entity other than the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> After costs rose, the local water district testified that project revenue could no longer cover the local share on the old terms: “miscellaneous revenues were expected to be more than sufficient to repay 35 percent of the AVC’s cost with interest and within 50 years. With the May, 2024, updated cost estimate, this is no longer true. Repayment can now be accomplished only if the interest rate is reduced to at least half the current project rate and the repayment period is at least 75 years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162131/https://docs.house.gov/meetings/II/II13/20241120/117720/HHRG-118-II13-Wstate-LongB-20241120.pdf" title="“Statement on H.R. 9514, Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act”, 2024-11-20">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> After the veto, the district’s president placed this constraint on the later part of the line: “It’s when we get to the second half of the project where it will be challenging to build and repay our portion of the debt” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162403/https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[4]</a>. He added: “Without this legislation there will be a point where we will have to stop.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162403/https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The project replaces poor groundwater with cleaner water: “The AVC will provide a new surface water source of high quality” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162131/https://docs.house.gov/meetings/II/II13/20241120/117720/HHRG-118-II13-Wstate-LongB-20241120.pdf" title="“Statement on H.R. 9514, Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act”, 2024-11-20">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Easing this repayment barrier helps communities finish the conduit and switch water sources, enabling the environmental benefits below.</li></ul></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026, H.R.131 would ease repayment of communities’ 35% construction share. The change extends the repayment limit “from 50 years to 75 years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187, committee report, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>. For costs not paid during construction, relief requires “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. The bill allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a> at “50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Financing is a barrier to completion: “the project remains unfinished, primarily due to financial constraints” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203151/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-21/html/CREC-2025-07-21-pt1-PgH3497.htm" title="Hoyle, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 21, 2025">[15]</a>. On January 8, the sponsor said the relief lets communities “afford their 35 percent cost share of the project that they are statutorily obligated to repay.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210625/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH205.htm" title="Boebert, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 8, 2026">[10]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Making that share easier to repay supports the remaining construction and eventual operation.</li></ul></div>
