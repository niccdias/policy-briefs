title: "H.R.131 × Tradition"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.131](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419140125/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/131)), the Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 8, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, July 21, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to honor the traditional practices of their culture. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.131?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference. Tradition here concerns preserving, observing or passing down identifiable inherited customs, religious practices and ways of life, valued for their continuity with the past. Religious endorsement of a general moral goal alone does not establish that connection.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on January 8, 2026, H.R.131 would ease repayment for the existing drinking-water pipeline. Current law requires repayment “within fifty years from the time the works are first available for the delivery of water” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187 print of existing law, law in force, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>. For the balance left after funding provided during construction, the bill requires “a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a> and allows “a period of not more than 75 years with simple interest at a rate that is equal to 50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. The required share stays at “35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. These terms help make completion affordable: the local water district's board president explains, “Without this legislation there will be a point where we will have to stop.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029162403/https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/20/arkansas-valley-pipeline-funding-building/" title="Jerd Smith, “Arkansas Valley pipeline has about 3 years of cash left to keep building, despite presidential veto”, 2026-01-20">[3]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.131?

**Bill description:** H.R.131 would have reduced what communities in Colorado must repay the federal government for building the Arkansas Valley Conduit, a federal water pipeline. It would have lowered the interest they owe and extended their repayment period.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> The bill keeps the construction repayment share at “35 percent of the cost of the conduit” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. For the balance after construction contributions, relief is “based on a demonstration of financial hardship, as determined by the Secretary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. Repayment can last “not more than 75 years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a> at “50 percent of the interest rate determined by the Secretary of the Treasury under section 2(c)” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192254/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr131/BILLS-119hr131eh.htm" title="H.R.131, bill text, July 21, 2025">[2]</a>. The law in force on January 8, 2026 requires repayment “within fifty years from the time the works are first available for the delivery of water” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187 print of existing law, law in force, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>. Its full interest rate rests on “the computed average interest rate payable by the Treasury upon its outstanding marketable public obligations” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210940/https://www.congress.gov/119/crpt/hrpt187/generated/CRPT-119hrpt187.htm" title="H. Rept. 119-187 print of existing law, law in force, July 10, 2025">[1]</a>.</p></div>
