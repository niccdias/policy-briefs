title: "H.R.28 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.28?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> At schools receiving federal funds, H.R.28 would make it unlawful “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It defines sex by birth: “sex shall be recognized based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Male and female below follow this rule.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> By January 14, 2025, the 2024 federal rule had already been struck down; that day's debate acknowledged “last week's court order striking down the regulation” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The existing federal sports rule allowed separate teams: “a recipient may operate or sponsor separate teams for members of each sex where selection for such teams is based upon competitive skill or the activity involved is a contact sport.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title34-vol1/pdf/CFR-2024-title34-vol1-sec106-41.pdf" title="“34 CFR § 106.41 — Athletics (2024 edition)”, 2024-07-01 (edition; PDF printed 2024-09-06)">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> College rules still allowed transgender women to compete under hormone-based eligibility rules. The NCAA's May 2024 policy said: “Total serum testosterone thresholds apply to any transgender woman competing on an NCAA women’s team.” <a href="https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/inclusion/lgbtq/SSI_TransgenderSADeadlinesAndThresholdsFall.pdf" title="Mickus, Mallory, “NCAA Transgender Student-Athlete Participation Policy: Sport-Specific Testosterone Thresholds and Championship Eligibility Deadlines, Fall Sports”, 2024-05 (updated May 2024)">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> H.R.28 writes its restriction into the law itself, which “is amended by adding at the end the following” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> H.R.28 would add an express federal birth-sex rule where existing policies allowed this participation. Covered schools would share the same rule, and an agency could not reverse it simply by changing its regulations.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It protects women's fair chance to earn sporting wins and recognition.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Male puberty builds strength: “increases in testosterone due to puberty promote muscular strength” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A review of 24 studies of transgender women who were not athletes found that, after 12–36 months of hormone treatment, they likely retained a strength advantage over women who were not transgender: “transwomen likely retain a strength advantage over cisgender women.” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Strength matters in speed and power events: “In sports demanding speed and power, muscular strength and the ability to generate high rates of force are recognised as key determinant in athletic success.” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> In strength-dependent events, the bill would keep retained male-puberty strength advantages out of the female category. Giving women a meaningful chance to earn sporting rewards serves [NAME]'s concern for equal opportunity and just treatment.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO has not shown that the strength differences it cites make competition under existing rules unfair.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Its review leaves the effects of hormone treatment in trained people unresolved: “the effects of GAHT on these parameters, or indeed athletic performance in transgender people who engage in training and competition, remain unknown.” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The review does not report whether the compared groups had similar training: “The levels of physical activity of the transwomen compared with cisgender women in the studies were not reported.” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a> Here, cisgender women means women who are not transgender.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> NCAA college eligibility already included hormone limits: “Total serum testosterone thresholds apply to any transgender woman competing on an NCAA women’s team.” <a href="https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/inclusion/lgbtq/SSI_TransgenderSADeadlinesAndThresholdsFall.pdf" title="Mickus, Mallory, “NCAA Transgender Student-Athlete Participation Policy: Sport-Specific Testosterone Thresholds and Championship Eligibility Deadlines, Fall Sports”, 2024-05 (updated May 2024)">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A 2024 study of athletes could not settle differences within particular sports: “The subgroups of sports that emerged were also too dissimilar to allow meaningful subgroup analysis.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11137468/" title="“Strength, power and aerobic capacity of transgender athletes: a cross-sectional study”, 2024-04-10 (first publication; June 2024 issue)">[6]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> An inferred strength advantage in people who are not athletes does not establish an unfair advantage in a strength-dependent event under those eligibility rules. The PRO has not supplied the step from physical traits to a gain in just treatment or equal opportunity.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It protects girls' places in sport and their chances to gain education, health, and leadership benefits.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Former college swimmer Paula Scanlan testified to actual lost places: “I know women who have lost roster spots and spots on the podium.” <a href="https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU10/20230727/116284/HHRG-118-JU10-Wstate-ScanlanP-20230727.pdf" title="“Paula Scanlan: Written testimony before the House Judiciary Subcommittee on the Constitution and Limited Government”, 2023-07-27">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill allows male training or practice only “so long as no female is deprived of a roster spot on a team or sport, opportunity to participate in a practice or competition, scholarship, admission to an educational institution, or any other benefit that accompanies participating in the athletic program or activity.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Where places or sports aid are limited, the competition ban and the practice condition protect female athletes from losing them to male participants. For a girl who needs sports aid to attend college, protecting that aid preserves a route into education.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Girls in positive sports programs report better mental health. A 2024 study found: “Girls who play sport in positive environments report significantly lower levels of anxiety and depression than those who previously played or never played.” <a href="https://www.womenssportsfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Thriving-Through-Sport-Research-Report.pdf" title="“Thriving Through Sport: The Transformative Impact on Girls’ Mental Health”, 2024">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A 2024 survey of U.S. adults who had played girls' or women's sports found reported gains in leadership skills: “Half of women (48.6%) credit the skills acquired through sports for their leadership development.” <a href="https://www.womenssportsfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Play-to-Lead-Report-2024.pdf" title="“Play to Lead: The Generational Impact of Sports on Women’s Leadership”, 2024">[9]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Keeping a girl's place in a good program preserves her chance to gain these benefits. Protecting routes into education, well-being, and adult leadership serves [NAME]'s concern for equal opportunities in life.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Reserving scarce places by birth sex does not establish that their allocation becomes fairer.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill's practice exception requires that “no female is deprived of a roster spot on a team or sport, opportunity to participate in a practice or competition, scholarship, admission to an educational institution, or any other benefit that accompanies participating in the athletic program or activity.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> This condition restricts practice when it would displace a female athlete. Calling another eligible athlete's selection a deprivation of equal opportunity still requires showing why that selection was unfair.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Scanlan reports lost places: “I know women who have lost roster spots and spots on the podium.” <a href="https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU10/20230727/116284/HHRG-118-JU10-Wstate-ScanlanP-20230727.pdf" title="“Paula Scanlan: Written testimony before the House Judiciary Subcommittee on the Constitution and Limited Government”, 2023-07-27">[7]</a> Her testimony gives no named cases showing that an unfair advantage caused those losses, or that a girl lost the aid she needed to attend college.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The mental-health finding concerns access to a good program: “Girls who play sport in positive environments report significantly lower levels of anxiety and depression than those who previously played or never played.” <a href="https://www.womenssportsfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Thriving-Through-Sport-Research-Report.pdf" title="“Thriving Through Sport: The Transformative Impact on Girls’ Mental Health”, 2024">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The leadership finding concerns benefits attributed to participation: “Half of women (48.6%) credit the skills acquired through sports for their leadership development.” <a href="https://www.womenssportsfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Play-to-Lead-Report-2024.pdf" title="“Play to Lead: The Generational Impact of Sports on Women’s Leadership”, 2024">[9]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> These benefits do not resolve who has a fair claim to a scarce place. The access argument depends on the fairness claim challenged in response 1; it does not independently establish that reserving benefits by birth sex advances equal opportunity.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> It protects physically vulnerable athletes from dangerous matchups and the lost games that safety concerns can cause.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> World Rugby links contact-sport risk to “the size, force- and power-producing advantages conferred by testosterone during puberty and adolescence, and the resultant player welfare risks this creates” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender" title="“Transgender Guidelines”, 2021-05-11">[10]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Its review explains the danger to a smaller player: “Forces and inertia faced by a smaller and slower player during frequent collisions are significantly greater when in contact with a much larger, faster player.” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender" title="“Transgender Guidelines”, 2021-05-11">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> World Rugby's review of 12 months of testosterone suppression concludes that “this comparatively small effect of testosterone reduction allows substantial and meaningful differences to remain.” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender" title="“Transgender Guidelines”, 2021-05-11">[10]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> In school contact competition such as rugby, excluding male competitors removes this source of size and force mismatch. That protects athletes who are less able to withstand those forces.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Safety concerns have also cost whole teams games. After Payton McNabb's 2022 volleyball injury, her school board voted “to forfeit the remaining varsity and junior varsity volleyball games against Highlands School, attended by the transgender player.” <a href="https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2023/apr/21/north-carolina-verge-transgender-sports-ban-after-/" title="Valerie Richardson, “North Carolina on verge of transgender sports ban after hearing from injured female athlete”, 2023-04-21">[11]</a> The report states: “The board cited safety concerns for players.” <a href="https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2023/apr/21/north-carolina-verge-transgender-sports-ban-after-/" title="Valerie Richardson, “North Carolina on verge of transgender sports ban after hearing from injured female athlete”, 2023-04-21">[11]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The bill also removes such cross-sex matchups as grounds for withdrawing girls' teams. Protecting athletes from added collision risks and preventing forfeits over these matchups serves [NAME]'s concern for the weak and vulnerable, including girls [NAME] does not know.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The rugby model and the cited forfeits do not establish added protection under the rules in force on January 14, 2025.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> World Rugby's collision estimate uses a comparison “when typical male mass is modelled against typical female body mass in the tackle.” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender" title="“Transgender Guidelines”, 2021-05-11">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO's strength review warns that transgender women before hormone treatment are not simply interchangeable with that male comparison group: “hormone-naive transwomen may not, on average, have the same athletic attributes as cisgender men.” <a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/15/865" title="“How does hormone transition in transgender women change body composition, muscle strength and haemoglobin? Systematic review with a focus on the implications for sport participation”, 2021-03-01">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> World Rugby's October 2020 research summary states: “To date, no direct studies on trans women rugby players.” <a href="https://resources.world.rugby/worldrugby/document/2020/10/09/a67e3cc3-7dea-4f1e-b523-2cba1073729d/Transgender-Research_Summary-of-data_ENGLISH-09.10.2020.pdf" title="“Summary of Transgender Biology and Performance Research”, 2020-10-09 (date in the official document filename)">[12]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Combining typical male measurements with general estimates of changes after hormone treatment does not establish the size and force mismatch of players eligible under existing school rules. The missing comparison is with the female opponents those rules already allow.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The volleyball injury occurred on “Sept. 1, 2022” <a href="https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2023/apr/21/north-carolina-verge-transgender-sports-ban-after-/" title="Valerie Richardson, “North Carolina on verge of transgender sports ban after hearing from injured female athlete”, 2023-04-21">[11]</a>, and the subsequent forfeits concerned games “against Highlands School, attended by the transgender player.” <a href="https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2023/apr/21/north-carolina-verge-transgender-sports-ban-after-/" title="Valerie Richardson, “North Carolina on verge of transgender sports ban after hearing from injured female athlete”, 2023-04-21">[11]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> North Carolina's later law “Prohibits male students from playing on middle school, high school, or collegiate athletics teams designated for females, women, or girls.” <a href="https://www.ncleg.gov/Legislation/SummariesPublication/Summary/2023/24/H574-SMBE-100(sl)-v-4/" title="“Summary of H574 (Session Year 2023): Fairness in Women&#x27;s Sports Act”, 2024-01-10 (last updated, as recorded in the official bill-summary index)">[13]</a> It “became effective August 16, 2023, and applies beginning with the 2023-2024 school year.” <a href="https://www.ncleg.gov/Legislation/SummariesPublication/Summary/2023/24/H574-SMBE-100(sl)-v-4/" title="“Summary of H574 (Session Year 2023): Fairness in Women&#x27;s Sports Act”, 2024-01-10 (last updated, as recorded in the official bill-summary index)">[13]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Those matchups were already barred before the federal vote. The episode does not establish an added protection for those teams, and it does not supply the missing comparison for schools where H.R.28 would change eligibility.</li></ul></td></tr>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.28?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.28 would require federally funded schools and colleges to exclude transgender girls and women from competition in girls’ and women’s sports under Title IX, the federal law against sex discrimination in education. It bars permitting “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> and determines sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> This would end participation already allowed under some rules: the NCAA’s May 2024 policy said, “Total serum testosterone thresholds apply to any transgender woman competing on an NCAA women’s team.” <a href="https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/inclusion/lgbtq/SSI_TransgenderSADeadlinesAndThresholdsWinter.pdf" title="National Collegiate Athletic Association, “NCAA Transgender Student-Athlete Participation Policy: Sport-Specific Testosterone Thresholds and Championship Eligibility Deadlines, Winter Sports”, 2024-05 (revision date stated in the document)">[14]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It takes away a route to learning and educational opportunities that other students keep.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Sports teach useful skills: “Sports provide meaningful opportunities for kids to feel confident in themselves and learn valuable life lessons about teamwork, leadership, and communication.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[15]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Team participation also brings educational openings. The bill itself names “scholarship, admission to an educational institution, or any other benefit that accompanies participating in the athletic program or activity.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The ban reaches beyond matches to “all programs or activities that are provided conditional upon participation with any athletic team.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The same birth-based rule also restricts nonbinary students it classifies as male. Nonbinary students do not identify only as girls or boys. The Equality Caucus explains: “It would also limit the ability of non-binary and intersex students to participate in these programs as well.” <a href="http://equality.house.gov/hr-28-119-pressrelease" title="Congressional Equality Caucus, press release, January 14, 2025">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Girls born with variations in sex traits also face uncertain eligibility. The National Women’s Law Center and Women’s Sports Foundation warn: “There is no principled way to apply the bill's unclear language to the many girls and young women born with intersex variations” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[17]</a>. They describe the resulting barrier: “Similar bans have been widely used to push girls and women born with these variations out of sports opportunities and have chilled their participation in school sports.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[17]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Blocking team competition and the activities tied to it takes away chances to learn and advance. [NAME]’s concern for equal opportunities includes transgender, nonbinary, and intersex students, giving a reason to oppose these barriers.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> Permission for “males to train or practice” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> restores neither eligibility to compete nor equal access to team places and benefits. Even practice is conditional: it is allowed only “so long as no female is deprived of a roster spot on a team or sport” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, among other protected opportunities.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Listing the benefits of a lost team place does not show that the eligibility rule denies equal opportunity.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Fair opportunity can require limits on entry to a sports category. In Clark, a court upheld excluding boys from girls’ volleyball because “due to average physiological differences, males would displace females to a substantial extent if they were allowed to compete for positions on the volleyball team.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/695/1126/443506/" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit; full opinion hosted by Justia, “Clark v. Arizona Interscholastic Association, 695 F.2d 1126 (9th Cir. 1982)”, 1982-12-02 (decision date)">[18]</a> The reason was a real difference affecting fair competition, rather than the mere existence of separate teams.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Previous eligibility under a testosterone limit does not establish equal competitive conditions. Reviewing physical traits after male puberty, Hilton and Lundberg reported that “the muscular advantage enjoyed by transgender women is only minimally reduced when testosterone is suppressed.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7846503/" title="Emma N. Hilton and Tommy R. Lundberg; Sports Medicine; full article hosted by PubMed Central, “Transgender Women in the Female Category of Sport: Perspectives on Testosterone Suppression and Performance Advantage”, 2020-12-08 (published online); 2021 issue">[19]</a> A current hormone threshold alone does not settle whether a category boundary is fair.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill restricts programs “designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It also permits students it classifies as male to “train or practice” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> with those programs under its conditions protecting female students’ places and benefits. Its text does not bar mixed competition or schooling.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Equal opportunity requires a fair basis for eligibility, not preservation of every existing route to scholarships, learning, or admission. The PRO must show why the disputed boundary unfairly blocks comparable students. Naming benefits and affected identities does not supply that step. Nor does the bill necessarily remove learning through the practice it permits.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It replaces individual protection against unfair treatment with automatic exclusion.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The rule makes no distinction by age or sport: “this bill is a blanket ban that treats every age student in every sport the same.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[20]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing law protected an individual girl’s participation. In its April 2024 B.P.J. decision, the Fourth Circuit noted that puberty-blocking treatment meant “B.P.J. has never experienced elevated levels of circulating testosterone.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; full opinion hosted by Justia, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[21]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The court held that applying West Virginia’s ban would “treat her worse than people to whom she is similarly situated, deprive her of any meaningful athletic opportunities, and do so on the basis of sex.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; full opinion hosted by Justia, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[21]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> H.R.28 would make birth sex decisive even in cases where an individual girl had established that exclusion treated her worse than comparable students. The fact that education uses federal money does not by itself make every condition on that money unjust.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO’s funding reason depends on establishing that the barred arrangement must remain available for fair opportunity, or that the limited enforcement process itself treats other students unjustly. Naming the stakes of noncompliance establishes neither. It does not repair the earlier fairness argument.</li></ul></td></tr>
