title: "H.R.28 × Universalism – Tolerance"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Tolerance. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to be tolerant toward all kinds of people and groups
- to listen to and understand people who are different to them
- to accept people even when they disagree with them

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.28?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Morgan's value of tolerance gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.28 because it makes schools exclude transgender girls and women and puts girls with different bodies or appearances under suspicion.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO does not show that tolerance gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.28, because accepting people does not decide who plays on girls' teams, and the bill does not tell anyone to reject or ignore transgender students.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> H.R.28 would make it a violation of Title IX, the federal law against sex discrimination in federally funded education, to let transgender girls and women compete in girls' and women's school sports. It bars “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> and defines sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill makes birth sex a blanket reason to exclude girls from girls' teams.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Its rule applies “equally to every sport, from K-12 schools to colleges.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[14]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Age, sport, skill and medical treatment cannot change the bill's birth-sex test. It rules out accepting a transgender girl based on her own circumstances.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> The practice exception leaves the competition ban in place. It allows “permitting males to train or practice with an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls so long as no female is deprived of a roster spot on a team or sport” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. Permission to practice under these conditions does not give a girl a place in competition.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> A boys' team does not replace acceptance on a girls' team. In a 2024 case involving a transgender girl who took puberty blockers, a federal appeals court explained that “offering B.P.J. a “choice” between not participating in sports and participating only on boys teams is no real choice at all.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan values accepting all kinds of people. A rule that excludes girls as a group, without considering them as individuals, gives Morgan a reason to oppose it.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> A rule about who plays on girls' teams does not reject the people it leaves out.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Separate teams for boys and girls were already lawful: “Long-standing Title IX regulations permit separation of the sexes in school sports programs provided schools offer equal athletic opportunities.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Even the court the PRO relies on said governments may draw and enforce that line: “We do not hold that government officials are forbidden from creating separate sports teams for boys and girls or that they lack power to police the line drawn between those teams.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> H.R.28 only sets where the line falls: “sex shall be recognized based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It covers only teams “designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It says nothing about friendship, respect, or how anyone treats transgender students.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> One rule for every age and sport is a choice about how to group teams, not a judgment of people. Tolerance asks Morgan to accept people. It does not say whether birth sex or gender identity should define the girls' team. The PRO assumes it does.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> Exclusion takes away a source of belonging from students who already face isolation and bullying.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Transgender, nonbinary and intersex students want the same things from teams as other students: “to be part of a team, learn sportsmanship, and challenge themselves.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> CDC's national survey of U.S. high school students found that “Transgender students reported the lowest prevalence of feeling close to others at school (36.6%)” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez and colleagues, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[16]</a>. It also found: “Approximately 40% of transgender and questioning students were bullied at school.” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez and colleagues, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[16]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Leadership Conference's letter explains how team exclusion worsens that isolation: “denying transgender, nonbinary, and intersex youth the chance to participate only serves to deny them an opportunity to be part of that community, further isolating and stigmatizing these youth.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The ban denies these students a way to be accepted as peers. Morgan's tolerance supports protecting that belonging, including when Morgan disagrees with how someone understands their gender.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO's evidence of isolation does not show that this rule causes it, and the bill leaves room to belong to a team.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The CDC report is a one-time snapshot, “a cross-sectional, school-based survey” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez and colleagues, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[16]</a>. It does not measure sports rules or compare students on and off girls' teams.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The CDC says these harms have many causes: “Violence, poor mental health, and suicide are not caused by any single factor” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez and colleagues, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[16]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The same report names other ways to build belonging, such as “student-led clubs offering a means for students with LGBTQ+ identities and allies to gather and provide support” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez and colleagues, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[16]</a>. H.R.28 does not touch these.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill lets schools keep transgender girls in team practice. It “does not prohibit male individuals from training or practicing with programs or activities for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[2]</a>, as long as no girl loses a spot or benefit. Practice still lets a student be part of a team and challenge herself.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO's letter says exclusion deepens isolation but gives no evidence that this rule does. Morgan's concern about bullying supports protecting these students. It does not pick a team rule.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> The bill turns existing choices to include transgender students into federal violations.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Inclusion was already part of school sports before the vote: “Many State schools and athletic associations across the country have allowed equal participation for transgender athletes for years” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[14]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing law also protected participation in at least one case. In April 2024, the Fourth Circuit ruled against applying West Virginia's sports ban to a transgender middle school girl: “B.P.J. has shown applying the Act to her would violate Title IX” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> H.R.28 would replace room for inclusion, including protection under Title IX in that case, with an express ban under the same law.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan values accepting people despite differences. Forcing schools to abandon rules that include those students gives Morgan a reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The legal protection the PRO cites was narrow, so the bill does not undo any broad duty to include.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court limited its ruling to “this particular case” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a> and “her specific Title IX claim” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That student had not gone through male puberty: “The undisputed evidence shows B.P.J. has never gone through the Tanner 2 stage.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a> (Tanner 2 is the start of puberty.)</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court refused to go further: “We also do not hold that Title IX requires schools to allow every transgender girl to play on girls teams” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> No federal rule required inclusion when the House voted. Courts had “reached different conclusions” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a>. The Education Department's proposed rule against blanket bans “was never finalized” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a> and was withdrawn on “Dec. 26, 2024” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Before the bill, including transgender girls was a local choice. H.R.28 changes who sets the team rule. Tolerance concerns how Morgan treats people. It does not say which level of government should write sports rules.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 4:</strong> The birth-sex rule invites scrutiny of girls who do not fit common ideas of how girls should look.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill sets a birth-biology test without saying how schools must check it: “H.R. 28 provides neither a specific enforcement mechanism nor resources to ensure that students participating in athletics meet these new standards.” <a href="https://hayes.house.gov/2025/1/hayes-votes-against-federal-overreach-in-student-athletics" title="Hayes, press release, January 14, 2025">[17]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The National Women's Law Center and Women's Sports Foundation warn that sex checks are “especially likely to be used to target Black and brown women and girls who do not conform to white ideals of femininity” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>. Their warning also covers “other women and girls who do not conform to sexist stereotypes, and nonbinary and gender nonconforming students” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Appearance-based accusations already caused harm. The same letter describes how “a Utah school board member publicly questioned the gender of a 16-year-old cisgender girl playing on a high school basketball team who wore short hair and baggy clothes.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a> She was not transgender. Afterward, “the student was subjected to harassment, bullying, and threats of violence, necessitating police protection for her and her family.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Requiring schools to enforce this test gives such suspicion a new purpose. Morgan's tolerance supports opposing a rule that exposes girls to scrutiny for looking different.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The bill's test turns on birth records, not looks, so the PRO has not shown that it adds scrutiny of how girls look.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The test is “reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It does not mention hair, clothes, build or race.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The committee chair said a record settles it: “There is no requirement for inspections, and there is no necessary effort other than going to a person's birth certificate.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[18]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO's Utah case began with one official's social media post, not with any eligibility check. The official “posted on Facebook a picture clearly identifying specific students” <a href="https://schools.utah.gov/board/utah/resources/BoardResolution202402.pdf" title="Utah State Board of Education (signed by James Moss, USBE Board Chair), “Public censure of Board Member Natalie Cline (Utah State Board of Education resolution, BoardResolution202402)”, 2024-02-14">[12]</a>. Utah's state school board voted 14–0 to formally rebuke the official: “the Board does hereby censure, disavow, and formally express its disapproval of Member Natalie Cline’s conduct described above.” <a href="https://schools.utah.gov/board/utah/resources/BoardResolution202402.pdf" title="Utah State Board of Education (signed by James Moss, USBE Board Chair), “Public censure of Board Member Natalie Cline (Utah State Board of Education resolution, BoardResolution202402)”, 2024-02-14">[12]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Harassing girls over their looks is wrong, and Morgan's tolerance supports stopping it. Utah treated it as misconduct. The PRO gives no link between a birth-record test and more of it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 5:</strong> The bill puts girls born with differences in sex traits at risk of exclusion.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Its birth-biology test leaves girls with intersex traits facing an unclear rule. The women's rights groups explain: “There is no principled way to apply the bill's unclear language to the many girls and young women born with intersex variations” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> They report a record of exclusion under such rules: “Similar bans have been widely used to push girls and women born with these variations out of sports opportunities and have chilled their participation in school sports.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Tolerance includes accepting people whose bodies differ from the usual male or female patterns. Making those differences a risk to a girl's place in sports gives Morgan another reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO does not show that the bill would exclude girls born with differences in sex traits.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill bars only “a person whose sex is male” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. An intersex variation (a difference in sex traits present at birth) is not named as a reason to exclude anyone.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The committee chair named the birth certificate as the check: “They simply have to go to the birth certificate.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[13]</a> Under that check, a girl recorded as female at birth can play.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO's letter offers only a general claim about “Similar bans” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>. It names no variation, school or case, and does not show how H.R.28's test would treat any such girl.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO shows that the wording could be unclear, not that these girls would lose their place. What is missing is proof that their protection changes, not a count of how many are affected.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 6:</strong> The bill's required study leaves out the experiences of the students its ban excludes.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study asks about losses from inclusion: “The study shall document the adverse psychological, developmental, participatory, and sociological results to girls of allowing males to compete” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It does not ask about the costs of exclusion to transgender students.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Those students have experiences lawmakers should hear. One transgender athlete asked them to “think of the children behind the story who are just trying to play a game with their peers.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan values listening to people who are different. Ordering a study that leaves their losses out while imposing their exclusion conflicts with that value.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> A study with a narrow legal job does not stop anyone from hearing transgender students.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study exists “to determine the meaning of the phrase `any other benefit' as used in subsection (d)(4)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, the clause on shared practice. To define that phrase, the study must look at what girls would lose.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill only orders a “report that contains the results of such study” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It limits no other study or hearing.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Lawmakers heard transgender students' side before voting. A member put a civil rights letter in the record: “I include in the Record the second letter I referenced during general debate on H.R. 28” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[19]</a>. The letter says these youth want “to be part of a team, learn sportsmanship, and challenge themselves” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a>. A member also read a transgender athlete's words on the floor: “As Flynn, a successful trans athlete, said” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A focused report on girls' losses is not a refusal to listen. Morgan's value calls for hearing both groups, and the bill allows both.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The PRO leaves out the girls who lose places, whom Morgan's value also asks Morgan to hear.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Morgan's value includes listening to people who differ from Morgan and accepting people who disagree.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Girls can lose places to transgender competitors. In B.P.J.'s case, the dissenting judge wrote that “over one hundred biological girls participating in these events were displaced by and denied athletic opportunities because of B.P.J.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/23-1078/23-1078-2024-04-16.html" title="Judge Toby Heytens (majority opinion), “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, No. 23-1078 (4th Cir. 2024)”, 2024-04-16">[15]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> H.R.28's study is built to record such losses. It “shall document the adverse psychological, developmental, participatory, and sociological results to girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Tolerance asks Morgan to weigh these girls' views too. The PRO counts only one group, so it cannot show that tolerance points toward opposing the bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> H.R.28's exclusion rule, loss of belonging, reversal of existing inclusion, risks to girls who look or develop differently, and failure to study excluded students' losses give Morgan's tolerance a reason to oppose it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO does not show that tolerance favors one team rule over another, or that H.R.28 causes the rejection, scrutiny or refusal to listen that the PRO claims.</p></td></tr>
