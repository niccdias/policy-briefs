<p><strong>H.R.28 · the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025</strong></p><p><span data-release-part="119_H_R_28:description">The bill would change Title IX, the federal law against sex discrimination in schools and colleges that receive federal money. Those schools could not let a person the bill classifies as male take part in a sports program for women or girls. The bill would decide sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” The bill would still allow males to train or practice with those teams if no female loses a team place, a chance to play, a scholarship, admission to a school or another sports benefit. The bill also orders a federal study of benefits that girls could lose.</span></p><p><strong>Comparison:</strong> <span data-release-part="119_H_R_28:comparison">If this bill took effect on January 14, 2025, versus law in force then</span></p><details><summary>Bill version and evidence cutoff</summary><p>Engrossed in House · 2025-01-14. <h2>Tradition</h2><ul data-release-part="HR28_TR:value"><li>to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking</li><li>to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion</li><li>to honor the traditional practices of their culture</li></ul><p><span data-release-part="HR28_TR:reference">Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference. Tradition here concerns preserving, observing or passing down identifiable inherited customs, religious practices and ways of life, valued for their continuity with the past. Religious endorsement of a general moral goal alone does not establish that connection.</span></p>

## Shared claims and background

<div class="approved-shared">
<details id="HR28_C01"><summary><span data-release-part="HR28_C01:title">The sports rule and its definition of sex</span></summary><p><span data-release-part="HR28_C01:statement">This rule covers a school or college that receives federal money and runs, sponsors or facilitates sports. It may not let a person whose sex is male, as the bill defines it, take part in a sports program designated for women or girls. The bill defines sex for this rule based only on reproductive biology and genetics at birth.</span></p><div data-release-part="HR28_C01:background"><p>The rule covers only programs designated for women or girls at places that receive federal money. The definition applies “For the purposes of this subsection” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. The text does not name transgender, nonbinary or intersex students, and it does not set a way to check sex.</p><p>A recipient of federal financial assistance is an organization that receives federal money. Title IX is the federal law against sex discrimination in education. It “prohibits sex discrimination in federally funded education programs.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029215055/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[2]</a></p></div><h3>Supporting evidence</h3><div data-release-part="HR28_C01:support"><p>The bill says it “shall be a violation of subsection (a) for a recipient of Federal financial assistance” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> to allow this participation. It adds that “sex shall be recognized based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></p></div><h3>Response</h3><div data-release-part="HR28_C01:response"></div><details><summary>Evidence about size, timing and reach</summary><div data-release-part="HR28_C01:strength"></div></details></details>
