title: "H.R.28 × Tradition"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Tradition. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking
- to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion
- to honor the traditional practices of their culture

*Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

H.R.28 contains no such provision.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A birth-sex rule can use records. Texas’s 2021 law uses “the student's official birth certificate” <a href="https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/873/billtext/html/HB00025F.htm" title="Texas Legislature, “H.B. 25, 87th Texas Legislature, Third Called Session — Enrolled version”, 2021-10-17 (enrolled text; date of final House concurrence certified in the text)">[23]</a>, with sex “entered at or near the time of the student's birth” <a href="https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/873/billtext/html/HB00025F.htm" title="Texas Legislature, “H.B. 25, 87th Texas Legislature, Third Called Session — Enrolled version”, 2021-10-17 (enrolled text; date of final House concurrence certified in the text)">[23]</a>. The bill’s floor manager likewise proposed records: “They simply have to go to the birth certificate.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[24]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Utah report describes a public accusation “inciting threats from her followers on social media” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, The Salt Lake Tribune, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07 (updated 2024-02-09)">[25]</a>. It reports no intimate exam of that girl. In a separate dispute, officials checked records: “The USHAA determined in that first case, after pulling the accused student’s records back to kindergarten, that she has always been female.” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, The Salt Lake Tribune, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07 (updated 2024-02-09)">[25]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Christian modesty does not forbid every medical check. Focus on the Family allows necessary care: “there are times when it’s necessary for a doctor or parent to check their genital area, such as during a medical exam.” <a href="https://www.focusonthefamily.com/parenting/teach-appropriate-touch/" title="Jackson Greer, “How to Teach Appropriate Touch to Your Kids”, 2016-03-14">[20]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> An eligibility rule does not itself mean forbidden exposure. The cited examples do not establish that H.R.28 would add needless intimate exams or that compliance would breach Christian modesty.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill takes school-sports decisions away from parents and local communities, breaking the tradition of local control of schools.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Before the bill, states and local bodies made these rules: “State legislatures, state athletic associations, and local school boards have passed laws and developed policies” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[8]</a> on who plays on girls' teams.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> H.R.28 would replace those choices with one federal rule for “every age, every sport, and every level of competition” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[18]</a>. Rep. Donald Norcross said it “inflicts blanket bans without the ability of states, local governments, schools, and parents to make reasonable exceptions.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260211002049/https://norcross.house.gov/2025/1/rep-norcross-releases-statement-on-h-r-28-vote" title="Norcross, press release, January 14, 2025 (archived copy; the original page is gone)">[26]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The religious right holds that school decisions belong close to home. The Family Research Council says, “To the maximum extent possible, decision-making regarding public education should be at the local level, reflecting the priorities and values of parents and residents of the local community.” <a href="https://www.frc.org/education" title="Family Research Council, “Education (issue position page)”, 2023 (Internet Archive capture of the undated page; the live page carries the same position statement)">[27]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Local control is an old American practice. The Supreme Court wrote, “No single tradition in public education is more deeply rooted than local control over the operation of schools” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/418/717/" title="Supreme Court of the United States (opinion of Chief Justice Burger), via Justia, “Milliken v. Bradley, 418 U.S. 717 (1974)”, 1974-07-25">[28]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> H.R.28 moves a choice about local school teams from parents, school boards and states to Washington.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> That breaks a tradition the religious right defends, so Morgan has a reason to oppose the bill.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> Supporters may say Title IX already governs school sports. But Title IX never settled this question: “Neither the statute nor the regulations currently address how Title IX applies to transgender athletes.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[8]</a> H.R.28 would add a new federal command, not restate an old one.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The religious right’s support for local schools does not establish a tradition-based reason to oppose a federal rule that protects its beliefs about sex.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Family Research Council qualifies local control with “To the maximum extent possible” <a href="https://www.frc.org/education" title="Family Research Council, “Education (issue position page)”, 2023 (Internet Archive capture of the undated page; the live page carries the same position statement)">[27]</a>. It also says schools should avoid “interfering with the transmission of parental values to children” <a href="https://www.frc.org/education" title="Family Research Council, “Education (issue position page)”, 2023 (Internet Archive capture of the undated page; the live page carries the same position statement)">[27]</a>. Its stated concern includes preserving what parents teach.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Federal law already limited local choices on this very issue. In the 2024 West Virginia case, a federal appeals court held that “application of the state law to this plaintiff violated Title IX” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[8]</a>. The ruling concerned one transgender girl, rather than striking down every school’s sex-based sports rule.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> As of January 14, 2025, local communities could already face federal limits on keeping girls’ teams based on birth sex. Writing that rule into Title IX, the federal law against sex discrimination in schools, would support communities trying to preserve it.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill’s sponsor lists “Family Research Council” <a href="https://steube.house.gov/press-releases/steubes-protection-of-women-and-girls-in-sports-act-passes-u-s-house-in-bipartisan-vote/" title="Steube, press release, January 14, 2025">[29]</a> among its endorsers. The same group whose local-control statement the PRO cites supports this federal sports rule.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO’s general local-control quotation does not show that this bill breaks the religious right’s tradition of protecting the beliefs parents pass on.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The bill’s main rule would make girls’ sports follow a Christian teaching the PRO leaves out.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> For schools that receive federal money, H.R.28 would make it a Title IX violation “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Its birth-sex definition would govern that rule.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Southern Baptist Convention affirms “God’s good design that gender identity is determined by biological sex and not by one’s self-perception” <a href="https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-transgender-identity/" title="Southern Baptist Convention (SBC.net), “On Transgender Identity (Resolution, 2014 Annual Meeting)”, 2014-06-01 (page date; adopted by messengers June 10, 2014)">[3]</a>. Keeping male and female distinct on that basis preserves a stated religious belief.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Ethics &amp; Religious Liberty Commission connects that teaching directly to sex-separated sports: “These biological differences are the purpose of sports, separated by sex.” <a href="https://erlc.com/policy-content/erlc-supports-the-protection-of-women-and-girls-in-sports-act-2025/" title="Policy Staff / Ethics &amp; Religious Liberty Commission, “ERLC Supports the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act 2025”, 2025-01-13">[2]</a> Its January 13, 2025 statement says, “The ERLC calls on Congress to pass the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act.” <a href="https://erlc.com/policy-content/erlc-supports-the-protection-of-women-and-girls-in-sports-act-2025/" title="Policy Staff / Ethics &amp; Religious Liberty Commission, “ERLC Supports the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act 2025”, 2025-01-13">[2]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Where those schools let students enter girls’ competition by gender identity, H.R.28 would require birth sex instead. This preserves a belief Morgan seeks to pass on.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> H.R.28 would expose girls to body checks that Christian modesty forbids and would take school-sports choices from local communities, so Tradition gives Morgan a reason to oppose it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO has not established its claimed breach of modesty or Christian school traditions, while the bill’s main rule preserves a belief central to Morgan’s tradition.</p></td></tr>
