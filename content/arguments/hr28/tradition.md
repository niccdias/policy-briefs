title: "H.R.28 × Tradition"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to honor the traditional practices of their culture. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If effective on January 14, 2025, H.R.28 would make male participation in female competition a Title IX violation for education programs receiving federal aid. Showing that sport and friendship help teach virtues does not show that preserving a placement contrary to that rule carries on the practice these teachings describe.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars “a person whose sex is male” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>; it does not name intersex variations as a ground for exclusion.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO's intersex source warns, “There is no principled way to apply the bill's unclear language to the many girls and young women born with intersex variations” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[18]</a>. The letter gives no example or rule showing which variations the bill would classify as male.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Having an intersex variation does not by itself put a child in the bill's excluded group. The warning leaves that step unshown. It cannot establish the claimed loss of these children's shared religious practice.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> The bill takes a decision about children's upbringing away from families and local groups already responsible for it.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Southern Baptist Convention teaches that “parents are the primary stewards of and decision-makers for their children, rather than teachers, doctors, or the state” <a href="https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-the-god-given-rights-and-responsibilities-of-parents/" title="“On the God-Given Rights and Responsibilities of Parents”, 2024-06-12">[20]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Catholic teaching protects work that smaller groups can do themselves; it calls it an injustice to “assign to a greater and higher association what lesser and subordinate organizations can do.” <a href="https://www.catholicculture.org/culture/library/view.cfm?id=7214" title="“Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church: Chapter Four”, 2004 (publication year stated on the source page)">[21]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Sports rules are already part of local responsibility: “Currently, schools, parents, and communities manage youth sports leagues and write rules about who can participate in different sports at different levels.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[15]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing individual reviews already weigh safety and fairness. As Representative Norcross explained, “It inflicts blanket bans without the ability of states, local governments, schools, and parents to make reasonable exceptions.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260211002049/https://norcross.house.gov/2025/1/rep-norcross-releases-statement-on-h-r-28-vote" title="Norcross, press release, January 14, 2025 (archived copy; the original page is gone)">[22]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Even when parents and a willing school approve a child's participation after individual review, the federal rule would bar girls' competition if it classifies that child as male. Preserving the family and local roles taught by [NAME]'s tradition gives a reason to oppose that loss of authority.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO has not shown that the particular sports exception removed belongs to the family and local duties its religious sources protect.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited Baptist resolution places parents' decisions “under the authority of God, recognizing that they will be held responsible for their choices” <a href="https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-the-god-given-rights-and-responsibilities-of-parents/" title="“On the God-Given Rights and Responsibilities of Parents”, 2024-06-12">[20]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Southern Baptist Ethics &amp; Religious Liberty Commission explains: “The duty of parental authority is not the right to teach children whatever they desire. Rather, it is normed by what is good, true, and righteous.” <a href="https://erlc.com/research/teach-them-diligently-to-your-children-a-biblical-and-theological-foundation-for-parental-rights/" title="Center, “A Biblical and Theological Foundation for Parental Rights”, 2024-05-23">[23]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> That same body addressed this sports decision on January 13, 2025: “Schools that allow biological males to participate in female sports programs are discriminating against biological females.” <a href="https://erlc.com/policy-content/erlc-supports-the-protection-of-women-and-girls-in-sports-act-2025/" title="Policy Staff, “ERLC Supports the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act 2025”, 2025-01-13">[24]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> In this account, parental and school approval does not turn that eligibility exception into an exercise of Christian care or rightful parental teaching. The PRO cannot infer that religious authority protects the exception simply because parents and a school want it.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Catholic principle of leaving work to smaller groups also serves duties beyond those groups. The PRO's source says, “To ensure the common good, the government of each country has the specific duty to harmonize the different sectoral interests with the requirements of justice” <a href="https://www.catholicculture.org/culture/library/view.cfm?id=7214" title="“Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church: Chapter Four”, 2004 (publication year stated on the source page)">[21]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Illinois's 2024–25 policy made eligibility a decision for the statewide sports association: “IHSA will make final rulings on student participation.” <a href="https://www.ihsa.org/documents/flip/Handbook/2024-25/Handbook.pdf" title="“IHSA Handbook with Illustrations, 2024–25 School Term”, 2024 (2024–25 edition; exact publication day not stated)">[25]</a> Its review considered whether eligibility would be “inconsistent with concepts of fairness in competition or present a risk of injury to the participants” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Showing that a lower body can run a review does not establish that its eligibility standard meets the religious account of justice. Its claim of lost religious authority therefore lacks the needed link.</li></ul></td></tr>
