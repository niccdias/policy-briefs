**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://web.archive.org/web/20260603151225/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28)), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 14, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, January 14, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to honor the traditional practices of their culture. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference. Tradition here concerns preserving, observing or passing down identifiable inherited customs, religious practices and ways of life, valued for their continuity with the past. Religious endorsement of a general moral goal alone does not establish that connection.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If effective on January 14, 2025, H.R.28 would make male participation in female competition a Title IX violation for education programs receiving federal aid. It would prohibit permitting “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It would base sex solely on “a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Male training or practice would remain allowed when it “does not deprive any female of corresponding opportunities or benefits.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260603151225/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[2]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> At covered schools receiving federal money, H.R.28 would make it unlawful “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It defines sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> This would end participation that some schools allowed on January 14, 2025: that day's debate recorded, “Many State schools and athletic associations across the country have allowed equal participation for transgender athletes for years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029195620/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[14]</a>.</p></div>
