title: "H.R.28 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.28?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law on January 14, 2025, H.R.28 would add a national eligibility rule to Title IX, the law against sex discrimination in federally funded education. It would make it unlawful for a funding recipient “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Sex would depend on “a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Its training and practice exception applies “as long as such training or practice does not deprive any female of corresponding opportunities or benefits.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[2]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It gives the United States an enforceable, lasting safeguard against injury risks from physical mismatches in women’s contact competition.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Title IX lacked an express rule for transgender athletes: “Neither the statute nor the regulations currently address how Title IX applies to transgender athletes.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The NCAA’s May 2024 rules allowed transgender women to qualify for women’s rugby through a testosterone limit. That removes this source of competition risk rather than relying on a testosterone limit to remove it.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Title IX already provides ways to enforce the new duty: “Title IX is primarily enforced in two ways: through private litigation in federal court and by federal agencies that distribute funding to education programs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Making that participation an express Title IX violation gives federal agencies and affected students a legal basis to demand compliance across covered schools, including where state rules permit it.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Writing the eligibility test into the law also prevents a future administration from reversing that duty simply by changing an agency’s interpretation of Title IX.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The January 2025 ruling had already removed the 2024 Title IX regulation: “a federal district court vacated them in January 2025.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a> Removing that regulation had not enacted the bill’s express national eligibility duty.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Enforceable, lasting protection against this physical risk strengthens the country’s ability to defend its citizens through federal institutions. Its national reach and protection from reversal by agency interpretation do not show that those institutions become better able to meet threats to society.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO needs a link from its sports protection to a safer, more stable country or a stronger state able to defend its citizens. Federal enforcement and a lasting rule do not supply that link.</li></ul></td></tr>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.28?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.28 would make allowing transgender girls and women classified as male at birth into girls’ and women’s competition a federal Title IX violation. Title IX “prohibits sex discrimination in federally funded education programs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a> The bill bars recipients of federal aid from allowing “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It defines sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> This changes the January 14, 2025 baseline: “Many State schools and athletic associations across the country have allowed equal participation for transgender athletes for years” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>. That baseline already reflects the loss of the 2024 Title IX regulations: “a federal district court vacated them in January 2025.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[3]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> The birth-sex rule creates pressure for invasive checks that expose girls to sexual abuse.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Schools must meet the new rule without a clear method for checking sex. Representative Hayes explains: “H.R. 28 provides neither a specific enforcement mechanism nor resources to ensure that students participating in athletics meet these new standards.” <a href="https://hayes.house.gov/2025/1/hayes-votes-against-federal-overreach-in-student-athletics" title="Hayes, press release, January 14, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Another birth-sex sports ban shows the enforcement risk. In 2024, a federal appeals court described Idaho’s law as allowing sex challenges and checks “including gynecological exams.” <a href="https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/06/07/20-35813.pdf" title="“Hecox v. Little, No. 20-35813, amended opinion”, 2024-06-07">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The National Women’s Law Center and Women’s Sports Foundation warn that checks under H.R.28 “may include forcing women and girls to submit to a variety of invasive, humiliating, and unscientific practices” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a>. They identify the danger: “These procedures make all women and girls vulnerable to sexual abuse” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a>.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The floor manager proposes checking “a person's birth certificate.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[12]</a> The bill gives that document no final say. Its test remains “reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> His assurance does not prevent schools from adopting invasive checks.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Creating an abuse risk in schools weakens the state’s protection of its citizens. [NAME]’s commitment to a strong state that can defend them gives a reason to oppose this rule.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO’s evidence does not show that H.R.28 would push schools toward invasive checks and increase exposure to sexual abuse.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Idaho added a specific rule for medical checks: “A dispute regarding a student’s sex shall be resolved by the school or institution by requesting that the student provide a health examination and consent form or other statement signed by the student’s personal health care provider that shall verify the student’s biological sex.” <a href="https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/06/07/20-35813.pdf" title="“Hecox v. Little, No. 20-35813, amended opinion”, 2024-06-07">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The same court reported: “Most states that have instituted categorical bans on transgender participation in student athletics have verified sex via a student’s birth certificate.” <a href="https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/06/07/20-35813.pdf" title="“Hecox v. Little, No. 20-35813, amended opinion”, 2024-06-07">[10]</a> It also said that, among the other 24 states with restrictions, “none has authorized a similar sex verification process.” <a href="https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2024/06/07/20-35813.pdf" title="“Hecox v. Little, No. 20-35813, amended opinion”, 2024-06-07">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> H.R.28 defines sex through “a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> without adding Idaho’s procedure for medical checks. Its floor manager proposed using records: “There is no requirement for inspections, and there is no necessary effort other than going to a person's birth certificate.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[12]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Idaho’s added procedure explains the risk of invasive checks in that example. Its warning therefore does not establish the added pressure needed for its abuse claim.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> The rule invites public accusations that threaten students and their communities.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights coalition warns that H.R.28 “will invite scrutiny and harassment of any other student perceived by anyone as not conforming to sex stereotypes” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[13]</a>. The risk reaches girls whose bodies or appearance prompt suspicion, whether they are transgender or not.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Such accusations already threaten school safety. The Salt Lake Tribune reported: “A Utah high school student needs police protection after a state school board member publicly singled out and suggested the student was transgender — without evidence — inciting threats from her followers on social media.” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07; updated 2024-02-09">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Hostility aimed at a group can intimidate a whole community. The Justice Department explains this wider harm in discussing hate crimes: “They represent an attack not just on the individual victim, but on the victim's community.” <a href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/dag/testimony/dagjudic080499.htm" title="“Prepared Statement Concerning Hate Crimes before the Committee on the Judiciary, U.S. House of Representatives”, 1999-08-04">[15]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Extending birth-sex eligibility rules to federally funded schools nationwide creates more occasions for this intimidation. Protecting citizens and their communities from threats gives [NAME] a reason to oppose it.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The Utah reporting does not establish that extending the eligibility rule would add public targeting or threats.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The same official had already prompted threats before Utah’s 2022 ban. The report dates her reprimand to “fall 2021” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07; updated 2024-02-09">[14]</a> and explains: “That came after Cline posted a message critical of LGBTQ students that led some of her followers to threaten violence.” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07; updated 2024-02-09">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The report’s wider pattern is framed as events after the law: “Since her measure was passed by the Legislature — in a vote overriding the governor’s veto — there has been a slew of accusations from parents and others trying to call out players they think might be transgender at schools across the state.” <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/sports/2024/02/07/utah-school-board-member-natalie/" title="Courtney Tanner, “Utah school board member Natalie Cline questions high school athlete’s gender”, 2024-02-07; updated 2024-02-09">[14]</a> It does not identify a change in those parents’ conduct caused by the law.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The prior threats give a concrete reason to question whether the eligibility rule explains the official’s later attack. The wider report does not separate an increase caused by the rule from hostility already present. The coalition predicts that increase but supplies no evidence that makes this distinction. The Justice Department’s explanation of community harm concerns what threats do; it does not show that H.R.28 would add threats. Nationwide coverage does not fill that missing link.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> Exclusion cuts access to sports as a way to protect lives and build safer, more resilient communities.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Ordering transgender students onto teams based on sex at birth drives some out of sport. A 2025 study summarizes earlier research: “policies requiring youth to play on teams with their assigned sex, dissuade youth from participating” <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2465702" title="Sarah M. Kaja, Amy L. Gower, Benjamin Parchem, Samantha J. Adler, Jenifer K. McGuire, “Sports Team Participation, Bias-Based Bullying, and Mental Health Among Transgender and Gender Diverse Adolescents”, 2025-03-11">[16]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The loss also reaches girls born with variations in sex traits, often called intersex variations. The women’s sports organizations report: “Similar bans have been widely used to push girls and women born with these variations out of sports opportunities and have chilled their participation in school sports.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a></li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The conditional practice exception permits “males to train or practice” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It guarantees no place in practice and leaves the competition ban intact.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Where schools now include them, the bill cuts off a route into sport for students it excludes or deters.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Sports offer social support linked to better mental health. A study of transgender teens and others with varied gender identities found that team athletes had a “lower prevalence of elevated depressive and anxiety symptoms than similar adolescents who did not participate” <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2465702" title="Sarah M. Kaja, Amy L. Gower, Benjamin Parchem, Samantha J. Adler, Jenifer K. McGuire, “Sports Team Participation, Bias-Based Bullying, and Mental Health Among Transgender and Gender Diverse Adolescents”, 2025-03-11">[16]</a>. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) also associates youth sports with “Reduced risk of suicide” <a href="https://odphp.health.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/YSS_Report_OnePager_2020-08-31_web.pdf" title="U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, “Benefits of Youth Sports”, 2020-09-17">[17]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The threat to young lives is substantial. CDC’s national 2023 high school survey found: “Approximately 26% of transgender and questioning students attempted suicide in the past year” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="CDCMMWR, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[18]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Sports provide a route to exercise and protection against disease. HHS associates sports with “Increased physical activity levels” <a href="https://odphp.health.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/YSS_Report_OnePager_2020-08-31_web.pdf" title="U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, “Benefits of Youth Sports”, 2020-09-17">[17]</a> and physical activity with “Reduced risk of cancer and diabetes” <a href="https://odphp.health.gov/sites/default/files/2020-09/YSS_Report_OnePager_2020-08-31_web.pdf" title="U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, “Benefits of Youth Sports”, 2020-09-17">[17]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Sports also provide a setting linked to less use of illegal drugs.
