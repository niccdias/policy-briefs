title: "H.R.28 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.28](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28), the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act of 2025. Judged as of January 14, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Security – Societal. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- that their country is secure and stable
- to have a strong state that can defend its citizens
- that their country protect itself against all threats

*Read "their country" as the United States.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor H.R.28?

<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> H.R.28 makes it a violation of Title IX, the federal ban on sex discrimination in education, for schools receiving federal funds “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It defines sex by “reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> Training and practice remain allowed “as long as such training or practice does not deprive any female of corresponding opportunities or benefits.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[2]</a></p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill gives the federal government an enforceable way to protect girls and women from injury caused by physical mismatches in school sports.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> World Rugby’s tackle model compares players of typical male and female weight with an all-female matchup. The mixed pairing “increases the magnitude of known risk factors for head injuries by between 20% and 30%.” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender/women" title="World Rugby, “Guideline for transgender women”, 2021-05-12">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Lowering testosterone, a hormone, after male puberty leaves substantial gaps in size and strength. Preventing an identified injury risk is a concrete way for a strong state to defend its citizens.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO's injury evidence covers players who went through male puberty in a contact sport, but the bill bans far more people and is not built around injury.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> World Rugby modeled tackles between elite adults: “we assessed the range of masses of players at the international level” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender/women" title="World Rugby, “Guideline for transgender women”, 2021-05-12">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> It warns that its findings may not carry over to other sports: “not all sports are affected similarly by the variables we have weighted as crucial for rugby (size, strength, speed, power)” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender/women" title="World Rugby, “Guideline for transgender women”, 2021-05-12">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Its concern is male puberty, so it lets some transgender women play: “Transgender women who transitioned pre-puberty and have not experienced the biological effects of testosterone during puberty and adolescence can play women's rugby” <a href="https://www.world.rugby/the-game/player-welfare/guidelines/transgender/women" title="World Rugby, “Guideline for transgender women”, 2021-05-12">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> H.R.28 bans by birth sex alone, set “solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It applies to every girls' sport, in schools “including in public elementary and secondary schools and in colleges and universities” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Courts reviewing similar state bans found no proven safety link. In New Hampshire, a federal court found “the state had not shown that the participation of transgender girls impacted fairness or safety of athletes on girls’ sports teams” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>. It added that “neither of the plaintiffs had undergone male puberty” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill lets males “train or practice” with girls' teams <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. Its limits protect places and benefits, such as a “roster spot on a team or sport” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, not physical safety.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study the bill orders lists the harms to document: “adverse psychological, developmental, participatory, and sociological results” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. Sports injury is not on the list.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Even in college sports, few athletes are affected. Citing NCAA testimony to Congress, Rep. Scott reported that “of the over 500,000 college athletes participating in NCAA sports, less than 10 are transgender” <a href="http://bobbyscott.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/scott-gop-bill-makes-school-sports-less-safe-women-and-girls" title="Scott, press release, January 15, 2025">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO's injury evidence fits only a small slice of what the bill bans. Federal enforcement adds a penalty. It does not prove that the ban protects citizens.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill gives schools and federal officials a clearer, steadier national rule.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Title IX left transgender athletes’ eligibility unsettled: “Neither the statute nor the regulations currently address how Title IX applies to transgender athletes.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The 2024 federal rules had already been struck down when the House voted: “a federal district court vacated them in January 2025” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> State restrictions left gaps. In the January 14 debate, Representative Walberg reported that “nearly half of the States have no protections in place for female athletes” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Writing the same duty into federal law makes protection less dependent on changing agency readings of Title IX or different state bans. That gives the government a steadier basis for action and supports the national stability Morgan values.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> A federal ban would not settle the rules; it would bring new court fights, unclear terms and clashes with states.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> When the House voted, courts were split on bans like this: “Some federal courts have ruled that the application of state laws banning transgender athletes from participating in sports consistent with their gender identity can violate these laws.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Congressional Research Service (CRS) warns that the same fight would reach H.R.28. If the Supreme Court applied a stricter test to such laws, that “could raise the question of whether such legislation comports with the Equal Protection Clause” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>, the Constitution's promise of equal treatment.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill leaves its own limits undefined. It orders a study “to determine the meaning of the phrase `any other benefit'” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, the phrase that decides when mixed practice is allowed.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Its birth-sex test is unclear for girls born intersex, meaning with sex traits that do not fit typical male or female patterns: “There is no principled way to apply the bill's unclear language to the many girls and young women born with intersex variations” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill would override states and schools that chose another rule. Some “allow students to compete on teams that align with their gender identity” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In 2025, federal agencies pushed this same rule on such states through Title IX. Because of Maine's policy, the Agriculture Department “froze certain funds relating to school meals and child nutrition programs” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>. A court blocked the freeze, ruling that the agency's actions “violated Title IX’s enforcement requirements” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> California also refused: “The agency issued a proposed resolution agreement, which the state rejected.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill would swap the disputes of January 2025 for new ones: constitutional lawsuits, fights over vague terms, and funding battles with states. That is not the stable country Morgan values.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> Enforcing a birth-sex test creates a new risk to girls, including girls who are not transgender.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Schools must sort players by “reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, but the bill does not say how to check.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Women's rights groups warned that it would lead schools to adopt “sex verification” practices that may include “a variety of invasive, humiliating, and unscientific practices” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Some bans like this have required exams. Idaho's law “requires physical examinations in the event of a dispute as to an athlete’s sex” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> One plaintiff challenging Idaho's law was “a cisgender woman who played on her high school soccer team” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>, meaning she is not transgender. She worried that “competitors might question her sex” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>. A federal court found that “both plaintiffs had a strong likelihood of succeeding on their as-applied equal protection claim” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>. In other words, the law likely denied each of them equal treatment.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In Utah, a school board member publicly questioned the gender of a 16-year-old girl who was not transgender. “As a result, the student was subjected to harassment, bullying, and threats of violence, necessitating police protection for her and her family.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="National Women&#x27;s Law Center and Women&#x27;s Sports Foundation, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> A federally enforced birth-sex test gives anyone grounds to challenge a girl who looks "wrong" to them. That exposes girls to exams, harassment and threats. The bill does not have to require exams for this risk to matter. A state that creates this risk is not better defending its citizens.</li></ul></td></tr>

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.28?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Morgan’s wish for a secure United States whose government protects its people gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.28.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO blames H.R.28 for harms that come from state laws, local politics or steps the bill does not require, and it leaves out that the bill shields girls from the very physical danger it cites.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> H.R.28 would make it a violation of Title IX, the law against sex discrimination in federally funded education, “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It would judge sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. That would bar transgender girls—girls recorded as male at birth—from girls’ competition at schools covered by the law.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> It would reverse a federal protection against exclusion that can push transgender girls toward physically dangerous competition.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> In April 2024, a federal appeals court ruled that applying West Virginia’s sports ban to B.P.J., a transgender girl taking medicine to block male puberty, “would violate Title IX” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a>. This protection existed before the January 14, 2025 vote.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court warned that competing on boys’ teams “would expose B.P.J. to the same risk of unfair competition—and, in some sports, physical danger—from which the defendants claim to be shielding cisgender girls.” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> H.R.28 would change Title IX from protecting girls in her position against exclusion to requiring their exclusion from girls’ competition. It would push them toward competition that can put them in physical danger. A state charged with defending its people should preserve protection against those risks.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> H.R.28 puts no one on a boys' team, and the court protection it would end was narrow and disputed.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill bars males only from programs “designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It does not assign anyone to a boys' team.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Schools may still let these students “train or practice with an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, as long as no girl loses a spot or benefit.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The danger the court described is narrow. It concerned a girl who had blocked male puberty, facing boys “larger, stronger, and faster than her because of the elevated levels of circulating testosterone she lacks” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> It arises only “in some sports” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a>. B.P.J.'s own sports, cross country and track, were “both non-contact sports” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court's order covered B.P.J. alone. The relief she sought was “one limited to her” <a href="https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231078.P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit; majority opinion by Judge Toby Heytens, “B.P.J. v. West Virginia State Board of Education, Nos. 23-1078 and 23-1130”, 2024-04-16">[10]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Other courts went the other way: “Some courts have ruled that restrictive state laws or policies can violate both Title IX and the Equal Protection Clause as applied to certain plaintiffs, but other courts have rejected such challenges.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> No federal rule guarded these students' places on girls' teams on January 14, 2025. The Education Department's (ED's) proposed sports rule “was never finalized, and ED eventually withdrew it before the Trump Administration took office” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> H.R.28 would end a narrow, contested protection, and it would send no one into danger. The PRO has not shown the bill raises anyone's risk of injury.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> It would take away a source of belonging from students already at high risk of violence and suicide.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> School sports help students belong to their communities. The Leadership Conference’s letter explains that exclusion denies transgender youth “an opportunity to be part of that community, further isolating and stigmatizing these youth” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="The Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights, letter inserted in the Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[11]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The CDC’s nationally representative 2023 high school survey found: “Approximately 26% of transgender and questioning students attempted suicide in the past year compared with 5% of cisgender male and 11% of cisgender female students.” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez et al. / Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, MMWR Supplements 73(4);50–58, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[12]</a> Here, questioning means unsure whether one is transgender; cisgender means one’s gender matches one’s sex at birth.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The same survey found: “An estimated 40% of transgender and questioning students were bullied at school” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez et al. / Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, MMWR Supplements 73(4);50–58, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The CDC identifies “being denied access to activities aligned with their gender identity” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez et al. / Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, MMWR Supplements 73(4);50–58, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[12]</a> as a possible source of harmful stress. It also reports that “school connectedness and activities to promote safe and supportive environments are associated with decreased odds of experiencing violence, poor mental health, and suicidal thoughts and behaviors among high school students” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez et al. / Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, MMWR Supplements 73(4);50–58, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[12]</a>. School connectedness means feeling that people at school care about you.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> By requiring exclusion from girls’ competition, H.R.28 would remove a way for transgender girls to belong at school. Protecting that connection helps a state prevent violence and threats to life among vulnerable young people. Requiring exclusion weakens that protection.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The CDC survey shows that these students face high risks, not that H.R.28 would raise them.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The survey is a one-time snapshot: “a cross-sectional, school-based survey” <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a6.htm" title="Nicolas A. Suarez et al. / Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, MMWR Supplements 73(4);50–58, “Disparities in School Connectedness, Unstable Housing, Experiences of Violence, Mental Health, and Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors Among Transgender and Cisgender High School Students — Youth Risk Behavior Survey, United States, 2023”, 2024-10-10">[12]</a>. It compares groups of students. It does not test any sports rule.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> It does not single out transgender girls, the students the bill affects.
