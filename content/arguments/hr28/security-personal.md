## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If effective on January 14, 2025, H.R.28 would reserve female competition in federally funded athletics for people female at birth: it forbids recipients “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill defines sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> On the vote date, NCAA eligibility still followed sport-specific rules: “Between January 19, 2022, and February 6, 2025, the NCAA called for the participation of transgender athletes to be regulated on a sport-by-sport basis.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029215055/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[2]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Shared practice remains allowed under a condition: “The bill does not prohibit male individuals from training or practicing with programs or activities for women or girls as long as such training or practice does not deprive any female of corresponding opportunities or benefits.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260603151225/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/28" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, January 14, 2025">[3]</a></li></ul></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> As of January 14, 2025, H.R.28 would bar transgender girls and women from girls' and women's competitions at federally funded schools. It would make it unlawful for funded sports programs “to permit a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>, defining sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. This would close options schools had then: many schools and athletic associations “have allowed equal participation for transgender athletes for years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029195620/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-01-14/html/CREC-2025-01-14-pt1-PgH126.htm" title="Bonamici, floor speech, Congressional Record, January 14, 2025">[9]</a>.</p></div>
