**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on January 14, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, January 14, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to care for nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to take part in activities to defend nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.28?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> H.R.28 would bar schools receiving federal aid from allowing “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It would define sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> It would also allow “males to train or practice” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a> under the safeguards quoted below. At the January 14, 2025 vote, NCAA rules allowed transgender women, who are male under the bill’s definition, to qualify under sport-specific conditions: “Between January 19, 2022, and February 6, 2025, the NCAA called for the participation of transgender athletes to be regulated on a sport-by-sport basis.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029215055/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48448/R48448.6.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48448, August 13, 2025">[2]</a> Its May 2024 rules set hormone limits: “Total serum testosterone thresholds apply to any transgender woman competing on an NCAA women’s team.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029172503/https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/inclusion/lgbtq/SSI_TransgenderSADeadlinesAndThresholdsSpring2025.pdf" title="Mickus, Mallory, “NCAA Transgender Student-Athlete Participation Policy: Sport-Specific Testosterone Thresholds and Championship Eligibility Deadlines, Spring Sports”, 2024-05 (Updated May 2024, as stated in the document)">[3]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.28?

**Bill description:** H.R.28 would bar federally funded schools from allowing students who were born male — as defined by reproductive biology and genetics — to compete in sports programs designated for women or girls. It would make such participation illegal sex discrimination under Title IX.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> At schools receiving federal aid, H.R.28 would prohibit allowing “a person whose sex is male to participate in an athletic program or activity that is designated for women or girls” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. It would define sex “based solely on a person's reproductive biology and genetics at birth.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201246/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr28/BILLS-119hr28eh.htm" title="H.R.28, bill text, January 14, 2025">[1]</a>. This would bar otherwise eligible transgender girls and women from competition in those programs. College sailing shows the change from the rules in force on January 14, 2025: its adopted policy stated “For competitions classified as Women’s events, all female-identifying and non-binary sailors are eligible to compete” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029144415/https://collegesailing.org/documents/2023-Annual-Meeting-Minutes.pdf" title="“Annual Meeting Minutes, May 22, 2023”, 2023-05-22">[10]</a>, and its fall 2024 rules retained “Women’s events: Woman or non-binary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029161805/https://collegesailing.org/documents/2024-Procedural-Rules-2024-07-20.pdf" title="“Procedural Rules for Intercollegiate Sailing Competition, Effective Fall 2024”, 2024-07-20">[11]</a>.</p></div>
