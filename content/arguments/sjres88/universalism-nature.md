**Bill:** [S.J.Res.88](https://web.archive.org/web/20260903044902/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88)), a joint resolution terminating the national emergency declared to impose global tariffs.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on October 30, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in Senate, October 30, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to care for nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to take part in activities to defend nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.J.Res.88?

**Bill description:** S.J.Res.88 would have ended the national emergency President Trump declared on April 2, 2025, to tax imports from nearly every country. Ending it would have canceled the resulting tariffs: a 10% tax on most imports, with higher rates on goods from certain countries.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If enacted on October 30, 2025, the bill would end the April 2 emergency and the extra taxes on imports based on it: “the national emergency declared on April 2, 2025, by the President in Executive Order 14257 (90 Fed. Reg. 15041) is terminated effective on the date of the enactment of this joint resolution.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210706/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/sjres88/BILLS-119sjres88es.htm" title="S.J.Res.88, bill text, October 30, 2025">[1]</a> Later tariff changes still rested on that order: “the terms of Executive Order 14257, as amended, shall continue to apply.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029212815/https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/further-modifying-the-reciprocal-tariff-rates/" title="“Further Modifying the Reciprocal Tariff Rates (Executive Order 14326)”, 2025-07-31">[2]</a> Ending the emergency means “the corresponding tariffs could not be applied.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260426004508/https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-congress-can-end-trumps-trade-war/" title="Center for American Progress, “How Congress Can End Trump’s Trade War - Center for American Progress”, April 10, 2025">[3]</a> This includes the emergency tax on the non-metal share of goods added to the metal tariff lists in August: “The non-steel and non-aluminum content will remain subject to the reciprocal and other applicable tariffs.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261009130614/https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/19/2025-15819/adoption-and-procedures-of-the-section-232-steel-and-aluminum-tariff-inclusions-process" title="“Adoption and Procedures of the Section 232 Steel and Aluminum Tariff Inclusions Process”, 2025-08-19">[4]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose S.J.Res.88?

**Bill description:** S.J.Res.88 would have ended the national emergency President Trump declared on April 2, 2025, to tax imports from nearly every country. Ending it would have canceled the resulting tariffs: a 10% tax on most imports, with higher rates on goods from certain countries.
