title: "S.J.Res.88 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [S.J.Res.88](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88), a joint resolution terminating the national emergency declared to impose global tariffs. Judged as of October 30, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.J.Res.88?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If it took effect on October 30, 2025, the resolution would end the April 2 emergency and stop charging its added taxes on new imports. The emergency “is terminated effective on the date of the enactment of this joint resolution.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/sjres88/BILLS-119sjres88es.htm" title="S.J.Res.88, bill text, October 30, 2025">[1]</a> Federal law requires that “any powers or authorities exercised by reason of said emergency shall cease to be exercised after such specified date” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title50/html/USCODE-2024-title50-chap34-subchapII-sec1622.htm" title="United States Congress, “50 U.S.C. § 1622 — National emergencies (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 1985-08-16">[2]</a>. The reasons below concern imports still covered on that date. Customs applies these taxes to goods “other than those that fall within the identified exceptions” <a href="https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/3ec7b5e" title="U.S. Customs and Border Protection, “CSMS # 65829726 — Guidance: Reciprocal Tariff Updates Effective August 7, 2025”, 2025-08-04">[3]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It cuts costs that weaken military suppliers and military buying power.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Military supply chains use imported parts. Its exclusions cover supplies “identical in nature to supplies purchased by the Contractor or any subcontractor in connection with its commercial business” <a href="https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.225-7013-duty-free-entry" title="U.S. Department of Defense; Acquisition.gov, “DFARS 252.225-7013 — Duty-Free Entry (AUG 2025)”, 2025-08 (clause version)">[5]</a> when “It is not economical or feasible to account for such supplies so as to ensure that the amount of the supplies for which duty-free entry is claimed does not exceed the amount purchased in connection with this contract.” <a href="https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.225-7013-duty-free-entry" title="U.S. Department of Defense; Acquisition.gov, “DFARS 252.225-7013 — Duty-Free Entry (AUG 2025)”, 2025-08 (clause version)">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Costs absorbed by suppliers threaten the firms and workers that make military equipment. Wacker, Ronald D. Sullivan, J.
