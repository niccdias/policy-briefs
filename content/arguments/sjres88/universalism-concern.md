title: "S.J.Res.88 × Universalism – Concern"

**Bill:** [S.J.Res.88](https://web.archive.org/web/20260903044902/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88)), a joint resolution terminating the national emergency declared to impose global tariffs.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on October 30, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in Senate, October 30, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.J.Res.88?

**Bill description:** S.J.Res.88 would have ended the national emergency President Trump declared on April 2, 2025, to tax imports from nearly every country. Ending it would have canceled the resulting tariffs: a 10% tax on most imports, with higher rates on goods from certain countries.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If enacted on October 30, 2025, S.J.Res.88 would end the April 2 emergency and the extra import taxes based on it, at the rates then in force. The emergency “is terminated effective on the date of the enactment of this joint resolution” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210706/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/sjres88/BILLS-119sjres88es.htm" title="S.J.Res.88, bill text, October 30, 2025">[1]</a>. The July order changing the rates still acts “to deal with the national emergency declared in Executive Order 14257” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029212815/https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/further-modifying-the-reciprocal-tariff-rates/" title="Executive Orders, “Further Modifying the Reciprocal Tariff Rates”, 2025-07-31">[2]</a>. Ending that emergency means “the corresponding tariffs could not be applied” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260426004508/https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-congress-can-end-trumps-trade-war/" title="Center for American Progress, “How Congress Can End Trump’s Trade War - Center for American Progress”, April 10, 2025">[3]</a>.</p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose S.J.Res.88?

**Bill description:** S.J.Res.88 would have ended the national emergency President Trump declared on April 2, 2025, to tax imports from nearly every country. Ending it would have canceled the resulting tariffs: a 10% tax on most imports, with higher rates on goods from certain countries.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If enacted on October 30, 2025, the resolution would end the April 2 tariff emergency: “the national emergency declared on April 2, 2025, by the President in Executive Order 14257 (90 Fed. Reg. 15041) is terminated effective on the date of the enactment of this joint resolution.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210706/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/sjres88/BILLS-119sjres88es.htm" title="S.J.Res.88, bill text, October 30, 2025">[1]</a> Its import taxes could no longer be used: “Doing so would mean that the corresponding tariffs could not be applied.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260426004508/https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-congress-can-end-trumps-trade-war/" title="Center for American Progress, “How Congress Can End Trump’s Trade War - Center for American Progress”, April 10, 2025">[3]</a></p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Cutting these taxes is a bargaining tool. The September 5 order makes tax cuts depend on “the scope and economic value of a trading partner’s commitments to the United States in its agreement on reciprocal trade” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261017063716/https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/modifying-the-scope-of-reciprocal-tariffs-and-establishing-procedures-for-implementing-trade-and-security-agreements/" title="Executive Orders, “Modifying the Scope of Reciprocal Tariffs and Establishing Procedures for Implementing Trade and Security Agreements (Executive Order 14346)”, 2025-09-05">[25]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Malaysia’s signed October 26 deal uses this emergency’s taxes. The United States agreed to “maintain at 19 percent the reciprocal tariffs first set forth in Executive Order 14257 of April 2, 2025, as amended” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261017063722/https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/10/joint-statement-on-united-states-malaysia-agreement-on-reciprocal-trade/" title="The White House; Governments of the United States and Malaysia, “Joint Statement on United States-Malaysia Agreement on Reciprocal Trade”, 2025-10-26">[26]</a> and identified goods “to receive a zero percent reciprocal tariff rate.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261017063722/https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/10/joint-statement-on-united-states-malaysia-agreement-on-reciprocal-trade/" title="The White House; Governments of the United States and Malaysia, “Joint Statement on United States-Malaysia Agreement on Reciprocal Trade”, 2025-10-26">[26]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Ending the emergency removes both price protection for covered U.S. goods and the option to offer cuts in these taxes in return for other governments’ actions.</li></ul></div>
