title: "S.J.Res.88 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [S.J.Res.88](https://web.archive.org/web/20260903044902/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-joint-resolution/88)), a joint resolution terminating the national emergency declared to impose global tariffs. Judged as of October 30, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.J.Res.88?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.J.Res.88?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If enacted on October 30, 2025, S.J.Res.88 would end the April 2 emergency and the extra import taxes based on it, at the rates then in force. The emergency “is terminated effective on the date of the enactment of this joint resolution” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029210706/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/sjres88/BILLS-119sjres88es.htm" title="S.J.Res.88, bill text, October 30, 2025">[1]</a>. The July order changing the rates still acts “to deal with the national emergency declared in Executive Order 14257” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029212815/https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/further-modifying-the-reciprocal-tariff-rates/" title="Executive Orders, “Further Modifying the Reciprocal Tariff Rates”, 2025-07-31">[2]</a>. Ending that emergency means “the corresponding tariffs could not be applied” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260426004508/https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-congress-can-end-trumps-trade-war/" title="Center for American Progress, “How Congress Can End Trump’s Trade War - Center for American Progress”, April 10, 2025">[3]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Removing these taxes helps poorer families afford basic needs and eases an unequal burden.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The taxes raise shoppers' costs. For firms that rebuild their supply chains, Marketplace reports: “Any price hikes resulting from the new system will likely remain in place.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20251017101400/https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/04/17/if-prices-go-up-due-to-tariffs-will-they-go-down-if-the-tariffs-go-away" title="Janet Nguyen, “If prices go up due to tariffs, will they go down if the tariffs go away?”, 2025-04-17">[7]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The missing step is evidence that this repeal lowers the prices of those necessities or prevents further increases. <tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Easing costs gives small business owners a fairer chance to keep their livelihoods and the jobs they support.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Chamber of Commerce reports: “Small businesses, manufacturers, and ranchers are struggling with higher costs” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204053/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-10-30/html/CREC-2025-10-30-pt1-PgS7842-2.htm" title="U.S. Chamber of Commerce; Neil L. <tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> Removing barriers to poor countries' exports protects workers' basic needs and their families' future chances.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The reciprocal taxes threaten demand for these workers' goods. The October clothing study warns that “the elevated tariffs are set to reduce demand, squeeze suppliers, and depress investment in future capacity” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029184636/https://www.rapidbd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RAPID-Policy-Brief_October-2025_U.S.-Reciprocal-Tariffs-Implications-for-Bangladesh-1.pdf" title="M.A. Razzaque, “U.S. Reciprocal Tariffs: Implications for Bangladesh”, 2025-10">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This reaches an important source of women's earnings.
