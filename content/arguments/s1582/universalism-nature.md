CRS states: “This bill establishes a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins (digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value).” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260512235039/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a></p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Under the rules in force before the bill, uncertainty had deterred major banks: “major banks and other traditional financial institutions largely stayed away from them, put off by regulatory uncertainty and crypto’s association with illicit transactions.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261024155055/https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[19]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 16 expressly opens the business to regulated firms: “Entities regulated by the primary Federal payment stablecoin regulators are authorized to engage in the payment stablecoin activities and investments contemplated by this Act” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[2]</a>. This gives firms a clear legal basis for taking part.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Stablecoins support crypto trading. Brookings reported in May 2025: “Stablecoins currently are used mainly for trading crypto assets” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029191856/https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[20]</a>. A study also found that “stablecoins increase the trading volume of Bitcoin.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029165258/https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/how-stable-are-stablecoins/" title="Lai T. Hoang, Dirk G. Baur, “How stable are stablecoins?”, 2021-07-08 (first online; journal issue 2024)">[21]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Bitcoin’s value affects the rewards for mining—the computer work that earns coins. The White House science office explains: “As a crypto-asset becomes more valuable, the mining rewards also become more valuable. This attracts more miners and computing resources to solve the cryptographic math problem.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Earthjustice clean-energy attorney Mandy DeRoche connects the bill’s wider acceptance of crypto to mining growth: “The more that crypto currency generally is legitimized and used, the more crypto mining there will be” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260406093812/https://insideclimatenews.org/news/16072025/genius-act-cryptocurrency-legislation-environmental-impact/" title="Derek Harrison, “Bipartisan Cryptocurrency Legislation Will Fuel Further Environmental Destruction, Environmental Advocates Say”, 2025-07-16">[23]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The bill encourages mining by expanding and legitimizing a trading channel for Bitcoin. This increases exposure to the environmental harms below.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> Running stablecoins on low-energy networks leaves their role in Bitcoin trading intact. Bitcoin “can only be created through mining” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029220046/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48963/R48963.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48963, May 29, 2026">[24]</a>; its mining rewards still attract computing power as its value grows.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Encouraging mining adds climate pollution that damages forests and other ecosystems.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Mining responds to financial rewards with real emissions. At Pennsylvania’s Scrubgrass coal plant, which supplies an on-site Bitcoin mine, researchers found that “carbon emissions respond swiftly to mining incentives” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204014/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31745/w31745.pdf" title="“Bitcoin and Carbon Dioxide Emissions: Evidence from Daily Production Decisions”, 2023-09">[25]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The risk extends to electricity bought from the grid. The White House report explains that “coal and natural gas often supply electricity generation for each additional unit of electricity demanded in the United States.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Buying existing renewable power that other users would consume can shift greenhouse gas (GHG) pollution to those users. The report explains: “When a crypto-asset mine purchases electricity from existing renewable sources, it displaces the GHG emissions in the near-term, shifting users of renewable sources to fossil fuel sources.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Climate damage includes “loss of forests, homes, and infrastructure from increasing wildfires” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The bill encourages an activity whose added power demand worsens climate risks. Protecting forests and ecosystems gives [NAME] a reason to oppose that expansion.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The climate claim skips the step from more stablecoin trading to higher Bitcoin mining rewards.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The trading study finds that “stablecoins increase the trading volume of Bitcoin.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029165258/https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/how-stable-are-stablecoins/" title="Lai T. Hoang, Dirk G. Baur, “How stable are stablecoins?”, 2021-07-08 (first online; journal issue 2024)">[21]</a> Trading volume measures how much changes hands, not how much Bitcoin’s price rises.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The mining report explains the price link: “As a crypto-asset becomes more valuable, the mining rewards also become more valuable. This attracts more miners and computing resources to solve the cryptographic math problem.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A 2018 study’s model separates trading from price gains: “Tether issuances do not impact subsequent Bitcoin returns, however, they do impact traded volumes.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20250406144041/https://smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/SSRN-id3175876.pdf" title="“The Impact of Tether Grants on Bitcoin”, 2018-05-09 (date written); posted 2018-06-14, as recorded by SSRN below">[26]</a> Returns here means price gains or losses.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A 2020 study of Tether flows to exchanges also reports: “We find no significant effect on the prices of Bitcoin and Ethereum” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261003002842/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27136/w27136.pdf" title="Richard K. Lyons and Ganesh Viswanath-Natraj, “What Keeps Stablecoins Stable?”, 2020-05">[27]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The coal-plant study tests a price change: “We exploit daily variation in Bitcoin price as a natural experiment for an 86 megawatt coal-fired power plant with on-site cryptomining.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204014/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31745/w31745.pdf" title="“Bitcoin and Carbon Dioxide Emissions: Evidence from Daily Production Decisions”, 2023-09">[25]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The trading-volume finding does not establish the price rise that links the bill to the coal plant’s added emissions. The grid and renewable-power arguments also start with added mining power demand; they do not establish that S.1582 would cause it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Encouraging mining adds air pollution that harms forests and lakes.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Scrubgrass study also found more nitrogen oxides, polluting gases from burning fuel: “We find that nitrogen oxide pollutants increase with higher Bitcoin prices” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204014/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31745/w31745.pdf" title="“Bitcoin and Carbon Dioxide Emissions: Evidence from Daily Production Decisions”, 2023-09">[25]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> These gases help form acid rain. [NAME] has a reason to oppose expanding this source of damage to forests and lakes.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The air-pollution claim also relies on a Bitcoin price rise that the PRO’s stablecoin evidence does not establish.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The coal-plant study reports: “We find that nitrogen oxide pollutants increase with higher Bitcoin prices” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204014/https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31745/w31745.pdf" title="“Bitcoin and Carbon Dioxide Emissions: Evidence from Daily Production Decisions”, 2023-09">[25]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> EPA explains the later harm: “Acid rain harms sensitive ecosystems such as lakes and forests.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261021171245/https://www.epa.gov/no2-pollution/basic-information-about-no2" title="US EPA,OAR, “Basic Information about NO2”, 2016-07-06 (publication date in search index)">[28]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> These sources connect higher Bitcoin prices to harmful pollution. They supply no link from S.1582’s stablecoin rules to those higher prices or added mining emissions.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> Encouraging mining increases pressure on scarce freshwater.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Mining consumes water through cooling and electricity production. Brookings identifies mining’s water risks “especially in drought-prone or resource-scarce areas” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261012194000/https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[30]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Expanding mining adds demand for a limited natural resource. Greater trading volume establishes neither. The warning about water scarcity does not show that S.1582 would increase that demand.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> Encouraging mining threatens fish and water quality through power-plant cooling.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Plants supplying mines with once-through cooling take water from rivers or lakes and return it heated, with cleaning chemicals. The withdrawal and discharge “can harm fish and wildlife, and can negatively impact recreation and water quality” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029143713/https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/09-2022-Crypto-Assets-and-Climate-Report.pdf" title="“Climate and Energy Implications of Crypto-Assets in the United States”, 2022-09-08">[22]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Expanding mining increases exposure to damage from taking and returning cooling water. The PRO has not connected stablecoin trading to that increase, so it has not established added harm from the bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> Encouraging mining damages land and adds waste from its power supply.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Generating mining electricity causes damage beyond air pollution.
