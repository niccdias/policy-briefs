title: "S.1582 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [S.1582](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582), the GENIUS Act. Judged as of July 17, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.1582?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on July 17, 2025, the bill would add national rules for licensing and overseeing payment stablecoin issuers: companies that create digital payment tokens redeemable for a fixed sum. “This bill establishes a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins (digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value).” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a> Existing state oversight typically imposed “more limited requirements than the bills propose.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12984/IF12984.3.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF12984, June 9, 2025">[2]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It strengthens the state’s tools for finding and cutting off funds for terrorists, cartels, and other security threats.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Stablecoins already serve these threats: “Evidence points to increased use for terrorist financing and money laundering.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Permitted issuers must check their account holders and report suspicious transactions: “identification and verification of account holders with the permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>; “monitoring and reporting of any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law or regulation” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Issuers must certify programs aimed at preventing “facilitating money laundering for cartels and organizations designated as foreign terrorist organizations” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. Failure to submit the certification lets regulators “revoke the approval of the payment stablecoin issuer under this section.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars officers and directors convicted of felonies involving “cybercrime, money laundering, financing of terrorism, or financial fraud” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. Federal licensing also weighs the “competence, experience, and integrity of the officers, directors, and principal shareholders” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It makes the ability and willingness to obey federal blocking orders a condition of issuance: “A permitted payment stablecoin issuer may issue payment stablecoins only if the issuer has the technological capability to comply, and will comply, with the terms of any lawful order.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> These orders require issuers to “seize, freeze, burn, or prevent the transfer of payment stablecoins issued by the person” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Treasury can act against foreign issuers that defy those orders, after notice, by “prohibiting digital asset service providers from facilitating secondary trading of payment stablecoins issued by the foreign payment stablecoin issuer in the United States.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The foreign-issuer registration review checks “whether the foreign payment stablecoin issuer presents illicit finance risks to the United States.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> Registered issuers must “consent to United States jurisdiction relating to the enforcement of this Act.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> That foreign-issuer route excludes countries under sweeping U.S. sanctions or designated as major money-laundering risks: the country must be “not subject to comprehensive economic sanctions by the United States or in a jurisdiction that the Secretary of the Treasury has determined to be a jurisdiction of primary money laundering concern.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Treasury must research better detection methods, and its financial-crime agency must issue guidance and rules on “Standards for payment stablecoin issuers' systems and practices to monitor transactions on blockchains, digital asset mixing services, tumblers, or other similar services” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. These services can hide who sends or receives money.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Licensing checks, reporting, blocking technology, and access to U.S. trading platforms give the state ways to trace and interrupt threat financing. Better detection rules extend those defenses as methods change. That strengthens the country’s ability to protect its citizens.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not identify the added ability to find and interrupt threat financing that its licensing and enforcement provisions are supposed to deliver.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal financial-crime rules already generally covered virtual-currency issuers that create and redeem coins: “exchangers and administrators generally qualify as money transmitters under the BSA” <a href="https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf" title="“Application of FinCEN’s Regulations to Certain Business Models Involving Convertible Virtual Currencies (FIN-2019-G001)”, May 9, 2019">[5]</a>. These rules required “verifying customer identification, filing reports, creating and retaining records, and responding to law enforcement requests” <a href="https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf" title="“Application of FinCEN’s Regulations to Certain Business Models Involving Convertible Virtual Currencies (FIN-2019-G001)”, May 9, 2019">[5]</a>. BSA means the Bank Secrecy Act.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Freezing threat-linked funds was also possible before the bill. In March 2025, Tether reported helping the Secret Service in “freezing $23 million in illicit funds linked to transactions on the Russian-sanctioned exchange, Garantex.” <a href="https://tether.io/news/tether-recognized-for-assisting-the-united-states-secret-service-in-23m-freeze-related-to-transfers-on-sanctioned-exchange-garantex/" title="“Tether Recognized for Assisting the United States Secret Service in $23m Freeze Related to Transfers on Sanctioned Exchange, Garantex”, March 7, 2025">[6]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill requires “identification and verification of account holders with the permitted payment stablecoin issuer, high-value transactions, and appropriate enhanced due diligence” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. The tracing problem extends to later transfers: “a stablecoin issuer cannot easily know or control who will ultimately be using its stablecoins after issuance.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Finding funds soon enough is a separate problem from having authority to freeze them: “current practices to block and freeze accounts may be too slow and too late to prevent illicit transactions” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[7]</a>. Section 9 requires research and later rules on detection; it calls for evaluating “Improvements in the ability of financial institutions to detect illicit activity involving digital assets.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Licensing, management checks, program certifications, and access conditions for foreign issuers govern issuers' conduct. The PRO has not shown how they improve the timely detection of threat-linked transfers after issuance. Existing reporting and freezes, plus a duty to research better methods, do not establish that added capability.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> It limits weak reserves and poor oversight that can turn mass withdrawals into a wider financial crisis.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Risky reserves expose payment money to a run—a rush to withdraw: “Private money backed by risky assets with inadequate capital would be vulnerable to destabilizing runs and lose its ability to function as a medium of exchange.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Forced sales can spread the damage: an issuer in crisis “might be forced to sell off its treasuries in panic, resulting in broader damage and loss of confidence to the financial system.” <a href="https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/the-genius-act-a-primer/" title="americanactionforum.org, analysis, July 24, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill requires full backing with cash or other assets readily sold or withdrawn: “Permitted issuers must maintain reserves backing the stablecoin on a one-to-one basis using U.S. currency or other similarly liquid assets, as specified.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It limits the use of backing assets for other purposes: “Reserves required under paragraph (1)(A) may not be pledged, rehypothecated, or reused by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer, either directly or indirectly” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. The listed exceptions cover specified reserve transactions, custody, and raising cash for redemptions.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Regulators must set “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions, and interest rate risk management standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. These address exposure to one bank and losses from changes in interest rates.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Reserve reports make the backing open to checks: issuers must “publish the monthly composition of the issuer's reserves on the website of the issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>, and reports must be “examined by a registered public accounting firm.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal examinations assess risks to “the stability of the financial system of the United States” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. For issuers under federal enforcement, regulators can order them to “take affirmative action to correct the conditions resulting from any such violation or practice.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> State regimes face federal certification against national requirements: the approval test is that the regime “meets or exceeds the standards and requirements described in subsection (a)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. In unusual and urgent circumstances, federal authorities can “take an enforcement action against a State qualified payment stablecoin issuer or an institution-affiliated party of such issuer for violations of this Act during such unusual and exigent circumstances.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Foreign issuers using the bill’s U.S. access route must face comparable regulation, including reserve rules: Treasury must find a regime “comparable to the regulatory and supervisory regime established under this Act, including, in particular, the requirements under section 4(a).” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> Registration review also considers “whether the foreign payment stablecoin presents a risk to the financial stability of the United States” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Full backing, limits on reserve risks and use, and enforceable oversight address the conditions that cause runs and forced sales. Reducing those routes to a financial crisis protects the stable U.S. economy [NAME] values.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO leaves out the cash-shortage mechanism that connects its permitted reserves to the panic selling it claims to reduce.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill allows reserves held as bank deposits and cash borrowed against Treasury securities: “funds held as demand deposits” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>; “money received under repurchase agreements, with the permitted payment stablecoin issuer acting as a seller of securities” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. A repurchase agreement is short-term borrowing against securities.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A promise to repay with these assets differs from cash available during stress. The PRO's source explains that “uninsured deposits are risky and illiquid” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a> and create “direct two-way interconnections between the risk of banks and the risk of stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Borrowing to meet withdrawals also creates a feedback problem: “While repo transactions are generally low risk, the provisions would allow stablecoin issuers to borrow, creating leverage. In periods when redemptions were increasing, this could prompt even more redemptions.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[7]</a> Leverage means using borrowed money.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill directs regulators to set capital, liquidity, and deposit-concentration rules. Their substance matters: “Without appropriate standards to reduce runs and limit interconnections, stablecoins could create the type of fire sale dynamics from prime money market mutual funds (MMFs) during the Global Financial Crisis.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Backing and reports do not show how cash stays available when deposits freeze or borrowing fuels further withdrawals. The PRO has not shown how the required standards would reduce these cash shortages and feedback sales. Certifying state rules or requiring comparable foreign rules does not fill that gap in its wider-crisis claim.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> It protects payment funds when an issuer or a firm safeguarding assets fails.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Covered custodians—firms safeguarding stablecoin reserves, coins used as collateral, or keys used to issue coins—must protect customer property from their own creditors: “take such steps as are appropriate to protect the payment stablecoins, private keys, cash, and other property of a customer from the claims of creditors of the person.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> The bill’s general rule is that the property “shall be segregated from and not be commingled with the assets of the person.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> In an issuer’s failure, coin holders have first claims on required reserves. Their claims rank “over the claims of the permitted payment stablecoin issuer and any other holder of claims against the permitted payment stablecoin issuer, with respect to required payment stablecoin reserves” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> If reserves fall short, holders’ unpaid claims also get priority on the issuer’s other assets to cover missing required reserves: “shall have first priority over any other claim” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> On the issuer’s request, if the court finds reserves available for payments to similarly placed holders, it must try to approve distributions promptly: “the court shall use best efforts to enter a final order to begin distributions under this paragraph not later than 14 days after the date of the required hearing.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> These protections serve access to payment money after failure: “Reserve assets of a stablecoin should be segregated and prioritized in bankruptcy, so that holders can still access their payment stablecoins if a stablecoin issuer were to fail.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Protecting payment assets and giving holders a route to recover them limits the disruption a failed firm causes to households, businesses, and commerce. That protects national economic stability.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO's route from creditor priority to less payment disruption skips a funding obstacle created by that same priority.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> When required reserves are missing, section 11 puts holders' claims ahead of the expenses of running the bankruptcy. Bankruptcy lawyers explain that these claims would outrank “general administrative claims relating to the estate representative preserving the bankruptcy estate” <a href="https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/the-proposed-genius-act-raises-concerns-over-insolvency-provisions" title="Morgan, Lewis &amp; Bockius LLP, “The Proposed GENIUS Act Raises Concerns Over Insolvency Provisions”, July 2, 2025">[10]</a>. Their warning concerns the repayment process itself: “The subordination of administrative claims to the shortfall claims may increase the likelihood that the estate will become administratively insolvent or that a reorganization of the debtor issuer will not be feasible.” <a href="https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/the-proposed-genius-act-raises-concerns-over-insolvency-provisions" title="Morgan, Lewis &amp; Bockius LLP, “The Proposed GENIUS Act Raises Concerns Over Insolvency Provisions”, July 2, 2025">[10]</a> This means too little money to pay for the process or keep the firm operating.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Excluding reserves from the issuer's bankruptcy estate does not release them for payments. The bill expressly freezes “the redemption of payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. Its target is a best-efforts order to begin distributions “not later than 14 days after the date of the required hearing.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> This is neither a deadline measured from failure nor a deadline for completing payouts.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The custody rule also has a cash exception. Bank deposits held as reserves “shall not be subject to any requirement relating to the separation of such cash from the property of the applicable depository institution” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. The general segregation rule therefore does not shield that cash from the bank's own failure.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO moves from stronger legal claims to better access to payment cash without resolving the obstacle to funding recovery, the cash-deposit exception, or the statutory pause. It has not shown that its priority rules deliver the less disruptive recovery it claims.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> It requires defenses against technical failures and cyberattacks on payments.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Stablecoins “could undermine the safety of payments if their operations are not robust to a range of operational and cyber threats.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Regulators must establish “appropriate operational, compliance, and information technology risk management principles-based requirements and standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Requiring issuers to manage these risks strengthens defenses against failures and attacks that interrupt payments. Reliable payments help keep U.S. commerce working under threat.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> A general duty to set issuer standards does not establish an added defense against the network failures behind the PRO's payment-security claim.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Security duties already existed. Federal rules covered “wire transferors” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know" title="Authorized user, “FTC Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know”, December 2024 (date at end of guide)">[11]</a> and required covered financial firms to “develop, implement, and maintain an information security program with administrative, technical, and physical safeguards designed to protect customer information.” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know" title="Authorized user, “FTC Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know”, December 2024 (date at end of guide)">[11]</a> New York virtual-currency licensees also had to “recover from Cyber Security Events and restore normal operations and services.” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/23-NYCRR-200.16" title="“23 NYCRR 200.16 — Cyber security program”, June 24, 2015 (adoption and effective date stated in the regulation notes)">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill directs regulators to set “appropriate operational, compliance, and information technology risk management principles-based requirements and standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. That instruction does not specify a new recovery method or how an issuer would restore a failed shared blockchain—the network recording its payments.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Analysis identifies the missing mechanism, asking whether coins could move to another blockchain after an outage: “Mechanisms to protect users in these situations exist in traditional payments, and work is ongoing to develop them for stablecoins based on permissionless blockchains.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[7]</a> Such blockchains let people join without an operator's approval.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO does not identify what protection its new standards add to existing duties or how they improve recovery of the shared network. Its claimed reduction in payment outages rests on those missing links.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> It limits the spread of stablecoin trouble into banking and ordinary commerce.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Public companies mainly outside finance, and companies they wholly or mostly own, need unanimous approval from the bill’s federal review committee before issuing stablecoins. The finding must include that issuance “will not pose a material risk to the safety and soundness of the United States banking system, the financial stability of the United States, or the Deposit Insurance Fund” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This review targets the spread of financial trouble: “This provision aims to separate damage to the financial side of a large company from spreading to its non-financial functions.” <a href="https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/the-genius-act-a-primer/" title="americanactionforum.org, analysis, July 24, 2025">[8]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill also narrows the issuer’s business: “Issuers’ activities are restricted to offering and redeeming stablecoins and ancillary activities.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a> Ancillary activities are supporting work, such as managing reserves and safeguarding assets.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> A safety gate for these commercial groups, together with limits on issuer activities, restricts routes through which payment troubles spread across the economy. That serves [NAME]’s concern for a stable country.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO gives the aim of its approval test and activity limits without showing how they reduce the spread of losses across corporate groups.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The approval test requires a finding that issuance “will not pose a material risk to the safety and soundness of the United States banking system, the financial stability of the United States, or the Deposit Insurance Fund” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. That is a forecast about risk, rather than a rule specifying how an issuer's losses must be kept from its parent or other businesses.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The activity limit governs the issuer: “A permitted payment stablecoin issuer may only” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> conduct the listed payment and supporting activities. It does not impose those business limits on the entire corporate group. The PRO's source describes the broader legal gap: “The GENIUS Act does not strictly protect the separation of banking and commerce” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO does not identify which links these provisions cut or how a covered group would better absorb losses before they reach its other businesses. A forecast at approval and a statement of purpose do not establish that fewer losses spread.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 6</span> It limits corporate barriers that can put payments under concentrated private control.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Such barriers threaten economic stability: “Serious risks to economic and monetary stability could arise if nonfinancial firms can take advantage of existing private data from their platforms or raise the costs of switching to other payment products.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars issuers from forcing customers to buy another paid product, or to avoid a competitor: an issuer “may not provide services to a customer on the condition that the customer obtain an additional paid product or service from the permitted payment stablecoin issuer, or any of its subsidiaries, or agree to not obtain an additional product or service from a competitor.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It also directs regulators to assess, and lets them set, standards to “promote compatibility and interoperability” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>, so payment systems can work with each other.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The ban protects customers’ access to competing services, while the standards power addresses barriers between payment systems. It also identifies the risk from firms that “raise the costs of switching to other payment products.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a> Neither problem requires a forced sale of another product.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars requiring an “additional paid product or service” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> and an agreement “to not obtain an additional product or service from a competitor.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> These sales conditions differ from the cost and technical ability to move money out of a network.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 12 says regulators “shall assess and, if necessary, may” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> prescribe standards for systems to work together. It supplies a power to set standards, rather than a requirement that issuers accept transfers across their networks.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO has not connected its sales ban and standards power to lower switching barriers. Permission to buy a competing service does not establish that payment balances can move there. Its conclusion about preventing money and commerce from being locked inside a platform skips that step.</li></ul></td></tr>

Both support the national stability [NAME] values.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The direct-interest ban does not establish the reduction in rewards for leaving bank deposits on which the PRO's funding claim rests.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill's ban is directed at issuers: “No permitted payment stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer shall pay the holder of any payment stablecoin any form of interest or yield” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The existing exchange arrangement uses another payment route: “the issuer passes interest on reserves to the exchange, which it uses to pay the investor yield.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13174/IF13174.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF13174, March 6, 2026">[13]</a> Yield means the return paid for holding a coin. The PRO needs a reduction in that incentive to support its claim about bank funding and monetary policy; the issuer-only wording does not establish it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 8</span> It supports U.S. capacity to develop and govern strategic payment technology at home.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A clear domestic framework supports development: “These measures lay out a missing legal framework for stablecoins” <a href="https://www.cato.org/commentary/preserve-dollars-reserve-currency-status-stablecoins" title="cato.org, analysis, May 21, 2025">[14]</a>; “These are reasonable guardrails that should encourage continued growth in this space.” <a href="https://www.cato.org/commentary/preserve-dollars-reserve-currency-status-stablecoins" title="cato.org, analysis, May 21, 2025">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Losing expertise abroad threatens that capacity: “Industry brain drain is a real risk to our leadership in technology and financial services” <a href="https://www.cato.org/commentary/preserve-dollars-reserve-currency-status-stablecoins" title="cato.org, analysis, May 21, 2025">[14]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Payment technology affects state power: “As financial systems determine geopolitical leverage, sanctions enforcement, data sovereignty, and the global role of the U.S. dollar, competitiveness in this area is essential.” <a href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/unstable-coins-stablecoin-regulation-market-structure-legislation-and-us-security-risks" title="csis.org, analysis, January 12, 2026">[15]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> A workable U.S. route for firms and expertise strengthens the country’s capacity to build and oversee technology central to its financial power. The PRO has not supplied the link between them; the strategic importance of payment technology cannot supply it.</li></ul></td></tr>

<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> It enlarges a path from runs on private money to banking stress, disrupted payments, and emergency public support.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> One dollar of backing does not always mean one safe, available dollar: “the permissible reserve assets include uninsured deposits in banks and shares of credit unions.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill uses a separate set of loss cushions for issuers: “The bill would require federal and state regulators to issue tailored capital, liquidity, and risk management rules for federal and state stablecoin issuers, but it exempts stablecoin issuers from the regulatory capital standards applied to traditional banks.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[20]</a> Capital is the issuer's own funding available to absorb losses.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Bank trouble and coin withdrawals can reinforce each other: “Stress at the bank that holds the PPSI’s uninsured deposits could trigger a stablecoin run and a stablecoin run would accelerate the deposit outflow from the already-stressed bank.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/public-comment-to-occ-on-implementation-of-the-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, May 1, 2026">[30]</a> PPSI means a permitted issuer; a run means many holders demand cash at once.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Cashing out also reaches government debt markets. Unequal staffing alone does not establish that these changes weaken crisis control.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 7</span> It allows an issuer to gain federal approval without an affirmative safety decision.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The licensing review must assess safeguards, including “The competence, experience, and integrity of the officers, directors, and principal shareholders of the applicant” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Yet a missed deadline can supply approval: “An application not acted on within 120 days would be deemed approved.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[20]</a> Section 5 applies this rule to complete applications.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Elapsed time can give a firm permission to issue payment money even without a decision that it passed those checks. Making an unfinished review enough for national payment access weakens federal control over risks to essential finance.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Automatic licensing approval does not waive operating safeguards, and the PRO does not show that it weakens the checks that applied before the bill.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Nonbank issuers previously faced supervision “primarily at the state level.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[37]</a> The bill adds a federal licensing route to that system.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The pre-vote baseline was not an express federal safety decision for every stablecoin activity. In March 2025, the OCC confirmed that certain bank stablecoin activities were allowed and announced: “The letter also rescinds the requirement for OCC-supervised institutions to receive supervisory nonobjection and demonstrate that they have adequate controls in place before they can engage in these cryptocurrency activities.” <a href="https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-16.html" title="“OCC Clarifies Bank Authority to Engage in Certain Cryptocurrency Activities”, 2025-03-07">[16]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill imposes a duty to review applications: a federal regulator must “evaluate and make a determination on each application based on the criteria established under this Act.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> The 120-day fallback does not cancel that duty or section 4's reserve and operating requirements.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Approval also brings continuing oversight. Section 6 says the federal regulator “shall examine a permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a> to assess “the financial, operational, technological, and other risks” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. For willful or reckless violations, it “may prohibit the permitted payment stablecoin issuer from issuing payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The relevant comparison is with earlier checks and enforcement, not a system in which every federal application receives an express yes. A missed licensing decision does not establish that this framework permits unsafe operations that the earlier rules would have stopped.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 8</span> It lets commercial platforms concentrate control over essential payments.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill permits commercial ownership of issuers. Its special approval test for publicly traded commercial companies leaves a gap: “privately held nonfinancial companies are not prohibited from issuing stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a> These firms still need an issuer license, but do not face that special test.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Payment networks become more useful as more people join them, favoring a few large providers: “it is likely there will be only a few stablecoin issuers in a concentrated market.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A commercial platform can use its other businesses to strengthen that control: “Serious risks to economic and monetary stability could arise if nonfinancial firms can take advantage of existing private data from their platforms or raise the costs of switching to other payment products.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The framework lets firms build payment systems that become hard to leave, concentrating dependence on their private business choices. Exposing essential payments to that control threatens the stability of U.S. money and payments.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The gap in the extra commercial-ownership test does not establish that platforms gain control over essential payments that users cannot leave.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Private domestic firms still need a license. Section 3 states: “It shall be unlawful for any person other than a permitted payment stablecoin issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 requires issuers to “establish clear and conspicuous procedures for timely redemption of outstanding payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>. It also bars an issuer from making its service conditional on a customer's agreement to avoid competitors: “or agree to not obtain an additional product or service from a competitor.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 12 gives regulators power to address barriers between systems. It authorizes them to “prescribe standards for permitted payment stablecoin issuers to promote compatibility and interoperability” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[4]</a>—rules to help different payment systems work together.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The ownership-test exception removes neither licensing nor redemption duties, and it does not authorize issuer exclusivity. The PRO must connect firms' data and network advantages to added dependence on their payment systems under these rules. A forecast of few providers does not establish that further link to control over essential U.S. payments.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 9</span> It expands a way for foreign interests to reward a president's business while he directs U.S. policy.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The president's family already has a stake in this market. Of World Liberty Financial and its USD1 coin, the New Yorker reports that the family “potentially stands to benefit greatly from an expansion in their use.” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[38]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Foreign counterparties can supply that income through the assets backing their coins: “Stablecoins can be lucrative for their issuers, who earn interest on the assets that back them.” <a href="https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news-fast-news/binance-aided-trump-crypto-firm-before-founder-cz-sought-pardon/" title="Bloomberg, republished by Moneyweb, “Binance aided Trump crypto firm before founder CZ sought pardon”, 2025-07-11">[39]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This channel existed before the vote. Reporting on the May USD1 transaction states that “the investment firm that the UAE founded, MGX, used the stablecoin to purchase a $2 billion stake in Binance.” <a href="https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news-fast-news/binance-aided-trump-crypto-firm-before-founder-cz-sought-pardon/" title="Bloomberg, republished by Moneyweb, “Binance aided Trump crypto firm before founder CZ sought pardon”, 2025-07-11">[39]</a> UAE means United Arab Emirates.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The bill's ethics clause leaves ownership conflicts open: “Common interpretations of the applicable conflicts of interest statute referenced in the GENIUS Act do not include blanket limitations on ownership of any asset or activity, including an implicit ban on operating a stablecoin.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48963/R48963.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48963, May 29, 2026">[23]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Foreign interests can raise income for the president's business by choosing and holding its coin. Expanding that business gives the person directing U.S. policy a private financial reason to please those counterparties. Protecting security decisions from that pressure is part of maintaining a strong U.S. government that serves the country.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO substitutes growth of the whole stablecoin market for a bill-caused increase in foreign payments to the president's firm.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited growth forecast concerns industry-wide links to banks: “The passage of either bills will likely accelerate the integration of private stablecoins into the retail banking environment.” <a href="https://www.aei.org/economics/stablecoin-legislation-and-the-private-stablecoin-banking-nexus/" title="aei.org, analysis, June 17, 2025">[24]</a> It does not forecast foreign purchases of World Liberty's USD1 coin.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited foreign transaction and its income preceded the vote. The July 11 report says “the investment firm that the UAE founded, MGX, used the stablecoin to purchase a $2 billion stake in Binance.” <a href="https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news-fast-news/binance-aided-trump-crypto-firm-before-founder-cz-sought-pardon/" title="Bloomberg, republished by Moneyweb, “Binance aided Trump crypto firm before founder CZ sought pardon”, 2025-07-11">[39]</a> It also reports: “The assets backing that sum generate interest income” <a href="https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news-fast-news/binance-aided-trump-crypto-firm-before-founder-cz-sought-pardon/" title="Bloomberg, republished by Moneyweb, “Binance aided Trump crypto firm before founder CZ sought pardon”, 2025-07-11">[39]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> These facts establish an existing income channel, not its enlargement by S.1582. The PRO needs a link from the bill to additional foreign business for that firm. Its market-wide forecast and pre-vote transaction do not supply that link, so the claimed added pressure on U.S. security decisions is unsubstantiated.</li></ul></td></tr>
