**Bill:** [S.1582](https://web.archive.org/web/20261013054841/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582)), the GENIUS Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on July 17, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in Senate, June 17, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.1582?

**Bill description:** S.1582 creates federal rules for "stablecoins" — digital coins an issuer must redeem for a fixed dollar value. Only permitted, regulated companies may issue them, and each coin must be fully backed one-to-one by cash or similar safe assets that issuers may not lend out.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with law in force on July 17, 2025, the bill adds a U.S. licensing system and common safeguards for payment stablecoins, digital tokens redeemable for a fixed amount of money. “This bill establishes a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins (digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value).” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260512235039/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a> The existing system lacked common federal rules: “the United States has operated without a clear Federal framework for dollar-backed payment stablecoins” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201543/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-17/html/CREC-2025-07-17-pt1-PgH3405.htm" title="Steil, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 17, 2025">[2]</a>.</p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose S.1582?

**Bill description:** S.1582 creates federal rules for "stablecoins" — digital coins an issuer must redeem for a fixed dollar value. Only permitted, regulated companies may issue them, and each coin must be fully backed one-to-one by cash or similar safe assets that issuers may not lend out.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> The bill creates national rules for issuing payment stablecoins—private digital tokens redeemable for a fixed amount of money: “This bill establishes a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins (digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value).” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260512235039/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a>. It encourages wider use by removing a barrier to entry: the administration says it “removes regulatory uncertainty that has hindered innovation and competitiveness in the digital assets sector.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261005020949/https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/SAP-S1582.pdf" title="The White House, Statement of Administration Policy, June 9, 2025">[35]</a>. Major banks had “largely stayed away from them, put off by regulatory uncertainty and crypto’s association with illicit transactions.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261024155055/https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[36]</a>.</p></div>
