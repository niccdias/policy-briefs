title: "S.1582 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [S.1582](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582), the GENIUS Act. Judged as of July 17, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.1582?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with law in force on July 17, 2025, the bill adds a U.S. licensing system and common safeguards for payment stablecoins, digital tokens redeemable for a fixed amount of money. “This bill establishes a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins (digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value).” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a> The existing system lacked common federal rules: “the United States has operated without a clear Federal framework for dollar-backed payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-17/html/CREC-2025-07-17-pt1-PgH3405.htm" title="Steil, floor speech, Congressional Record, July 17, 2025">[2]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Required backing protects money that people with little financial cushion need for essentials.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Issuers licensed under the bill must “maintain identifiable reserves backing the outstanding payment stablecoins of the permitted payment stablecoin issuer on an at least 1 to 1 basis” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. The backing must cover at least the full amount users are owed.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The permitted backing includes cash and short-term U.S. government debt: “The bill would limit permitted reserves to coins and currency, deposits held at insured banks and credit unions, short-dated Treasury bills” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Reuse is limited to listed reserve-investment, custody, and cash-out needs. Outside those purposes, reserves “may not be pledged, rehypothecated, or reused by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer, either directly or indirectly” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Safe, readily available backing supports repayment: “they need to be backed by safe and liquid assets, like cash, Treasury bills, and central bank reserves, so they can offer convertibility at par.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/essential-features-for-a-safe-and-trusted-payment-stablecoin/" title="Brookings Institution, “Essential features for a safe and trusted payment stablecoin | Brookings”, May 8, 2025">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Ordinary crypto users are less able to absorb losses: “most retail participants lack the resources, information, and financial resilience needed to navigate this volatile space.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Dollar-linked coins also serve people facing unstable currencies: “Stablecoins can also mitigate local currency and inflationary risks, major advantages in volatile markets.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Aid recipients already use them. UNHCR “successfully executed a small pilot to distribute cash assistance to a group of people displaced by the war in Ukraine in Circle Internet Financial’s USD Coin (USDC)” <a href="https://www.unhcr.org/news/press-releases/unhcr-wins-award-innovative-use-blockchain-solutions-provide-cash-forcibly" title="“UNHCR wins award for innovative use of blockchain solutions to provide cash to forcibly displaced in Ukraine”, 2023-03-23">[8]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Enforceable backing rules protect against coins issued without enough money to repay users. That protects small savings and aid payments, including money vulnerable people abroad need for food, shelter, and care.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO establishes a need for safe backing, but its aid example does not establish that the bill improves that backing.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The pilot used “Circle Internet Financial’s USD Coin (USDC)” <a href="https://www.unhcr.org/news/press-releases/unhcr-wins-award-innovative-use-blockchain-solutions-provide-cash-forcibly" title="“UNHCR wins award for innovative use of blockchain solutions to provide cash to forcibly displaced in Ukraine”, 2023-03-23">[8]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Before the vote, Circle said its reserves were “composed solely of low-risk and readily liquid assets” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a> and held in amounts that “equal or exceed the value of Circle stablecoins in circulation” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a>. It described its compliance with existing law as including that it did “not lend, borrow against, or encumber the reserves” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For issuers under its supervision, New York already required that “The stablecoin must be fully backed by a Reserve of assets” <a href="https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins" title="“Guidance on the Issuance of U.S. Dollar-Backed Stablecoins”, 2022-06-08">[10]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO credits the bill with backing already present in its concrete aid example. Its other evidence of people's need for safe money does not identify a change in backing that supplies the claimed added protection.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Cash-out rules and advance fee notices protect people who depend on ready access to their money.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Issuers must publish procedures for “timely redemption of outstanding payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Discretionary limits require a regulator's decision: “any discretionary limitations on timely redemptions can only be imposed by a State qualified payment stablecoin regulator, the Corporation, the Comptroller, or the Board” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Purchase and cash-out fees must be disclosed in plain language, and “such fees can only be changed upon not less than 7 days' prior notice to consumers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Issuers lose the freedom to impose their own discretionary cash-out limits or change fees without warning. This protects people whose essential spending leaves little room for delays or surprise charges.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO leaves out the link between issuer cash-out terms and the terms governing a household's access to spending money.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 regulates “the issuer's redemption policy” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> and requires that “such fees can only be changed upon not less than 7 days' prior notice to consumers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. This notice sits within the issuer's duty.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Circle's pre-vote prospectus explains: “Transactions between third parties may be subject to fees charged by the parties to the transaction or the secondary marketplace on which the transaction occurs.” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a> Those fees and terms “are governed by agreements between the parties to the transactions and/or the rules of the secondary marketplace” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> New York-supervised issuers already had to give “any lawful holder of the stablecoin a right to redeem units of the stablecoin from the Issuer in a timely fashion at par” <a href="https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins" title="“Guidance on the Issuance of U.S. Dollar-Backed Stablecoins”, 2022-06-08">[10]</a>, subject to the guidance's stated conditions and fees.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The issuer's notice does not govern every wallet, exchange, or cash-pickup firm's charges and delays. The PRO has not shown how its issuer-level change improves the household access on which this reason rests.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 3</span> Checked reports and truthful marketing protect people who cannot investigate a financial firm themselves.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Issuers must “publish the monthly composition of the issuer's reserves on the website of the issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Monthly reports must be “examined by a registered public accounting firm.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Senior executives must submit “a certification as to the accuracy of the monthly report” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. This makes named managers answer for the information users receive.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bans false claims of government backing or insurance: “It shall be unlawful to represent that payment stablecoins are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, guaranteed by the United States Government, or subject to Federal deposit insurance or Federal share insurance.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Such deception harms people relying on savings. Brookings describes Celsius customers misled about safety or insurance: “Many victims had invested their retirement savings or emergency funds.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Public reports, outside checks, and marketing rules make accurate safety information less dependent on a customer's expertise or wealth. That gives people [NAME] does not know fairer treatment when deciding whom to trust with their money.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not establish new protection against the misplaced trust illustrated by its Celsius example.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Circle already published “independent, third-party monthly assurance over the value and composition of these assets” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a>. New York already required covered issuers to “make the CPA’s reports available to the public” <a href="https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins" title="“Guidance on the Issuance of U.S. Dollar-Backed Stablecoins”, 2022-06-08">[10]</a>; these are independent accountants' reports.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The FDIC stated in 2022: “The Federal Deposit Insurance Act (FDI Act) prohibits any person from representing or implying that an uninsured product is FDIC–insured or from knowingly misrepresenting the extent and manner of deposit insurance.” <a href="https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2022/pr22060.html" title="“FDIC Issues Cease and Desist Letters to Five Companies For Making Crypto-Related False or Misleading Representations about Deposit Insurance”, 2022-08-19">[11]</a> It had issued “letters demanding five companies and their officers, directors, and employees cease and desist” <a href="https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2022/pr22060.html" title="“FDIC Issues Cease and Desist Letters to Five Companies For Making Crypto-Related False or Misleading Representations about Deposit Insurance”, 2022-08-19">[11]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited harm concerns “major crypto platforms such as Celsius and Voyager” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a>. A report about a coin issuer's reserves is not a report about a separate platform's ability to return its customers' money.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The insurance deception was already illegal, and issuer reports already existed for USDC. The PRO has not established how these provisions newly relieve customers of the need to judge the safety of the firms holding their savings.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> Customer ownership and repayment priority protect users when a firm fails.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 10 sets customer-ownership duties for covered firms holding backing assets, coins pledged for debts, or keys used to issue coins: “as belonging to such customer and not as the property of such person” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Its default separation rule requires customer assets to be “segregated from and not be commingled with the assets of the person.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Customers' claims to coins held by those firms rank ahead of other creditors, unless the customer expressly agrees otherwise: “the claims of the customer against such person with respect to such payment stablecoins shall have priority” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> In an issuer's failure, holders have first claim to required reserves, shared in proportion to their claims: “the claim of a person holding payment stablecoins issued by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer shall have priority, on a ratable basis with the claims of other persons holding such payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Unpaid coin claims also receive “first priority over any other claim” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> against the issuer's remaining assets, to the extent the issuer failed to hold legally required reserves.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> In a court case to reorganize an issuer, a regulator “shall raise, and shall appear and be heard on, any issue, including the protection of customers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Users gain protection against unrelated business debts, a proportionate share of reserves, and a public voice in the issuer's failure. These rules support just treatment of customers who lack the power to bargain for protection themselves.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not compare the new repayment rules with existing customer rights and the bill's permission to borrow against reserves.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> New York already required: “The Reserve assets shall be held at these depository institutions and custodians for the benefit of the holders of the stablecoin, with appropriate titling of accounts.” <a href="https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins" title="“Guidance on the Issuance of U.S. Dollar-Backed Stablecoins”, 2022-06-08">[10]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 permits using Treasury reserves as collateral for borrowing to meet redemptions: “creating liquidity to meet reasonable expectations of requests to redeem payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Brookings explains the effect of these secured loans, called repurchase agreements: in bankruptcy, “the repo lender could seize the Treasury collateral if the stablecoin issuer could not return the cash.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> In that event, “assets in segregated accounts would fall short of what is needed for 1:1 redemption.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a> A right to share those assets does not restore the seized collateral.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The claimed improvement in failure protection requires a comparison of what holders can claim under both systems, including secured lenders' rights. Priority and a regulator's appearance in court do not supply that missing comparison. The PRO has not established better recovery over existing protected reserves.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> Required safeguards for issuers' computer systems address theft and payment failures.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> These risks threaten users' money: “Stablecoins could fall victim to a cyberattack, with hackers draining funds or manipulating token issuances.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[7]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 directs federal regulators to set “appropriate operational, compliance, and information technology risk management principles-based requirements and standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Requiring issuers to manage these risks adds protection against theft and disrupted payments. That matters especially for people with few resources to replace lost money or wait for a failed payment system to recover.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO assumes that a direction to write computer-risk rules strengthens protections without comparing existing duties.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> New York's rules already required license holders to “establish and maintain an effective cyber security program” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20150604135045/http://www.dfs.ny.gov/legal/regulations/adoptions/dfsp200t.pdf" title="ASand, “23 NYCRR Part 200: Virtual Currencies (final adopted regulation)”, 2015-06-24 (effective date; confirmed by Cornell LII section 200.16 adoption note) (archived copy of the regulator&#x27;s original; the cited copy is gone)">[13]</a>, with duties to detect attacks, respond, and restore service.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal data-security duties also existed. The FTC's December 2024 guide includes “check cashers, wire transferors” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know" title="Authorized user, “FTC Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know”, December 2024 (date printed at end of full text)">[14]</a> among covered financial institutions. It requires covered firms to “develop, implement, and maintain an information security program with administrative, technical, and physical safeguards designed to protect customer information.” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know" title="Authorized user, “FTC Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know”, December 2024 (date printed at end of full text)">[14]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Circle already said that “we are required to safeguard customers’ assets using security controls that meet our regulatory obligations” <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1876042/000119312525135795/d737521d424b4.htm" title="“Circle Internet Group, Inc.: IPO prospectus, Form 424B4”, 2025-06-04 (prospectus date; filed 2025-06-05)">[9]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Theft and outages establish the importance of safeguards. They do not establish which existing control the bill strengthens or which gap it closes. The PRO leaves that comparison out of its claimed reduction in users' risks.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 6</span> Screening managers and public enforcement protect customers from misconduct.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill excludes managers convicted of specified crimes: “The bill would prohibit anyone who has been convicted of certain financial crimes of being an officer or director of an issuer.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[4]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal licensing reviews must assess “The competence, experience, and integrity of the officers, directors, and principal shareholders of the applicant” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Federal regulators can order a violating issuer to “take affirmative action to correct the conditions resulting from any such violation or practice.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> State regulators receive “supervisory, examination, and enforcement authority over all State qualified payment stablecoin issuers of such State.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Public officials must check managers and gain powers to correct misconduct. Customers receive protection that does not depend on their own money or skill in investigating and challenging a firm.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO leaves out both existing public screening and the bill's approval route without a completed decision.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 5 sets the decision deadline at “Not later than 120 days after receiving a substantially complete application under subsection (a)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. If the regulator misses the specified deadline, “the application shall be deemed approved.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing New York licensing already required “a background report prepared by an independent investigatory agency” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20150604135045/http://www.dfs.ny.gov/legal/regulations/adoptions/dfsp200t.pdf" title="ASand, “23 NYCRR Part 200: Virtual Currencies (final adopted regulation)”, 2015-06-24 (effective date; confirmed by Cornell LII section 200.16 adoption note) (archived copy of the regulator&#x27;s original; the cited copy is gone)">[13]</a> for specified applicants, officers, and owners. The regulator had to investigate fitness and could act against misconduct: “The superintendent may suspend or revoke a license” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20150604135045/http://www.dfs.ny.gov/legal/regulations/adoptions/dfsp200t.pdf" title="ASand, “23 NYCRR Part 200: Virtual Currencies (final adopted regulation)”, 2015-06-24 (effective date; confirmed by Cornell LII section 200.16 adoption note) (archived copy of the regulator&#x27;s original; the cited copy is gone)">[13]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO does not compare existing screening with a regime that allows approval after a missed deadline. Its list of checks and enforcement powers does not establish the claimed added protection from misconduct.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 7</span> Checks on wider financial risks protect people who never use stablecoins.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A major crypto failure, including a stablecoin issuer's collapse, “could affect pensions, savings, small business loans, and more.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 6 requires examinations of federally supervised issuers for risks to “the stability of the financial system of the United States” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> and checks their systems for controlling those risks.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 requires federal rules on “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions, and interest rate risk management standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Protecting pensions, savings, and credit from those risks extends [NAME]'s concern to strangers who never chose to use stablecoins.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not establish stronger protection from losses spreading through banks by listing examinations and diversification rules alone.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> New York already controlled bank-deposit concentration through “percentage-of-Reserve or absolute-dollar-value caps” <a href="https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220608_issuance_stablecoins" title="“Guidance on the Issuance of U.S. Dollar-Backed Stablecoins”, 2022-06-08">[10]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill changes banks' protection against the same exposures: it “exempts stablecoin issuers from the regulatory capital standards applied to traditional banks.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[4]</a> Capital is money available to absorb losses. The bill instead directs tailored issuer capital rules that “do not exceed requirements that are sufficient to ensure the ongoing operations of permitted payment stablecoin issuers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO's reliance on diversification leaves out that loss-absorbing role. The PRO does not make that comparison, so its list of oversight duties does not establish the claimed stronger protection for pensions, savings, and loans.</li></ul></td></tr>

The Supreme Court explained that it condemned tying when a seller has “some special ability—usually called "market power"—to force a purchaser to do something that he would not do in a competitive market.” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/466/2" title="“Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Such agreements can help or harm competition.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Legal status alone does not settle fairness, and a contractual condition alone does not establish an unjust cost or lost opportunity. The PRO needs to show what unfair pressure or exclusion the bill removes beyond existing protection against harmful conduct.</li></ul></td></tr>

Agreement to data use is not an additional paid product.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> This provision alone does not rule out making agreement a condition of service. The PRO needs a reason why obtaining that agreement gives customers fair control over their private payment histories; the word consent does not supply it.</li></ul></td></tr>

Major banks had “largely stayed away from them, put off by regulatory uncertainty and crypto’s association with illicit transactions.” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[32]</a>.</p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> Moving household money into stablecoins exposes essential savings to uninsured losses.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Replacing an insured bank balance with tokens removes the insurance on that money: stablecoins are “not federally insured” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[4]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The backing can itself lose value: “the permissible reserve assets include uninsured deposits in banks and shares of credit unions” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a>; “uninsured deposits are risky and illiquid” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill also permits borrowing against Treasury securities held as backing. If the issuer cannot repay that loan, the lender can take those securities first: “assets in segregated accounts would fall short of what is needed for 1:1 redemption.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Expanding this substitute for bank savings exposes people to losing money needed for daily life. Under that condition, the PRO's source says “a given volume of stablecoins should be able to support a higher volume of payments.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 requires rules addressing the cited backing risks: “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions, and interest rate risk management standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Uninsured tokens and a possible loss of Treasury backing do not establish the missing move of essential household savings. The PRO must connect wider payment use to that exposure under the bill's rules; its general claim of market growth does not do so.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 2</span> Bankruptcy can block access to payment money even when backing assets remain.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For a nonbank issuer in bankruptcy, withdrawals from those assets are frozen: “Section 11(c) of the proposed Act applies the automatic stay to actions against the reserves.” <a href="https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/the-proposed-genius-act-raises-concerns-over-insolvency-provisions" title="“The Proposed GENIUS Act Raises Concerns Over Insolvency Provisions”, July 2, 2025">[34]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill sets no firm deadline for completing repayment: “the court shall use best efforts to enter a final order to begin distributions under this paragraph not later than 14 days after the date of the required hearing.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Having assets available is different from having money available to pay for food or rent. The cited lawyers explain that the automatic stay “prevents creditors from enforcing their claims against assets of the bankruptcy estate” <a href="https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/the-proposed-genius-act-raises-concerns-over-insolvency-provisions" title="“The Proposed GENIUS Act Raises Concerns Over Insolvency Provisions”, July 2, 2025">[34]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill both excludes required reserves from that estate and applies the pause to them. Section 11 requires the issuer's request for release on “the petition date or as soon as practicable thereafter” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The comparison must include the new separation of reserves and faster release process, not just the pause. The lack of a guaranteed payout date does not establish an added danger of losing access under S.1582.</li></ul></td></tr>

The PRO needs to establish the claimed refund and help gap under the laws governing the accounts in question; omissions from this one statute do not establish it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 4</span> The bill removes an existing fraud watchdog from permitted stablecoin sales.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The U.S. commodity regulator has used its fraud powers against false stablecoin reserve claims. It requires monthly reserve information to be “examined by a registered public accounting firm” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> and punishes an executive's certification made “knowing that such certification is false” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 6 lets federal supervisors order an issuer to “take affirmative action to correct the conditions resulting from any such violation or practice.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> Section 7 gives state supervisors “supervisory, examination, and enforcement authority” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> over their approved issuers.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The cited misconduct remains subject to direct backing, reporting, and enforcement duties. Counting the lost commodity-law route without assessing these replacements does not show that customers lose protection against that fraud.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 5</span> Nationwide approval can take effect without a completed safety decision or another state's licensing check.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A substantially complete federal application must be decided within 120 days. If the regulator misses the deadline, “the application shall be deemed approved.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For a federally approved nonbank, that approval would “supersede and preempt any State requirement for a charter, license, or other authorization to do business” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Such an issuer can gain nationwide access through official silence, while states lose the power to require another license before it serves their residents. Protecting residents from entry without a completed safety decision gives [NAME] a reason to oppose this rule.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Approval after a missed deadline does not make the bill's safety rules or supervision optional.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The 120-day clock starts only when the application contains enough information “to render a decision on whether the applicant satisfies the factors described in subsection (c).” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> That is a requirement for decision-ready information, not a finding that the applicant is safe.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The regulator must “evaluate and make a determination on each application based on the criteria established under this Act.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> After approval, section 6 says it “shall examine a permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> in the federal system.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The existing state framework was not a uniform stronger standard. Losing a second state license does not itself establish weaker protection than the prior system; the PRO has not shown where the replacement standards leave residents less protected.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 6</span> Ordinary holders can face unequal or costly access to their tokens' cash value.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill requires disclosure of redemption fees but sets “no maximum amount that can be charged.” <a href="https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Stablecoins-Report-Final-10.6.25.pdf" title="“The GENIUS Act of 2025 Does Not Adequately Protect Consumers Who Use Stablecoins to Make Payments”, September 2025; released October 6, 2025">[35]</a>. Redemption means turning tokens back into money.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It does not establish equal redemption terms for all customers; Public Citizen identifies room for “favoring some customers over others” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-17/html/CREC-2025-07-17-pt1-PgH3405.htm" title="Public Citizen; Public Citizen., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, July 17, 2025">[39]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Access through middlemen is central to the existing business: “most stablecoin users do not transact directly with the issuer” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>. One documented barrier is Tether's rule that it “imposes a lower bound limit on redemptions of $100,000” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Widening use without equal, affordable access to redemption leaves small holders dependent on fees, middlemen, and terms they cannot meet. Fair treatment gives [NAME] a reason to oppose expanding payments on those terms.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> An existing issuer's redemption threshold does not establish that the bill expands payments on unaffordable or unequal terms.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 requires “clear and conspicuous procedures for timely redemption of outstanding payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. It also requires plain disclosure of fees, which “can only be changed upon not less than 7 days' prior notice to consumers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Tether's threshold is not the only existing arrangement. The same source describes Circle as having “much lower redemption limits and fees.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Tether's terms are neither a requirement of S.1582 nor the terms of every available route to cash. The lack of a fee ceiling does not establish that the new redemption duties price small holders out or deny them fair access.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 7</span> The interest ban prevents direct holders from bargaining for a share of their money's earnings.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars issuers from paying interest just for holding tokens: “No permitted payment stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer shall pay the holder of any payment stablecoin any form of interest or yield (whether in cash, tokens, or other consideration) solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention of such payment stablecoin.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> In direct issuer-to-user arrangements, the forgone payments are “instead retained (likely as profit) by the issuer.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13174/IF13174.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF13174, March 6, 2026">[40]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Users supply the money and bear its uninsured risk, yet the law blocks them from bargaining for interest solely for holding it. That promise differs from a share of the issuer's investment earnings.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Providing that service has costs. Total income from reserves is therefore not the same as profit taken without providing a service.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO has not explained why funding a payment service from reserve income is unjust unless customers can also bargain for interest. A restriction on that contract term does not establish exploitation or unequal opportunity.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 8</span> Workers owed wages lose priority when legally required reserves are missing.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For the shortfall in reserves the law required an issuer to maintain, holders' remaining bankruptcy claims “shall have first priority over any other claim, including over any expenses and claims that have priority under that subsection” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Those displaced claims include “priority wage and tax claims” <a href="https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/the-proposed-genius-act-raises-concerns-over-insolvency-provisions" title="“The Proposed GENIUS Act Raises Concerns Over Insolvency Provisions”, July 2, 2025">[34]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> When the remaining assets run out, paying token holders first leaves less for workers already owed wages. Protecting those workers and treating their claims justly gives [NAME] a reason to oppose this change.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> Moving wage claims below claims for missing customer backing does not, by itself, establish an unjust preference.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The shortfall claim is “a claim against the estate” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>, so it reaches the issuer's other assets. But its priority is limited to what “compliance with section 4 of the GENIUS Act would have required additional reserves to be maintained by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer for payment stablecoin holders.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This rule repays money the issuer was required to protect. Concern for vulnerable people applies to those unpaid customers as well as unpaid workers; it supplies no automatic rule that wages must come before repayment of missing customer money.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The change in legal rank does not establish that the stronger are favored over the vulnerable, or that workers are treated unjustly. The PRO needs a reason why these claims should rank below wages, not just a statement that wages rank lower.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 9</span> Drawing funds away from community banks threatens opportunities in poor and rural communities.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The lending analysis warns that deposits “could flow out of insured depository institutions and into payment stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.progressivepolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PPI_Stablecoins-Will-Lessen-Community-Lending.pdf" title="progressivepolicy.org, analysis, September 18, 2025">[28]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Issuers cannot replace those banks' loans directly: “they must invest virtually all their assets in designated short-term financial instruments held as reserves.” <a href="https://www.ineteconomics.org/perspectives/blog/time-bomb-how-uninsured-stablecoins-and-crypto-derivatives-threaten-financial-and-economic-stability" title="ineteconomics.org, analysis, October 6, 2025">[41]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The resulting lending loss would be “especially acute in rural and low-income areas” <a href="https://www.progressivepolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PPI_Stablecoins-Will-Lessen-Community-Lending.pdf" title="progressivepolicy.org, analysis, September 18, 2025">[28]</a>; “stablecoins have no community lending obligations.” <a href="https://www.progressivepolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PPI_Stablecoins-Will-Lessen-Community-Lending.pdf" title="progressivepolicy.org, analysis, September 18, 2025">[28]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Shifting funding away from community banks threatens a source of home, farm, and small-business loans in those places. Protecting people's chances to build a livelihood gives [NAME] a reason to oppose this expansion.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The community-lending forecast relies on a mistaken reading of exposed deposits and skips how reserve purchases affect bank funding.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Its rural-lending warning rests on “a one-third decline in deposit levels” <a href="https://www.progressivepolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PPI_Stablecoins-Will-Lessen-Community-Lending.pdf" title="progressivepolicy.org, analysis, September 18, 2025">[28]</a>. Yet CRS describes the underlying transactional-deposit pool as a “$6.6 trillion market” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13174/IF13174.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF13174, March 6, 2026">[40]</a> identified as potentially at risk, not a forecast that the whole pool will leave.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The original Treasury advisory presentation labels its scenarios “Hypothetical Design Components That Could Influence Market Behaviour” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/TBACCharge2Q22025.pdf" title="“Digital Money — TBAC Presentation”, April 30, 2025">[42]</a>. A pool of deposits that could switch is not a predicted outflow.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Buying Treasury backing can return the money to banks through the seller. In that case, the Council of Economic Advisers explains that total deposits are “unchanged—they have simply moved from one institution to another.” <a href="https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Effects-of-Stablecoin-Yield-Prohibition-on-Bank-Lending.pdf" title="Kamhi, Nya EOP/CEA (Intern), “Effects of Stablecoin Yield Prohibition on Bank Lending”, April 2026">[43]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Stablecoin balances do not measure deposits lost to the banking system. A claim about poor and rural borrowers also needs to establish which local banks lose usable funding. The cited forecast does not supply that link merely because issuers cannot make loans themselves.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 10</span> A run can carry private payment risks into the wider economy and public finances.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill permits bank deposits as backing. A rush to cash out tokens can therefore pull funds from banks: “a stablecoin issuer deemed at risk of breaking the buck could lead to a rapid withdrawal of deposits at banks that otherwise were sound.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>. Breaking the buck means falling below the promised dollar value.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Forced sales of backing assets can spread the stress: “These dynamics could create fire-sale patterns and turn a stablecoin run into stress on the banking system.” <a href="https://www.csis.org/analysis/unstable-coins-stablecoin-regulation-market-structure-legislation-and-us-security-risks" title="csis.org, analysis, January 12, 2026">[44]</a>. A fire sale is a hurried sale to raise cash.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A major issuer's collapse “could affect pensions, savings, small business loans, and more.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Pressure for a public rescue follows the same crisis: “Regulators would feel compelled to step in to prevent the collapse of the payment system.” <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2025/06/17/opinion/genius-act-stablecoin-crypto.html" title="nytimes.com, “The Genius Act Will Bring Economic Chaos”, June 17, 2025">[45]</a>. If taxpayers fund a rescue, “even those who never engaged with crypto could bear the cost.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Expanding these links exposes uninvolved workers, retirees, borrowers, and taxpayers to private firms' risks. [NAME] has reason to protect vulnerable people from the spillover and resist making strangers pay for it.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The run scenarios do not establish that the bill increases the particular bank-withdrawal and forced-sale risks they describe.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 requires capital and liquidity rules and standards for “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Those rules address the bank links on which the PRO's scenario depends.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Using Treasury backing to raise cash for redemptions must involve clearing through a registered clearing agency or prior regulatory approval. The second route requires that the issuer “receives the prior approval of its primary Federal payment stablecoin regulator or State payment stablecoin regulator, as applicable.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The PRO's reserve-risk source makes the danger depend on inadequate safeguards: “Without appropriate standards to reduce runs and limit interconnections, stablecoins could create the type of fire sale dynamics from prime money market mutual funds (MMFs) during the Global Financial Crisis.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO must show why growth under the required limits increases those spillovers. Describing a run and pressure for a rescue without that comparison does not establish an added threat to uninvolved people from S.1582.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 11</span> Commercial platforms gain another way to pressure people who depend on them for income.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill opens issuance to commercial firms, including U.S. firms whose shares are not publicly traded: “privately held nonfinancial companies are not prohibited from issuing stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A platform can use its existing business to “raise the costs of switching to other payment products” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[12]</a>. Public Citizen gives the example of a retailer that “might disfavor a subcontractor if it failed to use the aggregator's stablecoin.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-07-17/html/CREC-2025-07-17-pt1-PgH3405.htm" title="Public Citizen; Public Citizen., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, July 17, 2025">[39]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill's rule against forcing customers to buy another product is “significantly narrower than the statutory prohibition on tying applicable to banks” <a href="https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Stablecoin-Legislation-Senate-Passes-GENIUS-Act.pdf" title="“Stablecoin Legislation: The Senate Passes the GENIUS Act to Establish a Regulatory Framework for Stablecoins Issued or Sold in the United States”, June 23, 2025">[46]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Expanding this business model exposes small sellers and workers to payment terms they cannot readily refuse without risking income. Equal opportunity gives [NAME] a reason to resist adding this power to dominant platforms.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> A comparison with bank rules does not establish that the bill gives commercial platforms a new power to pressure sellers or workers.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> CRS says firms already are “generally allowed to blend commercial and financial” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12984/IF12984.3.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF12984, June 9, 2025">[38]</a> activities outside banking. A bank's stricter rule is not the prior rule for every commercial platform.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 adds a barrier for public commercial companies and their controlled firms: issuance requires “a unanimous vote of the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> making safety and compliance findings. These include a finding that “the public company and the affiliates of the public company will comply with the tying prohibitions under paragraph (8).” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO's comparison does not identify a protection these platforms previously faced and now lose. Its subcontractor example shows a possible abuse of platform power, but does not establish that S.1582 creates that power.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 12</span> Commercial issuance gives firms more payment data for steering customers' choices and prices.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Combining commerce with stablecoin issuance gives firms access to “detailed information about their financial assets and spending patterns.” <a href="https://www.ineteconomics.org/perspectives/blog/time-bomb-how-uninsured-stablecoins-and-crypto-derivatives-threaten-financial-and-economic-stability" title="ineteconomics.org, analysis, October 6, 2025">[41]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill's special limits on advertising, sale, and sharing of payment data by covered public companies have an exception: “unless the public company receives consent from the consumer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This data has practical uses beyond advertising. The FTC found that pricing firms “can show higher priced products based on consumers’ search and purchase activity.” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer" title="“FTC Surveillance Pricing Study Indicates Wide Range of Personal Data Used to Set Individualized Consumer Prices”, January 17, 2025">[47]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Giving sellers more financial information gives them another tool for steering people toward more expensive choices based on their needs. Protecting customers from that unequal power gives [NAME] a reason to oppose the expansion.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO has not connected the bill's consent exception to the unfair use of payment data in its pricing example.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> For covered public commercial companies, section 4 restricts using transaction data to “target, personalize, or rank advertising or other content” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> without consumer consent. Ranking more expensive products is the very kind of use this condition addresses.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The FTC source describes a general pricing study, not stablecoin data obtained through S.1582. It says its report “only includes hypothetical examples of surveillance pricing.” <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer" title="“FTC Surveillance Pricing Study Indicates Wide Range of Personal Data Used to Set Individualized Consumer Prices”, January 17, 2025">[47]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> An exception requiring permission does not establish that permission is forced or deceptive. For firms outside this special rule, the FTC study still supplies no link from the bill to added unfair data use. Technical ability to personalize offers does not itself establish the claimed unjust treatment.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 13</span> Expanding the President's stablecoin business increases the risk of privileged access to public power.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Trump family's stablecoin business stands to benefit from wider use, while the bill's stated ethics safeguard “wouldn’t apply to the President or Vice-President” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[32]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> A President-owned stablecoin “creates new possibilities for interested parties to funnel money to him and his companies.” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[32]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Enlarging a business through which interested parties can enrich the President gives wealthy interests another route to seek favor. The PRO's June source reports: “The market cap of USD1 already stands at about $2.2 billion.” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[32]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Section 4 preserves “the continued application of applicable ethics statutes and regulations” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. It does not create the President's existing gap in those rules.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The missing link is an increase in politically motivated business caused by S.1582, not proof of a completed corrupt decision. General industry growth and an existing ethics gap do not establish that increase or the claimed added risk of privileged access.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 14</span> Wider dollar-token use threatens financial stability in vulnerable foreign economies.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Making dollar stablecoins more attractive can cause “greater substitution away from local foreign currencies.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> This makes money easier to move out quickly: “users can hold dollar proxies at home that can be instantly exchanged for real dollars offshore.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[7]</a>. The risk is greater in “already volatile markets facing high inflation and sharp currency swings.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[7]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Wider substitution can also “undermine the monetary policy sovereignty of foreign countries.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>—their ability to manage their own money and economic conditions.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Promoting dollar tokens exposes people in weaker economies to faster financial outflows and weaker tools for stabilizing their economies. [NAME]'s concern for vulnerable people everywhere gives a reason to oppose imposing those risks on strangers abroad.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The foreign-currency passages do not establish added destabilizing outflows caused by this U.S. framework.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The cited mechanism says users “can hold dollar proxies at home that can be instantly exchanged for real dollars offshore.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[7]</a> The ability to exchange quickly does not establish that additional local savings leave because of S.1582.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The other source expressly leaves demand subject to local policy: “countries may ban or limit the use of USD stablecoins domestically in order to protect their seigniorage and monetary sovereignty.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO needs a link from this bill to additional currency substitution and destabilizing outflows under those countries' rules. Faster transfer technology and a conditional warning about lost monetary control do not establish that link, or that residents' protection and opportunities decline because of it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 15</span> Replacing public cash shifts income away from shared public needs.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> When stablecoins replace currency, the income from issuing money changes hands: “the seigniorage of stablecoins would accrue to the stablecoin issuer rather than the U.S. Treasury.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[33]</a>. <tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 16</span> The public helps pay to oversee firms' new payment business.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill creates new costs for the Federal Reserve to oversee stablecoins.
