---
title: "S.1582 × Universalism – Concern"
---

**Bill:** [S.1582](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582), the GENIUS Act. Judged as of July 17, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Concern. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected
- that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life
- that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor S.1582?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Universalism–Concern gives Morgan a reason to favor S.1582, because the bill protects people who hold stablecoins, including poor and vulnerable people at home and abroad, and gives every holder an equal right to be repaid.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO fails to show that Universalism–Concern gives Morgan a reason to favor S.1582: its rules leave key losses and unequal cash-outs in place, while the growth it promotes puts poor communities' resources at risk.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> The bill sets federal safety rules for payment stablecoins, “digital assets which an issuer must redeem for a fixed value” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, July 17, 2025">[1]</a>. Every coin must be fully backed: “Issuers would be required to hold at least one dollar of permitted reserves for every one dollar of stablecoins issued.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a> Holders come first if the company fails: “The bill would grant stablecoin holders priority over all other claims against the issuer in bankruptcy” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> Full backing in safe assets protects the holders who can least afford a loss.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The main danger to holders is being unable to cash out: “The major risk to holders of stablecoins is that the issuer may not be willing or able to fulfill redemption requests for their tokens at face value.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> This has happened. In 2022 the TerraUSD coin collapsed, “eliminating over $45 billion in value within a week.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Before the bill, stablecoins were mostly under state rules, “typically under more limited requirements than the bills propose.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12984/IF12984.3.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF12984, June 9, 2025">[4]</a> Company reports on reserves were patchy: “implementation has been uneven in the absence of regulatory standards.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill limits reserves to safe assets such as “coins and currency, deposits held at insured banks and credit unions, short-dated Treasury bills” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a>. Treasury bills are short-term loans to the U.S. government.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Apart from a few narrow uses, the company may not borrow against the reserves or reuse them: they “may not be pledged, rehypothecated, or reused” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Holders can check the backing. Each month the company must “publish the monthly composition of the issuer's reserves on the website of the issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a> and have the report “examined by a registered public accounting firm” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The chief executive and chief financial officers “shall submit a certification as to the accuracy of the monthly report” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. One who signs “knowing that such certification is false shall be subject to the same criminal penalties” as for false corporate financial reports <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> An outside analysis found that the bill's backing rule “significantly reduces the risk to holders” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Small investors take the worst losses when crypto fails: “most retail participants lack the resources, information, and financial resilience needed to navigate this volatile space. As a result, they disproportionately bear the brunt of losses.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> People without bank accounts are more likely to pay or send money with crypto: “Use of cryptocurrency for financial transactions was more common among the unbanked” <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-banking-and-credit.htm" title="Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, “Report on the Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2024 - May 2025 - Banking and Credit”, May 2025">[8]</a>. Low-income adults are far more likely to lack a bank account: “Twenty-two percent of adults with income below $25,000 were unbanked compared with 1 percent of adults with income of $100,000 or more.” <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-banking-and-credit.htm" title="Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, “Report on the Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2024 - May 2025 - Banking and Credit”, May 2025">[8]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Full, safe, checked backing shields the people least able to absorb a loss. This serves Morgan's concern that the weak and vulnerable be protected.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Full backing and monthly checks do not ensure that a small saver can recover a dollar for each coin during a crisis.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill permits reserves that can lose value or become hard to reach: “the permissible reserve assets include uninsured deposits in banks and shares of credit unions” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a>. A bank's insured status does not mean all the money held there is insured.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> This risk has already hit an asset-backed coin: “Circle’s USDC broke its peg when investors rapidly redeemed stablecoins due to concerns that Circle would be unable to access the $3.3 billion in reserves it held at the now-defunct Silicon Valley Bank” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>. Breaking the peg means trading below the promised $1.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill provides no insurance for the holder: “Payment stablecoins shall not be backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, guaranteed by the United States Government, subject to deposit insurance by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, or subject to share insurance by the National Credit Union Administration.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill leaves limits on money held at each bank to rules on “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. CRS concludes that “some run risk would remain” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF12984/IF12984.3.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IF12984, June 9, 2025">[4]</a>. A run means many holders trying to cash out at once.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A small saver who moves insured bank deposits into coins gives up insurance and can face a sale below $1 during a run. Reporting the reserves does not replace that lost protection for a household that needs cash urgently.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> Every holder, small or large, gets an equal right to be repaid first if the company fails.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Small holders already get worse terms than big ones. The largest stablecoin company, Tether, “charges up to a 1% fee and requires a minimum of $100,000 in tokens to redeem them for dollars.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In crypto crashes, big players tend to get out first: “large bitcoin holders tend to exit early, while retail investors are left to absorb the losses.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under the bill, every holder has a legal claim, whatever the amount: “any person holding a payment stablecoin issued by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer shall be deemed to hold a claim” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Holders get first claim on the reserves, ahead of the company's other creditors. They share in proportion to what each holds (“ratable” means in proportion): priority “on a ratable basis with the claims of other persons holding such payment stablecoins, over the claims of the permitted payment stablecoin issuer and any other holder of claims against the permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court pays out the reserves to all holders alike, “on a ratable basis to similarly situated payment stablecoin holders” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> If the reserves fall short, holders' unpaid claims still come first, “including over any expenses and claims that have priority under that subsection” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. That puts them ahead even of costs and debts that bankruptcy law normally pays first.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A person with $50 in a stablecoin gets the same share per dollar as a large trader, and is paid before the company's lenders. This treats strangers justly and equally, as Morgan wants.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Equal shares after failure do not end the unequal cash-out terms that expose small holders to losses before failure.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Most holders sell through middlemen: “most stablecoin users do not transact directly with the issuer but instead purchase and redeem stablecoins on the secondary market through arbitrageurs” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[10]</a>. These firms buy coins on trading sites and exchange them with the issuer for dollars.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The price small holders receive can fall even while direct cash-outs continue at $1. In May 2022, Tether's coin was “trading on the secondary market at values as low as 94 cents on the dollar despite continuing to honor requests redeeming tokens for dollars in full” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Section 4 requires timely redemption and disclosure of fees; it does not require free redemption or the same fees and minimum amounts for everyone. Its fee rule is to “publicly, clearly, and conspicuously disclose in plain language all fees associated with purchasing or redeeming the payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Bankruptcy also stops redemptions. Section 11 adds “the redemption of payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a> to the transactions frozen by the bankruptcy process. The court has a “best efforts” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a> target to order payouts “not later than 14 days after the date of the required hearing” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. That is no promise of cash within 14 days of filing.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Even the reserve pool can shrink. The bill allows borrowing against Treasury bills to meet cash-out requests. Brookings explains that “the repo lender could seize the Treasury collateral if the stablecoin issuer could not return the cash. In this event, assets in segregated accounts would fall short of what is needed for 1:1 redemption.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-issues-for-regulators-as-they-implement-genius-act/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 21, 2025">[9]</a> Here, a repo is a short-term loan secured by those bills.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A holder who must sell at a discount before bankruptcy has already lost money; equal shares later do not repay that loss. Someone who waits faces a freeze and possible shortfalls. The PRO confuses equal legal rank with equal access to cash.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> The bill bans the false claims and bad actors that have cost vulnerable people their savings.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Crypto fraud hits older people hard: “individuals over the age of 60 filing over 33,000 complaints and losing a collective $2.8 billion.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> False safety claims have done real harm. Some customers of the failed crypto lender Celsius were misled by claims that it was “safer than a bank” or had federal deposit insurance <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a>. “Many victims had invested their retirement savings or emergency funds.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill makes that lie illegal for stablecoins. It is “unlawful to represent that payment stablecoins are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, guaranteed by the United States Government, or subject to Federal deposit insurance” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Only coins that meet the bill's rules may use the name: “It shall be unlawful to market a product in the United States as a payment stablecoin unless the product is issued pursuant to this Act.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> TerraUSD was an “algorithmic stablecoin” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>, and such coins “are not backed by redeemable reserves” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A coin like TerraUSD could not be sold in the U.S. as a payment stablecoin.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Fees must be plain. Issuers must “disclose in plain language all fees associated with purchasing or redeeming the payment stablecoins” and give “not less than 7 days' prior notice” before changing them <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> People convicted of “a felony offense involving insider trading, embezzlement, cybercrime, money laundering, financing of terrorism, or financial fraud” may not run an issuer <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> These rules shield the people least able to spot a false promise, such as older adults, and require honest dealing with strangers. This serves Morgan's concern for the vulnerable and for just treatment of everyone.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO treats an existing ban as new protection and uses broad crypto losses to claim that narrower stablecoin rules will shield older savers.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> False claims of federal deposit insurance were already illegal. In August 2022, the FDIC stated: “The Federal Deposit Insurance Act (FDI Act) prohibits any person from representing or implying that an uninsured product is FDIC–insured or from knowingly misrepresenting the extent and manner of deposit insurance.” <a href="https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2022/pr22060.html" title="Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, “FDIC Issues Cease and Desist Letters to Five Companies For Making Crypto-Related False or Misleading Representations about Deposit Insurance”, August 19, 2022">[11]</a> The same release records action against five companies making false claims about insurance.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO's loss figures cover “crypto-related complaints” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a>. Its own source identifies scams involving “fake tech support calls, impersonation of government officials, and extortion” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a>. Those scams need no false claim about a stablecoin's backing or insurance.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Checking an issuer does not check every later payment. Brookings explains that “while an issuer or exchange can vet the customer with whom it interacts directly, it has little control over where stablecoins go when they are used to execute payment transactions by the holder” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[10]</a>. A fully backed coin can still carry a victim's money to a scammer.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill does not require the crypto ATM safeguards Senator Durbin sought: operators would have to “warn consumers about scams, provide live customer support, and develop comprehensive anti-fraud policies” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2025-05-20/html/CREC-2025-05-20-pt1-PgS2983-6.htm" title="Durbin, floor speech, Congressional Record, May 20, 2025">[12]</a>. These were proposed additions, not rules in the passed text.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Restricting a label also leaves the underlying danger. Brookings says non-payment stablecoins “include algorithmic stablecoins, and remain in the purview of state regulators” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>. Section 14 orders Treasury to “carry out a study of non-payment stablecoins, including endogenously collateralized payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>; it does not ban them. These include coins whose backing depends entirely on another token from the same creator.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO has not tied the savings losses it describes to new safeguards that would stop them. Disclosure rules, issuer checks and a protected label leave the routes by which scammers take vulnerable people's money open.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 4:</strong> Safer dollar stablecoins give poor people around the world a fairer chance to save and send money.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Many of the world's poor have no bank: “Approximately 1.4 billion adults worldwide remain unbanked” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/international-economies/commentary/stablecoins-strengthen-the-dollar-and-empower-the-developing" title="heritage.org, analysis, December 3, 2025">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Stablecoins reach them: “Stablecoins are also a good option for the unbanked—there are no minimum fees, identification requirements are less onerous, and transactions can be made with a phone.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> People in countries with unstable money use them to save in dollars. In a survey of crypto users in Brazil, Turkey, Nigeria, India, and Indonesia, “47% of respondents reported that saving money in U.S. dollars was a primary reason for using stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Aid groups use them to reach people in need. The World Food Programme and the UN refugee agency “have turned to dollar-denominated stablecoins to disburse aid to vulnerable populations” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> They cut the cost of sending money home. “Africans abroad sent home $54 billion in remittances in 2023, but traditional channels charge senders an average of nearly 8% in fees.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/international-economies/commentary/stablecoins-strengthen-the-dollar-and-empower-the-developing" title="heritage.org, analysis, December 3, 2025">[13]</a> In one Kenyan test, stablecoins “reduced fees from 28.8% to just 2%, allowing gig workers to keep more of their earnings.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/international-economies/commentary/stablecoins-strengthen-the-dollar-and-empower-the-developing" title="heritage.org, analysis, December 3, 2025">[13]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill's repayment rule is not limited to Americans. It covers the claim of any “person holding payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a> and sets no limit by country.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Use has surged since the bill passed: “In the wake of the GENIUS Act, dollar-backed, privately issued stablecoins are surging in popularity” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/international-economies/commentary/stablecoins-strengthen-the-dollar-and-empower-the-developing" title="heritage.org, analysis, December 3, 2025">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill makes a cheap way to save and send dollars without a bank safer for the world's poor, and protects them the same as Americans. This serves Morgan's wish that every person in the world have equal opportunities and be treated justly.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO's worldwide examples do not establish that the bill gives those users its repayment right or protects poorer countries from the risks of faster growth.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The cited uses predate the bill. The dollar-savings finding comes from “a 2024 survey sponsored by Visa” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>. Brookings draws its examples of aid and other worldwide uses from “a 2024 report by Chainalysis” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a>. These examples do not establish what the bill adds to those services.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The survey also shows why coverage matters: “Tether’s USDT, the largest stablecoin accounting for $118 billion of the total $170 billion stablecoins in circulation, was the most commonly used dollar-backed token” <a href="https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2024-09-12-survey-visa-brevan-howard-castle-island-ventures-stablecoins-real-world-application-316048" title="Daniel Kuhn (edited by Vishal Chawla), The Block, “Survey conducted by Visa, Brevan Howard and Castle Island Ventures says stablecoins are finding real-world application”, September 12, 2024">[14]</a>. That finding concerns surveyed crypto users, not all poor people.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Section 11's repayment right covers coins “issued by the permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. That issuer must be “a person formed in the United States” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. The right follows the issuer's status, not every dollar coin worldwide.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Foreign issuers have a separate route to U.S. sales under section 18. That route does not make a foreign issuer a permitted U.S. issuer or extend section 11 to its coins.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Growth in dollar coins also threatens resources in poorer countries. A 2024 survey of crypto users in Brazil, Turkey, Nigeria, India and Indonesia found that “47% of respondents reported that saving money in U.S. dollars was a primary reason for using stablecoins.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO’s development-policy source explains the protection this offers: “Stablecoins can also mitigate local currency and inflationary risks, major advantages in volatile markets.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a> It also says the GENIUS Act “significantly reduces the risk to holders” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The same source says stablecoins “offer much lower costs than standard transmission mechanisms (like Western Union or the banking protocol SWIFT), making them good options for remittance transfers” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>—money migrants send home. It also reports that humanitarian groups “have turned to dollar-denominated stablecoins to disburse aid to vulnerable populations” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The overseas bank-drain estimate covers stablecoins generally: “Analysts at Standard Chartered predict stablecoins could draw $1 trillion in deposits from banks in emerging markets over the next three years” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-will-international-financial-institutions-manage-stablecoin-stampede" title="cgdev.org, analysis, October 10, 2025">[6]</a>. The source does not separate this bill’s effect from demand for dollars under existing law.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The source on African public finances makes the budget effect depend on tax responses: “The overall fiscal impact will depend on the responsiveness of tax systems to changes in nominal GDP and whether other tax revenues can compensate for the loss.” <a href="https://www.cgdev.org/blog/how-stablecoins-could-further-weaken-africas-public-finances" title="Sanjeev Gupta | Center for Global Development, “How Stablecoins Could Further Weaken Africa’s Public Finances”, 2025-08-12">[29]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> For people using coins issued under the bill, its backing rules protect savings, remittances and aid. Those protections serve equal opportunity. The PRO’s conditional revenue forecast does not establish that the bill would cut schools or clinics.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 5:</strong> The bill ties stablecoins to banks, so a stablecoin crash could spread and hurt people who never owned any.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill lets banks issue their own stablecoins: it “codified permission for banks to issue their own stablecoins and perform other activities, thereby strengthening certain linkages between industries” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48963/R48963.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R48963, May 29, 2026">[30]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Issuers escape the bank rules that require a cushion of owners’ money to absorb losses: the bill “exempts stablecoin issuers from the regulatory capital standards applied to traditional banks” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A run could jump to banks: “a run on stablecoins—in which their holders try to turn them into actual cash—could conceivably generate a run on the banks” <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[18]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In past crises, people who took no risks paid dearly: “millions of Americans lost their homes, jobs, and retirement savings—even those who had never received a subprime loan or made risky investments” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan wants the vulnerable shielded from risks they did not choose. A bill that links crypto risk to ordinary banks gives Morgan a reason to oppose it.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO omits safeguards aimed directly at stopping trouble with stablecoins from spreading to ordinary banks and their customers.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bank links were expanding before this bill. In June 2025, the PRO’s source reported that “key regulators, including the Federal Reserve, FDIC, and OCC, have recently rescinded previous guidance that urged caution for banks engaging in crypto activities.” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/protecting-the-american-public-from-crypto-risks-and-harms/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, June 2, 2025">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill places bank issuance in subsidiaries: stablecoins “could be issued by banks and credit unions (through subsidiaries)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a>. Those issuers must meet the backing rules described in Response 1.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The exemption from ordinary bank capital rules comes with rules for issuers’ own cushions against losses, cash needs and risk. The same CRS sentence cited by the PRO says the bill “would require federal and state regulators to issue tailored capital, liquidity, and risk management rules for federal and state stablecoin issuers” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Section 4 requires standards for spreading backing assets across investments and banks: “reserve asset diversification, including deposit concentration at banking institutions, and interest rate risk management standards” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. This directly targets the danger of one issuer’s withdrawals overwhelming a bank.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Bank subsidiaries face federal examinations of risks to “the stability of the financial system of the United States” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>. For willful or reckless violations, their federal regulator “may prohibit the permitted payment stablecoin issuer from issuing payment stablecoins” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> These rules address risks to people who never hold a stablecoin. The PRO’s reference to the 2008 crisis does not show that adding these controls to existing bank links makes bystanders less protected.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> S.1582 grows a kind of money with no insurance and no duty to lend locally. It exposes savers, scam targets, local borrowers, people in poorer countries, and bystanders to new harm, so Morgan’s concern for the vulnerable and for equal opportunity gives a reason to oppose it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO does not establish that protecting vulnerable people or equal opportunity gives Morgan a reason to oppose S.1582, because its harm claims leave out safeguards that directly serve those concerns.</p></td></tr>
