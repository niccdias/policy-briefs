title: "S.1582 × Universalism – Tolerance"
---

**Bill:** [S.1582](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582), the GENIUS Act. Judged as of July 17, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Tolerance. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to be tolerant toward all kinds of people and groups
- to listen to and understand people who are different to them
- to accept people even when they disagree with them

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor S.1582?

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose S.1582?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Universalism–Tolerance gives Morgan a reason to oppose S.1582, because the bill requires tools to watch and freeze people's digital dollars, and such tools have been turned on lawful groups that others dislike.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO fails to connect S.1582’s issuer rules to greater harm to lawful, unpopular groups: reporting and freezing already existed, and the bill ties their use to law and requires a privacy assessment.</p></td></tr>

That is the law that makes banks check who their customers are and secretly report suspicious payments to the government: “A permitted payment stablecoin issuer shall be treated as a financial institution for purposes of the Bank Secrecy Act” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. The Cato Institute calls this “the most recent expansion of the Bank Secrecy Act” <a href="https://www.cato.org/blog/bank-secrecy-act-qa" title="cato.org, analysis, October 28, 2025">[11]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>

2025-10-17 (comment letter)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> People never learn they were reported: “It is against the law for a bank to tell you that they had to file a suspicious activity report.” <a href="https://www.cato.org/blog/bank-secrecy-act-qa" title="cato.org, analysis, October 28, 2025">[11]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Banks face penalties for missing a report but are shielded for filing one, so they “file defensively, even when there is little reason to do so” <a href="https://judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/republicans-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/How-Federal-Law-Enforcement-Commandeered-Financial-Institutions-to-Spy.pdf" title="“Financial Surveillance in the United States: How Federal Law Enforcement Commandeered Financial Institutions to Spy on Americans (Interim Staff Report)”, 2024-03-06">[14]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Being watched changes what people do: “When people know that every transaction may be recorded, analyzed, or flagged, they begin to self-censor and avoid lawful but potentially stigmatized activity such as donating to controversial causes, supporting marginalized communities, or engaging in political dissent.” <a href="https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-on-treasurys-request-for-comment-on-innovative-methods-to-detect-illicit-activity-involving-digital-assets/" title="Peter Van Valkenburgh, “Comment of Coin Center on Treasury’s Request for Comment on Innovative Methods to Detect Illicit Activity Involving Digital Assets”, 2025-10-20 (web publication); Platforms may not offer their coins “unless the foreign payment stablecoin issuer has the technological capability to comply, and will comply, with the terms of any lawful order” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> After three years, crypto trading platforms must stop selling coins from outside the system: “it shall be unlawful for a digital asset service provider to offer or sell a payment stablecoin to a person in the United States, unless the payment stablecoin is issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A freeze order does not need a judge. It may come from a court “or by an authorized Federal agency pursuant to its statutory authority” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The order can force the issuer to “seize, freeze, burn, or prevent the transfer of payment stablecoins issued by the person” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. ("Burn" means destroy.)</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That reaches anyone holding the coins, not just the issuer's customers, since stablecoins “can be transferred peer-to-peer independently of the stablecoin issuer or any central authority” <a href="https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-the-differences-between-payment-stablecoins-and-tokenized-bank-deposits/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, April 14, 2026">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Governments have used freezes against peaceful protest: “in Canada, authorities directed banks to freeze accounts of peaceful protestors without court orders” <a href="https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-on-treasurys-request-for-comment-on-innovative-methods-to-detect-illicit-activity-involving-digital-assets/" title="Peter Van Valkenburgh, “Comment of Coin Center on Treasury’s Request for Comment on Innovative Methods to Detect Illicit Activity Involving Digital Assets”, 2025-10-20 (web publication); 2025-10-17 (comment letter)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Coin Center warns that without clear limits, a stablecoin freeze switch “could easily become a tool for extrajudicial control or political abuse” <a href="https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-on-treasurys-request-for-comment-on-innovative-methods-to-detect-illicit-activity-involving-digital-assets/" title="Peter Van Valkenburgh, “Comment of Coin Center on Treasury’s Request for Comment on Innovative Methods to Detect Illicit Activity Involving Digital Assets”, 2025-10-20 (web publication); 2025-10-17 (comment letter)">[12]</a>, meaning control that bypasses the courts.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> The bill says an order must be “subject to judicial or administrative review or appeal as provided by law” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. But that review may be done by an agency instead of a court, and it comes after the money is locked. Coin Center expects “inevitable false positive freezes” <a href="https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-on-treasurys-request-for-comment-on-innovative-methods-to-detect-illicit-activity-involving-digital-assets/" title="Peter Van Valkenburgh, “Comment of Coin Center on Treasury’s Request for Comment on Innovative Methods to Detect Illicit Activity Involving Digital Assets”, 2025-10-20 (web publication); 2025-10-17 (comment letter)">[12]</a> and calls fighting them in court “a necessary but also costly, slow and wasteful process” <a href="https://coincenter.org/comment-of-coin-center-on-treasurys-request-for-comment-on-innovative-methods-to-detect-illicit-activity-involving-digital-assets/" title="Peter Van Valkenburgh, “Comment of Coin Center on Treasury’s Request for Comment on Innovative Methods to Detect Illicit Activity Involving Digital Assets”, 2025-10-20 (web publication); 2025-10-17 (comment letter)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Within three years, every stablecoin sold on U.S. platforms would come with a switch that a federal agency can order flipped. A disliked group could lose its money while it fights a slow appeal.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan accepts people even when disagreeing with them, so Morgan has a reason to oppose building a cut-off switch into the money that unpopular groups would use.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> A required freeze tool does not give officials a new legal power to cut off a group’s money because they dislike it.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The required tools target “specific or impermissible transactions that violate Federal or State laws, rules, or regulations” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. The PRO leaves out that limit when quoting the freeze requirement.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Section 2(16) requires a lawful order to be “final and valid” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. An agency must act “pursuant to its statutory authority” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. The order must specify “the payment stablecoins or accounts subject to blocking with reasonable particularity” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a> and be “subject to judicial or administrative review or appeal as provided by law” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a>. Agency review does not turn dislike into a legal ground for freezing money.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill expressly preserves Treasury’s existing freeze authority: “Nothing in this Act shall alter the existing authority of the Secretary of the Treasury to block, restrict, or limit transactions involving payment stablecoins that reference or are denominated in United States dollars that are subject to the jurisdiction of the United States.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[1]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> USDC already had blocking controls in 2022. Circle reported: “Centre, the consortium behind USDC, followed the U.S. Treasury sanctions against Tornado Cash and blocked the related addresses.” <a href="https://www.circle.com/blog/the-responsibility-of-trust" title="“Uncharted | The Responsibility of Trust”, 2022-08-09 (published); 2024-09-23 (updated)">[17]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Controls reaching coins beyond the issuer’s own platform also predate the bill. In December 2023, Tether announced it would “offer on the secondary market, the Sanctions controls it already enforces for wallets on its platform” <a href="https://tether.io/news/tether-introduces-new-policy-to-strengthen-ecosystem-security/" title="“Tether Introduces New Policy to Strengthen Ecosystem Security”, 2023-12-09">[18]</a>. The secondary market means trading after coins are first issued. Tether also reported helping freeze “approximately 326 wallets totaling approximately USDT 435 Million for the Department of Justice, US Secret Service, and FBI” <a href="https://assets.ctfassets.net/vyse88cgwfbl/6KDtp7U4IcH03zPWnpG11n/1b052835c72f2c7be0bb5ec5bd5a89fc/Tether_Lummis_Hill_Follow_up_Letter.pdf" title="“Letter to Senator Cynthia M. Lummis and Representative J. French Hill (follow-up letter)”, 2023-12-15">[19]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Canada’s example depended on separate emergency powers. Its Federal Court described temporary measures under the Emergencies Act that “authorized financial institutions to disclose information on designated persons and entities to federal officials, and to suspend their accounts” <a href="https://www.fct-cf.ca/Content/assets/pdf/base/2024-01-23-News-Bulletin-Public-Order-Emergency.pdf" title="“News Bulletin: Public Order Emergency decisions (files T-306-22, T-316-22, T-347-22, T-382-22)”, 2024-01-23">[20]</a>. That example does not establish that S.1582 grants officials power to freeze funds for lawful dissent.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The three-year sales deadline still leaves freeze orders bound by these legal limits. Requiring issuers to maintain controls does not expand the grounds for ordering a freeze. Existing controls and abuses under a different country’s emergency law do not show that this bill authorizes officials to silence disliked groups. The PRO has not established its link to Morgan’s tolerance.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> S.1582 would require stablecoin issuers to watch, secretly report, and freeze in ways that have already been turned against lawful, unpopular groups, so Morgan's tolerance gives a reason to oppose it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Existing financial controls and feared implementation choices do not establish that Morgan’s tolerance gives a reason to oppose S.1582.</p></td></tr>
