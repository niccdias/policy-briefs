**Bill:** [S.1582](https://web.archive.org/web/20261013054841/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582)), the GENIUS Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on July 17, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in Senate, June 17, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by an average American: ordinary personal health and safety risks in U.S. circumstances, without assuming a specially exposed person.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.1582?

**Bill description:** S.1582 creates federal rules for "stablecoins" — digital coins an issuer must redeem for a fixed dollar value. Only permitted, regulated companies may issue them, and each coin must be fully backed one-to-one by cash or similar safe assets that issuers may not lend out.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> S.1582 would strengthen controls on payment stablecoins, digital payment tokens “redeemable at a predetermined fixed amount (e.g., $1).” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029214851/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[1]</a> It builds on existing law: “reporting requirements for anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) apply equally to the transmission of crypto assets.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261015134137/https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[2]</a> Section 4 makes the ability and duty to obey federal blocking orders a condition of issuing tokens: “only if the issuer has the technological capability to comply, and will comply, with the terms of any lawful order.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> These orders can require issuers to “seize, freeze, burn, or prevent the transfer of payment stablecoins issued by the person” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Section 8 also conditions foreign stablecoins’ availability through U.S. service providers on the issuer’s ability to obey and actual compliance. Treasury can enforce this through a notice “prohibiting digital asset service providers from facilitating secondary trading of payment stablecoins issued by the foreign payment stablecoin issuer in the United States.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose S.1582?

**Bill description:** S.1582 creates federal rules for "stablecoins" — digital coins an issuer must redeem for a fixed dollar value. Only permitted, regulated companies may issue them, and each coin must be fully backed one-to-one by cash or similar safe assets that issuers may not lend out.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> The bill would create a national system of federal and state approval for stablecoin issuers and encourage wider use in payments. Stablecoins are digital tokens meant to exchange for a fixed amount of money. “Stablecoins could be issued by banks and credit unions (through subsidiaries) or nonbanks.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029214851/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[1]</a> The framework would remove a barrier to wider entry: major financial firms previously “largely stayed away from them, put off by regulatory uncertainty and crypto’s association with illicit transactions.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261024155055/https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/why-passing-the-stablecoin-genius-act-might-not-be-so-smart" title="The New Yorker, “Why Passing the Stablecoin GENIUS Act Might Not Be So Smart | The New Yorker”, June 23, 2025">[21]</a> These tokens would reach household payment money: they are “used as a substitute for cash or bank transaction accounts.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261021222848/https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, March 3, 2026">[22]</a></p></div>

Avoiding this added source of interruption gives [NAME] a health reason to oppose.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> Holding financial reserves does not by itself keep the network processing payments.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The network evidence does not establish that the bill would increase interruptions to ordinary Americans’ care.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The technical paper sets out risks under assumed growth.
