**Bill:** [S.1582](https://web.archive.org/web/20261013054841/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582)), the GENIUS Act. Judged as of July 17, 2025, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.1582?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.1582?

<tr class="main"><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> S.1582 would strengthen controls on payment stablecoins, digital payment tokens “redeemable at a predetermined fixed amount (e.g., $1).” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029214851/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12553/IN12553.5.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report IN12553, July 18, 2025">[1]</a> It builds on existing law: “reporting requirements for anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) apply equally to the transmission of crypto assets.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261015134137/https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-stablecoins-and-how-are-they-regulated/" title="brookings.edu, analysis, October 24, 2025">[2]</a> Section 4 makes the ability and duty to obey federal blocking orders a condition of issuing tokens: “only if the issuer has the technological capability to comply, and will comply, with the terms of any lawful order.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a> These orders can require issuers to “seize, freeze, burn, or prevent the transfer of payment stablecoins issued by the person” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a>. Section 8 also conditions foreign stablecoins’ availability through U.S. service providers on the issuer’s ability to obey and actual compliance. Treasury can enforce this through a notice “prohibiting digital asset service providers from facilitating secondary trading of payment stablecoins issued by the foreign payment stablecoin issuer in the United States.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029204723/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s1582/BILLS-119s1582es.htm" title="S.1582, bill text, June 17, 2025">[3]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
