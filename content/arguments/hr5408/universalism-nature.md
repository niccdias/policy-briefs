**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://web.archive.org/web/20260618183816/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408)), the Faster Labor Contracts Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on June 9, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, June 9, 2026. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to care for nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to take part in activities to defend nature. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.5408?

**Bill description:** H.R.5408 would require an employer to begin bargaining soon after its workers vote to form a union. If the two sides cannot agree on a first contract, the dispute may proceed to federal mediation and, if mediation fails, to an arbitration panel whose decision would be binding.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on June 9, 2026, the bill would give newly recognized or certified unions under the National Labor Relations Act deadlines and a binding way to settle stalled first-contract talks. Existing law says bargaining “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260703004621/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="“29 U.S.C. § 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code edition)”, 2024 (2024 U.S. Code edition)">[1]</a>. The bill adds “mandatory deadlines for parties negotiating an initial collective bargaining agreement (CBA) and provides for mediation and arbitration to finalize CBAs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, June 9, 2026 (no public archived copy)">[2]</a> Either party could request mediation. If it fails, the bill requires referral to a panel whose decision would be “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[3]</a>. This strengthens workers’ bargaining position against delay: Congress finds that employers use delays “to sap labor organization resolve and secure more favorable terms for the employer.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[3]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.5408?

**Bill description:** H.R.5408 would require an employer to begin bargaining soon after its workers vote to form a union. If the two sides cannot agree on a first contract, the dispute may proceed to federal mediation and, if mediation fails, to an arbitration panel whose decision would be binding.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> For first union contracts left unsettled after talks and federal mediation, the bill lets a three-person panel impose terms without a worker approval vote: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[3]</a> Under the law in force on June 9, 2026, the duty to bargain “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205012/https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/356/342" title="“NLRB v. Wooster Division of Borg-Warner Corp., 356 U.S. 342”, 1958-05-05 (decision date)">[11]</a>.</p></div>
