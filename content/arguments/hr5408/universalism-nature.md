title: "H.R.5408 × Universalism – Nature"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Nature. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to care for nature
- to take part in activities to defend nature
- to protect the natural environment from destruction or pollution

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

It does not require an environmental promise or a process for enforcing one.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Securing a first contract does not itself give workers a contract claim against pollution; the PRO has not shown that the needed promise would be included.</li></ul></td></tr>

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408?

<table class="pc">

<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Universalism–Nature gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408 because imposed first union contracts would put conservation budgets and volunteer work at risk.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO fails because the harms it cites arise under current law, while the bill rarely leads to an imposed contract and ties any award to what a nature group can afford.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> H.R.5408 would let a three-person panel impose a first union contract after bargaining and federal mediation fail. Either side may request mediation after 90 days of bargaining; referral to the panel follows 30 days after that request without a deal. Both periods can be extended by agreement. Each side selects one panel member and jointly chooses a neutral third member. Its majority decides: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a> Current law requires good-faith bargaining but “does not, however, compel either party to agree to a proposal by the other” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/basic-page/node-3024/basicguide.pdf" title="Office of the General Counsel, National Labor Relations Board; U.S. Government Printing Office, “Basic Guide to the National Labor Relations Act”, 1997 (revised edition; date printed on title page)">[12]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> Imposed pay terms would put money for conservation work at risk.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Environmental nonprofits already have staff unions using federal labor law. Defenders of Wildlife's union “filed unfair labor practice allegations with the National Labor Relations Board following layoffs at that conservation group” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> New unions bring more conservation groups into first-contract talks. After winning its January 2026 election, EDF's union announced: “EDF Together looks forward to beginning bargaining with EDF management promptly to secure a strong contract for members.” <a href="https://newsguild.org/workers-at-environmental-defense-fund-win-union-election-forming-the-largest-green-nonprofit-union-in-the-u-s/" title="Washington Baltimore News Guild | The NewsGuild - TNG-CWA, “Workers at Environmental Defense Fund win union election, forming the largest green nonprofit union in the U.S.”, 2026-01-23">[14]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> These talks concern spending on staff: “Across environmental organizations, common sticking points include pay and benefits” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>. Environment America's president described the stakes as “the thoughtful use of our members’ and contributors’ money in advancing our mission to protect our air, water and open spaces” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Staff costs can outgrow income. Sierra Club reported that “while revenue has been relatively flat from 2017 until 2024, the costs of salaries and benefits have nearly doubled during that time” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/more-layoffs-at-sierra-club/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “More layoffs at Sierra Club”, 2024-05-20">[15]</a>. Its budget called for “$21.5 million from workforce reductions” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/more-layoffs-at-sierra-club/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “More layoffs at Sierra Club”, 2024-05-20">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Cutting conservation staff threatens the mission. An award can fit the total budget while leaving less for other staff or projects.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A panel could require pay spending a nature group has not agreed to, forcing cuts elsewhere in its conservation work. An earlier U.S. bill, the Employee Free Choice Act, would have allowed arbitration “after 120 days of inconclusive bargaining, including 30 days with help from the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/ib275/" title="Patrick Eagan-Van Meter and Ross Eisenbrey | Economic Policy Institute, “Business Success and First Contract Arbitration (Issue Brief #275)”, 2010-03-30">[17]</a>. That is the same 90-plus-30-day timeline as H.R.5408. In Manitoba, whose law is the “most similar” to that bill, “in eight years only 16 contract negotiations went to arbitration” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/ib275/" title="Patrick Eagan-Van Meter and Ross Eisenbrey | Economic Policy Institute, “Business Success and First Contract Arbitration (Issue Brief #275)”, 2010-03-30">[17]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The threat of a panel pushes both sides to settle. A study of Canadian provinces found that “first contracts are rarely imposed by third parties” and that these laws create “an incentive for the parties to bargain in good faith and negotiate an agreement” <a href="https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/ilrrev/v63y2010i4p585-605.html" title="Susan J. T. Johnson | ILR Review (Cornell University, ILR School), vol. 63(4), pages 585-605; record hosted by IDEAS/RePEc, “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (abstract and citation record)”, 2010 (ILR Review, July 2010 issue; RePEc record dated 2010-02-02)">[18]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Imposed contracts did not sink employers: “Of the 16 Manitoba businesses that had a first collective bargaining agreement imposed by the labor board, 14 or 87.5% were still in operation in 2009” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/ib275/" title="Patrick Eagan-Van Meter and Ross Eisenbrey | Economic Policy Institute, “Business Success and First Contract Arbitration (Issue Brief #275)”, 2010-03-30">[17]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The nature group helps pick the panel: “one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>. The panel's first test is “the employer's financial status and prospects” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Sierra Club's staff costs grew from hiring, not from an imposed contract. It said: “Funding flowed and staffing grew rapidly when we were fighting Donald Trump’s attacks on the environment” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/more-layoffs-at-sierra-club/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “More layoffs at Sierra Club”, 2024-05-20">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Sierra Club staff already had a contract. They were set “to renegotiate their union contract” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>. The bill covers only “an initial collective bargaining agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Staff pay buys conservation work. EDF's union members are “scientists, attorneys, economists, communicators, and advocates” <a href="https://newsguild.org/workers-at-environmental-defense-fund-win-union-election-forming-the-largest-green-nonprofit-union-in-the-u-s/" title="Washington Baltimore News Guild | The NewsGuild - TNG-CWA, “Workers at Environmental Defense Fund win union election, forming the largest green nonprofit union in the U.S.”, 2026-01-23">[14]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Low pay drives these workers out. In a union poll at Environment America's parent group, “a large majority said they’d had to rely on help from family to cover expenses” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>. A former organizer there warned: “We can’t afford to be burning people out and having them give up after a year or two” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a>. The bill's test of whether workers can “sustain themselves” on their pay targets this problem <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO's harm needs a rare award that a panel, with the group's own member and a duty to weigh its finances, sets beyond what the group can bear. The PRO shows no such case. Pay that keeps skilled staff on the job supports conservation. Point 1 gives Morgan no reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> Imposed work rules could limit the conservation tasks volunteers may take on.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Volunteers do hands-on work to protect nature. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance's 2025 report states: “Volunteers joined our teams in the Biodiversity Reserve to monitor wildlife and remove invasive species like stinknet, helping preserve native habitat.” <a href="https://sandiegozoowildlifealliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2025%20Annual%20Report.pdf" title="San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, “2025 Annual Report: Transforming Conservation Together”, 2026-04 (publisher&#x27;s upload directory; report covers 2025 and carries ©2026)">[19]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A U.S. nonprofit conservation employer already has union contract limits on volunteer work. The Zoological Society of San Diego's 2025–2029 contract states: “The Employer agrees that volunteers shall not be used to displace or reduce the hours of regularly scheduled qualified employees but may do work supplemental thereto after written notification to the Union.” <a href="https://www.teamsters481.org/documents/contracts/zoological-society-of-san-diego-2025-2029.pdf" title="Teamsters Local 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, “Agreement Between Teamsters Local Union No. 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, February 1, 2025–January 31, 2029”, 2025 (agreement effective February 1, 2025; signature page dated July 2025)">[20]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Under H.R.5408, a panel could impose similar limits in a first contract at a newly unionized conservation group. The group could no longer reject those terms to preserve volunteer roles. That puts Morgan's opportunities to take part in nature protection at risk.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO's volunteer clause protects volunteer work, and the bill says nothing about volunteers.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The clause keeps the employer's right to use volunteers: “The Employer reserves the right to use volunteers to facilitate or enhance achievement of stated institutional goals.” <a href="https://www.teamsters481.org/documents/contracts/zoological-society-of-san-diego-2025-2029.pdf" title="Teamsters Local 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, “Agreement Between Teamsters Local Union No. 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, February 1, 2025–January 31, 2029”, 2025 (agreement effective February 1, 2025; signature page dated July 2025)">[20]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> It bars only using volunteers to replace paid staff. Extra volunteer work needs only notice, not union consent: volunteers “may do work supplemental thereto after written notification to the Union” <a href="https://www.teamsters481.org/documents/contracts/zoological-society-of-san-diego-2025-2029.pdf" title="Teamsters Local 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, “Agreement Between Teamsters Local Union No. 481 and Zoological Society of San Diego, February 1, 2025–January 31, 2029”, 2025 (agreement effective February 1, 2025; signature page dated July 2025)">[20]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under that contract, the zoo runs a large volunteer program: “1,889 Invaluable Volunteers work alongside our teams.” <a href="https://sandiegozoowildlifealliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2025%20Annual%20Report.pdf" title="San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, “2025 Annual Report: Transforming Conservation Together”, 2026-04 (publisher&#x27;s upload directory; report covers 2025 and carries ©2026)">[19]</a> They gave “264,000+ Hours of Service” <a href="https://sandiegozoowildlifealliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2025%20Annual%20Report.pdf" title="San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, “2025 Annual Report: Transforming Conservation Together”, 2026-04 (publisher&#x27;s upload directory; report covers 2025 and carries ©2026)">[19]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That includes hands-on habitat work: “Volunteers joined our teams in the Biodiversity Reserve to monitor wildlife and remove invasive species like stinknet, helping preserve native habitat.” <a href="https://sandiegozoowildlifealliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/2025%20Annual%20Report.pdf" title="San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, “2025 Annual Report: Transforming Conservation Together”, 2026-04 (publisher&#x27;s upload directory; report covers 2025 and carries ©2026)">[19]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill's tests for an award are about money and size, such as “the size and type of the employer's operations and business” and “the employees' cost of living” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>. None of the five tests mention volunteers.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Even if a panel copied this clause, volunteers like Morgan could keep doing extra habitat work, as they do at the zoo. Point 2 shows no loss of ways to defend nature.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The bill would shorten the first-contract fights that now pull conservation staff off their work.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> These fights run longer at nonprofits: “The average time between forming a union and a contract being signed is 458 days, and it is 548 days for nonprofits.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Hoyle, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[21]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Green groups fit this pattern: “Audubon’s union and its management have been negotiating over a collective bargaining agreement for nearly two years.” <a href="https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-state-of-the-green-unions/" title="Robin Bravender | E&amp;E News by POLITICO, “The state of the green unions”, 2023-09-05">[13]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Long fights lead to strikes.
