title: "H.R.5408 × Security – Personal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Security – Personal. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to avoid disease and protect their health
- to be personally safe and secure
- to avoid anything dangerous

*Read this value as held by an average American: ordinary personal health and safety risks in U.S. circumstances, without assuming a specially exposed person.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill lets outsiders impose safety rules that workers cannot vote down, even if the rules miss dangers the workers know about.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under current law, no side can be forced to accept terms. It can “dictate wages, benefits, workplace policies, safety rules and other core terms of employment for up to two years” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers lose their power to refuse safety rules they think are unsafe.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The neutral member casts the deciding vote: “the arbitrator has the most power” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[20]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Panel members may be strangers to the workplace: “government bureaucrats can parachute into workplaces they have never set foot in” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[22]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill does not tell the panel to weigh safety. The decision “shall be based on” five factors <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>: the employer's finances, its size and type, workers' cost of living, whether workers can live on their pay, and what other employers pay. None mentions safety or hazards.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Workers know the dangers best. OSHA, the federal job-safety agency, points to “workers, who often have the best understanding of the conditions that create hazards and insights into how they can be controlled” <a href="https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3885.pdf" title="Occupational Safety and Health Administration, U.S. Department of Labor, “Recommended Practices for Safety and Health Programs”, October 2016">[23]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Business groups warn that “arbitrators could be empowered to impose work and safety rules that are not adequately tailored to a facility's specific operations” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The panel's safety rules can miss dangers that workers would have caught.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> For two years, changing a bad rule takes the employer's written consent: the contract binds “unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers could spend two years under safety rules written by outsiders, with no duty to weigh safety, that they could not vote down or quickly fix.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> That raises the risk of injury and illness at work, which gives Morgan, who wants to avoid danger, a reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The safety argument overlooks both the employer’s power under current law and workers’ continuing role in finding and reporting hazards.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Today, after good-faith talks truly reach a deadlock, “the employer may declare impasse, and then implement the last offer presented to the union” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/employer-union-rights-and-obligations" title="National Labor Relations Board, “Employer/Union Rights and Obligations”, Page undated; text confirmed in Internet Archive snapshot of January 7, 2025">[24]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The subjects covered by those talks include “insurance, safety practices and other mandatory subjects” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/employer-union-rights-and-obligations" title="National Labor Relations Board, “Employer/Union Rights and Obligations”, Page undated; text confirmed in Internet Archive snapshot of January 7, 2025">[24]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill adds a duty “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Rejecting a contract today does not stop the employer from imposing its last offer after a true deadlock. The bill blocks that route to weaker safety terms or health benefits while an agreement is pending.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Each side can choose a panel member. The bill provides for “one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel must consider “the size and type of the employer's operations and business” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers have a route to bring their knowledge of hazards into the decision. The lack of a separate safety factor does not show that the panel will ignore workplace dangers.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill amends “Section 8 of the National Labor Relations Act (29 U.S.C. 158)” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. Employers remain bound by the separate job-safety law: they “shall comply with occupational safety and health standards promulgated under this Act.” <a href="https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/oshact/section5-duties" title="United States Congress; reproduced by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, U.S. Department of Labor, “OSH Act of 1970, Section 5: Duties (29 U.S.C. 654)”, December 29, 1970 (enactment)">[25]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Joint guidance from OSHA, the federal job-safety agency, and the National Labor Relations Board states: “Workers must be able to report safety and health concerns, at any time, and be protected from retaliation.” <a href="https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/NLRB_OSHA_Worker_Voice_Fact_Sheet.pdf" title="Occupational Safety and Health Administration and National Labor Relations Board, “Building Safe &amp; Healthy Workplaces that Promote Worker Voice”, October 31, 2023 (release announced by both agencies)">[26]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> A two-year contract does not make the employer’s consent a condition for reporting hazards or enforcing safety law. The PRO has not shown that panel-written rules would raise the risk of injury or disease.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> An imposed contract the employer cannot afford can cost workers their jobs, and losing a job harms health.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Rep. Tim Walberg, who chairs the House Education and Workforce Committee, warned that imposed contracts “could mandate spending that small employers cannot sustain, threaten jobs, kill growth, and in some cases, shut businesses down entirely.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[22]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> If that happens, no one can undo the contract: “If the arbitrators finalize a contract that results in workforce reductions, facility closures, or even bankruptcy, there is no recourse for either party.” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Most working Americans get health insurance through their jobs: “In 2024, an estimated 181 million Americans had an employment-based plan, including 85.6 percent of full-time workers” <a href="https://doi.org/10.3386/w34874" title="Jessamyn Schaller and Mariana Zerpa; National Bureau of Economic Research, Working Paper 34874, “Job Loss and Health Insurance Coverage Before and After the Affordable Care Act”, February 2026">[27]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under today's health law, losing a job still cuts the share of workers with any health insurance by up to about 10 percentage points: “In the post-ACA period, the peak decline is roughly 10 percentage points” <a href="https://doi.org/10.3386/w34874" title="Jessamyn Schaller and Mariana Zerpa; National Bureau of Economic Research, Working Paper 34874, “Job Loss and Health Insurance Coverage Before and After the Affordable Care Act”, February 2026">[27]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Losing a long-held job raises the risk of death: “for high-seniority male workers, mortality rates in the year after displacement are 50%–100% higher than would otherwise have been expected.” <a href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/124/3/1265/1905153" title="Daniel Sullivan (Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago) and Till von Wachter (Columbia University); The Quarterly Journal of Economics, Volume 124, Issue 3, Pages 1265–1306, Oxford University Press, “Job Displacement and Mortality: An Analysis Using Administrative Data”, August 1, 2009">[28]</a> (“Displacement” means losing a job in a mass layoff.)</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A contract the employer cannot afford, which no one can appeal, puts workers' jobs, health coverage and lives at risk.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> That gives Morgan, who wants to protect their health, a reason to oppose the bill.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO does not establish that the bill would cause the job losses needed for its health argument.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel’s decision must be based on “the employer's financial status and prospects” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill directly contradicts the PRO’s source, which says arbitrators “are not required to take into consideration if the contract they impose is financially viable for the business” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The contract can change during the two years: it binds “unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The claim of “no recourse” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a> leaves out an express route to revise costly terms by agreement.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A survey of Manitoba firms given imposed first contracts found: “Of the 16 Manitoba businesses that had a first collective bargaining agreement imposed by the labor board, 14 or 87.5% were still in operation in 2009” <a href="https://files.epi.org/page/-/ib275/ib275.pdf" title="Ross Eisenbrey and Patrick Eagan-Van Meter; Economic Policy Institute, “Business Success and First Contract Arbitration (EPI Issue Brief #275)”, March 30, 2010">[29]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO’s studies ask how job loss affects insurance and the risk of death: “causal effects of displacement on insurance coverage” <a href="https://doi.org/10.3386/w34874" title="Jessamyn Schaller and Mariana Zerpa; National Bureau of Economic Research, Working Paper 34874, “Job Loss and Health Insurance Coverage Before and After the Affordable Care Act”, February 2026">[27]</a> and “effects of job displacement on mortality” <a href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/124/3/1265/1905153" title="Daniel Displacement and Mortality: An Analysis Using Administrative Data”, August 1, 2009">[28]</a>. Neither tests whether first-contract arbitration causes job loss.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO does not show why the bill’s required financial checks would fail and produce extra job losses. The health studies cannot supply that missing link.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> Arbitration also offers a way to settle first contracts without putting workers’ jobs and health coverage at risk through strikes.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> If mediation fails, the bill sends the dispute to “a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A study of Canadian laws found that having first-contract arbitration “reduces first agreement work stoppage incidence by at least 50 percent” <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/001979391006300402" title="Susan Johnson (Wilfrid Laurier University); Industrial and Labor Relations Review, Vol. 63, No. 4, SAGE Publications, “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages”, July 1, 2010">[30]</a>. Work stoppages are strikes or lockouts.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> U.S. workers striking for better pay or working conditions can face permanent replacements. The National Labor Relations Board explains: “If the employer has hired bona fide permanent replacements who are filling the jobs of the economic strikers when the strikers apply unconditionally to go back to work, the strikers are not entitled to reinstatement at that time.” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/basic-page/node-3024/basicguide.pdf" title="Office of the General Counsel, National Labor Relations Board (U.S. Government Printing Office), “Basic Guide to the National Labor Relations Act: General Principles of Law Under the Statute and Procedures of the National Labor Relations Board”, 1997">[31]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Settling talks without a strike avoids that route to losing access to a job and its health coverage. The Canadian findings support this protective path, which the PRO leaves out.</li></ul></td></tr>
