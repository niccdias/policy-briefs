**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://web.archive.org/web/20260618183816/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408)), the Faster Labor Contracts Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on June 9, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, June 9, 2026. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by an average American: ordinary personal health and safety risks in U.S. circumstances, without assuming a specially exposed person.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.5408?

**Bill description:** H.R.5408 would require an employer to begin bargaining soon after its workers vote to form a union. If the two sides cannot agree on a first contract, the dispute may proceed to federal mediation and, if mediation fails, to an arbitration panel whose decision would be binding.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> The bill creates a path to a first labor contract that both sides must follow: “This bill establishes mandatory deadlines for parties negotiating an initial collective bargaining agreement (CBA) and provides for mediation and arbitration to finalize CBAs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, June 9, 2026 (no public archived copy)">[1]</a> If talks and mediation fail, a three-person panel settles the dispute: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a> This changes the law in force on June 9, 2026, which “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal” <a href="https://www.littler.com/files/littler_on_collective_bargaining.pdf" title="Blomberg, Angela, “Littler on Collective Bargaining”, 2024 edition (the original page is gone, and no archived copy was found)">[3]</a>. The bill also requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.5408?

**Bill description:** H.R.5408 would require an employer to begin bargaining soon after its workers vote to form a union. If the two sides cannot agree on a first contract, the dispute may proceed to federal mediation and, if mediation fails, to an arbitration panel whose decision would be binding.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> For first union contracts still unsettled after bargaining and mediation, the bill makes a panel’s decision binding for two years: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a></p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Current law requires good-faith bargaining but says it “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260703004621/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="“29 U.S.C. 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 1974-07-26">[23]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Members normally approve contracts under union rules. As Heritage explains, “nearly all union constitutions and bylaws require union members’ ratification of collective bargaining agreements before they are signed and enforced.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260307172802/https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[24]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> Arbitration bypasses that approval check. Workers cannot reject the binding first contract through their usual vote; changing it during those two years requires both parties’ written consent.</li><li class="note"><span class="lbl">Note</span> The union’s panel seat does not preserve members’ final say. The bill provides “one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a> A majority can outvote the union’s choice.</li></ul></div>
