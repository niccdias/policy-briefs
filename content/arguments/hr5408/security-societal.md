<p><strong>H.R.5408 · the Faster Labor Contracts Act</strong></p><p><span data-release-part="119_H_R_5408:description">This bill would change U.S. labor law for workers who have newly formed a union. An employer would have to begin talks soon after the union asks. If the two sides cannot agree on a first contract, either side can ask a federal agency for mediation, which is outside help with the talks. If mediation does not produce an agreement, a three-person panel decides, and both sides must follow the panel’s decision for two years. The bill would also require current pay and working conditions to stay in place during talks and would keep an employer’s duty to bargain until workers remove the union in an election.</span></p><p><strong>Comparison:</strong> <span data-release-part="119_H_R_5408:comparison">If this bill took effect on June 9, 2026, versus law in force then</span></p><details><summary>Bill version and evidence cutoff</summary><p>Engrossed in House · 2026-06-09. Evidence cutoff: 2026-07-17.</p><a href="https://niccdias.github.io/policy-briefs/arguments/hr5408/universalism-nature">Bill and sources</a></details>

<div class="approved-shared">
<details id="HR5408_C01"><summary><span data-release-part="HR5408_C01:title">The bill’s process for a binding first contract</span></summary><p><span data-release-part="HR5408_C01:statement">For bargaining toward a first contract after a union is newly recognized or certified: if there is no agreement 90 days after bargaining starts, either side may request federal mediation. If there is no agreement 30 days after that request, the federal mediation agency must refer the dispute to a three-person panel. The panel majority’s decision binds both sides for two years unless both agree in writing to change it. Either period can be longer only if both sides agree.</span></p><div data-release-part="HR5408_C01:background"><p>This route applies only to a first contract. It does not apply to later contracts. The bill sets no deadline for the panel’s decision: “it does not limit the timeline of arbitration.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260307172802/https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[UNC 2]</a></p><p>A union is an organization that represents workers in talks with their employer. A first contract is the first written work agreement between a new union and an employer. Mediation means an outside person helps the two sides talk; the mediator cannot decide for them. Arbitration means outside decision-makers decide. The Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) is the U.S. agency that provides mediators. Under the law in force on June 9, 2026, the duty to bargain “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260703004621/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="“29 U.S.C. § 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code edition)”, 2024 (2024 U.S. Code edition)">[UNN 1]</a>.</p></div><h3>Supporting evidence</h3><div data-release-part="HR5408_C01:support"><p>The bill states the first step: “either party may notify the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service that a dispute exists, and may request mediation.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[UNN 3]</a> It states the next step: “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[UNN 3]</a>. The panel has “one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[UNC 1]</a></p></div><h3>Response</h3><div data-release-part="HR5408_C01:response"></div><details><summary>Evidence about size, timing and reach</summary><div data-release-part="HR5408_C01:strength"></div></details></details>
