title: "H.R.5408 × Security – Societal"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Security – Societal. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- that their country is secure and stable
- to have a strong state that can defend its citizens
- that their country protect itself against all threats

*Read "their country" as the United States.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor H.R.5408?

Most of the Providence strike, like most strikes, falls outside the bill.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill gives the federal government a stronger tool to protect the public from labor deadlocks.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Current law requires good-faith talks but says that duty “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="United States Congress; U.S. Government Publishing Office, “29 U.S.C. §158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 1974-07-26">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill adds a binding way to settle the dispute. That serves Morgan’s wish for a strong state that protects its citizens.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The bill hands a new job to an agency too short-staffed to do it, so it does not give the state a stronger tool.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) is badly short of staff: “only 58 mediators remain at the agency” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="Lynn Rhinehart; Economic Policy Institute, “The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That is below what its own leaders said it needs for its current work: “FMCS needed at least 80–100 mediators to meet its statutory duties” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="Lynn Rhinehart; Economic Policy Institute, “The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In April 2026 the President went further: “The President’s 2027 request to Congress proposes closing FMCS” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2027-Congressional-Budget.pdf" title="Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS), “Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service, Fiscal Year 2027 Congressional Budget Submission”, 2026-04-03 (submitted to the Congress April 3, 2026)">[11]</a>. The request funds 18 staff, down from 95, which is “the maximum FTE necessary to conduct an orderly cessation of operations” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2027-Congressional-Budget.pdf" title="Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS), “Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service, Fiscal Year 2027 Congressional Budget Submission”, 2026-04-03 (submitted to the Congress April 3, 2026)">[11]</a>, that is, enough to shut down.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill adds work but no money or staff. FMCS must answer every request from any new union or employer, however small: “Whenever such a request is received, the Service shall promptly communicate with the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Supporters of the bill's approach agree the agency cannot do this as it stands. The bill does not bar strikes while it waits.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill's binding settlement rests on an agency the President wants closed, may come late, and could pull scarce mediators away from larger disputes like the Rhode Island hospital's. That is not a stronger state protecting its citizens.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> Settled contracts make work and business planning more stable.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> During talks, the bill requires the employer “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel’s decision is “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill’s sponsors explain that multi-year contracts make “budgeting more predictable and long-term financial planning easier to manage” <a href="https://thehill.com/opinion/congress-blog/5522225-faster-labor-contracts-act/" title="thehill.com, “Faster Labor Contracts Act: Boosting Union Negotiations”, September 26, 2025">[14]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> When setting terms, the panel must consider “the employer's financial status and prospects” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a> and “the employees' cost of living” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Keeping work rules in place during talks and settling terms for two years helps workers and firms plan their pay and costs. The Supreme Court ruled such a change unlawful as “a circumvention of the duty to negotiate” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/369/736" title="U.S. Supreme Court; full opinion hosted by Cornell Legal Information Institute, “National Labor Relations Board v. Katz, 369 U.S. 736 (1962)”, 1962-05-21 (decision date)">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel's terms last only two years: they are “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>. The panel exists only for the first contract: “an initial collective bargaining agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> For every later contract, current law returns, and neither side has to agree: the duty to bargain “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="United States Congress; U.S. Government Publishing Office, “29 U.S.C. §158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 1974-07-26">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The sponsors' praise was for “the multi-year contracts unions negotiate” <a href="https://thehill.com/opinion/congress-blog/5522225-faster-labor-contracts-act/" title="thehill.com, “Faster Labor Contracts Act: Boosting Union Negotiations”, September 26, 2025">[14]</a>, not terms set by a panel.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Imposed terms may not hold. A Heritage Foundation analyst warned that if the parties do not consent, “that contract could be unenforceable” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[16]</a>. Rep. Walberg warned on the House floor that such contracts “could mandate spending that small employers cannot sustain” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[17]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel must base its terms on money and business size alone. The bill says “Such decision shall be based on” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a> the employer's finances, its size and type, living costs, workers' ability to support their families, and pay at similar firms. None of these is staffing or safety.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Yet the PRO's own health-care example was a fight over “systemic understaffing, which leads them to care for more patients than they feel is safe” <a href="https://www.opb.org/article/2025/01/10/providence-nurses-and-some-doctors-walkout-as-open-ended-strike-begins/" title="Amelia Templeton, OPB (Oregon Public Broadcasting), “Providence nurses, and some doctors, walk out as open-ended strike begins”, 2025-01-10">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill adds two years of fixed terms on top of rules that already exist, and those terms may not last or address safety. The PRO does not link them to safer care or a more secure country.</li></ul></td></tr>

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Security – Societal gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408: it loads a shrunken federal mediation agency with new required cases, which pulls mediators away from labor disputes that threaten defense, health care and power.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO fails to show that Security – Societal gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408: it assumes added mediation work means less protection for essential services and leaves out the bill's way to prevent strikes.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> The bill orders the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) to step into every stalled talk over a first contract (the first labor contract after workers form a union) that either side brings to it. FMCS sends neutral go-betweens, called mediators, to help unions and employers reach a deal. After 90 days of talks without a deal: “Whenever such a request is received, the Service shall promptly communicate with the parties and use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to secure an agreement.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a> If there is still no deal 30 days later, FMCS must hand the case to a panel that writes a binding contract: “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill's required cases would pull FMCS's few mediators away from strikes that threaten defense, health care and power.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under current law, outside health care, FMCS chooses its cases. It steps in “whenever in its judgment such dispute threatens to cause a substantial interruption of commerce.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> As of early 2026, FMCS uses that choice to skip small cases outside health care: “FMCS issued a new policy providing mediators for all health care disputes but limiting mediation for all other private-sector disputes to those involving bargaining units of 250 or more employees” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill's order uses nearly the same words as the health-care rule, under which FMCS “shall promptly communicate with the parties and use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to bring them to agreement.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A federal court read those words as a duty to take every request: “That language creates a mandatory duty on behalf of FMCS to mediate, when called upon, in the healthcare industry.” <a href="https://www.jacksonlewis.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/nysd_AFT-v-Davis.640504.124.0.pdf" title="“American Federation of Teachers, AFL-CIO et al. v. Davis et al., No. 25-cv-3072 (AS), Opinion and Order”, 2025-12-30">[18]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Under the bill, FMCS could not turn away a first-contract case, however small.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Most union votes are in small workplaces: “In 2025, 60 percent of all representation elections involved units of 25 employees or fewer, and only 2 percent featured units of 500 or more.” <a href="https://nilrr.org/big-labor-union-organizing-plummets-in-2025/" title="“Big Labor Union Organizing Plummets in 2025”, 2026-01-21">[19]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Most new unions take far longer than 90 days to get a contract, so most could demand FMCS help: “only 36% of newly organized bargaining units achieve an initial collective bargaining agreement within a year” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[2]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A former head of the federal labor board who favors the bill's approach expects a surge: “This mandatory requirement would presumably significantly increase the demand for the FMCS’s services, as opposed to the current, voluntary system.” <a href="https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[20]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill also gives FMCS a new job: setting up the panels and filling any seat the two sides leave empty: “the Service shall designate any members not selected by the individual or labor organization or by the employer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> FMCS would face many new, small, required cases on top of its current work.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> FMCS already has fewer mediators than its own leaders said it needs for its current duties: “FMCS’s leadership stated that FMCS needed at least 80–100 mediators to meet its statutory duties” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> “Only 58 mediators remain at an agency that previously employed almost three times that number.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill adds no money for the new work. A National Restaurant Association official noted: “The FLCA also assumes a significant expansion in federal mediation capacity that has not been funded.” <a href="https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[21]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Each required first-contract case would take mediator time away from large disputes.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> FMCS calls its large-dispute work a national-security job: “FMCS collective bargaining mediation work particularly in the area of critical infrastructure is essential to national security.” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FMCS-PAR-FY2025-version-2.pdf" title="Davis Anna, “Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service: FY 2025 Performance and Accountability Report”, 2025-11-14 (signed report; official PDF uploaded February 2026)">[22]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> FMCS ties its work in defense, health care and power to protecting lives. Even in a government shutdown, it plans to keep mediating where “the parties are in certain critical industries, including most particularly the defense, health care, and power generation industries” and its help is needed “to protect life or property” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-Lapse-of-Approps-20260114.pdf" title="“Lapse Plan for Agency Operations in the Absence of Appropriations”, 2026-01-14">[23]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> When mediators were pulled away in 2025, a hospital was left facing a strike: “FMCS mediation to resolve a dispute with United Nurses and Allied Professionals was canceled, placing the hospital at risk of a major work stoppage.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill raises the risk that strikes at defense plants, hospitals and power plants get late help or none. That weakens the government's power to protect its people and keep the country stable, which Morgan holds very important.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> FMCS's current budget does not cover this work. Congress has held the agency near its old budget: it “has continued to fund the agency at $48.7 million, close to its FY 2025 level.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a> Even pro-union backers of first-contract arbitration say Congress “must increase FMCS’s funding and first contract mediation staffing and expertise if the Faster Labor Contracts Act or PRO Act become law” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a>. The bill does not.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO does not establish that helping newly unionized workers get a first contract would take help away from disputes that threaten defense, health care or power.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Current law already requires the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) to mediate first-contract disputes at health care institutions: “Where the bargaining is for an initial agreement following certification or recognition” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/158" title="“29 U.S. Code § 158 - Unfair labor practices”, 1974-07-26 (date of the last amendment shown; text as in force before H.R. 5408)">[24]</a>. After notice, “the Service shall promptly communicate with the parties and use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to bring them to agreement.” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/158" title="“29 U.S. Code § 158 - Unfair labor practices”, 1974-07-26 (date of the last amendment shown; text as in force before H.R. 5408)">[24]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO cannot count all hospital first-contract mediation as new work caused by this bill.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Unresolved cases move to a separate panel after a “30-day period beginning on the date on which the request for mediation is made” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>, unless both sides agree to more time: “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The parties choose the panel first: “one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Failed mediation has an endpoint, and the contract decision moves to a separate panel.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> There is an official estimate for a comparable process. The Economic Policy Institute says the bill “contains the first contract mediation and arbitration provisions from the PRO Act” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a>. In its 2019 PRO Act estimate, the Congressional Budget Office reported “around 500 notifications annually” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2019-12/hr2474_0.pdf" title="“Cost Estimate: H.R. 2474, Protecting the Right to Organize Act of 2019, as ordered reported by the House Committee on Education and Labor on September 25, 2019”, 2019-12-05">[25]</a>, with mediation in “roughly 10 percent of initial collective bargaining cases” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2019-12/hr2474_0.pdf" title="“Cost Estimate: H.R. 2474, Protecting the Right to Organize Act of 2019, as ordered reported by the House Committee on Education and Labor on September 25, 2019”, 2019-12-05">[25]</a>, and projected that “the number of initial cases mediated by FMCS would double under the bill because either party could request mediation” <a href="https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2019-12/hr2474_0.pdf" title="“Cost Estimate: H.R. 2474, Protecting the Right to Organize Act of 2019, as ordered reported by the House Committee on Education and Labor on September 25, 2019”, 2019-12-05">[25]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> That estimate implied about 50 added mediation cases a year under a comparable process. A large new caseload cannot be inferred just from how long first contracts take today.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A Canadian study of letting a panel set the first contract's terms found that this encourages agreement by “creating an incentive for the parties to bargain in good faith and negotiate an agreement.” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA working paper 2009-01)”, 2008-12 (date printed on the paper)">[6]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Delays under today's law cannot simply be carried forward as demand under a law that lets a panel settle the contract.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> FMCS reports that its priorities include “health care and critical infrastructure cases where there would be a substantial interruption of interstate commerce.” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FMCS-PAR-FY2025-version-2.pdf" title="Davis Anna, “Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service: FY 2025 Performance and Accountability Report”, 2025-11-14 (signed report; official PDF uploaded February 2026)">[22]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill's duty to provide first-contract mediation does not order small cases ahead of urgent ones. An added duty alone does not establish that essential services lose help.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The hospital example followed an order to “reduce its staff to five mediators” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a>. The resulting cancellation put the hospital “at risk of a major work stoppage.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/publication/the-trump-administrations-short-sighted-attacks-on-the-federal-mediation-and-conciliation-service/" title="“The Trump administration’s short-sighted attacks on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service”, 2026-03-02">[10]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> That example shows the harm of removing mediators. It does not show what this bill's process of mediation followed by a panel decision would do.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing emergency powers also remain available. After a board of inquiry reports, the President can direct the Attorney General to seek a court order against a strike or lockout that “affects an entire industry or a substantial part thereof” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/178" title="“29 U.S. Code § 178 - Injunctions during national emergency”, 1949-05-24 (date of the last amendment shown; enacted June 23, 1947; text as in force before H.R. 5408)">[26]</a> and “will imperil the national health or safety” <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/178" title="“29 U.S. Code § 178 - Injunctions during national emergency”, 1949-05-24 (date of the last amendment shown; enacted June 23, 1947; text as in force before H.R. 5408)">[26]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill leaves the state able to seek a halt to work stoppages that meet those national-emergency conditions.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The bill offers a way to settle first-contract disputes without strikes, supporting stable essential services.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> A stalled first contract can end in a panel decision “settling the dispute” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>, which is “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Susan Johnson's Canadian study found that first-contract arbitration reduced the chance of a strike or lockout during first-contract talks “by at least 50 percent.” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA working paper 2009-01)”, 2008-12 (date printed on the paper)">[6]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Knowing that a panel can decide the contract gives employers and unions a reason to reach a deal. The panel can settle terms when talks fail. Resolving those disputes without strikes protects the continuity of services Morgan values.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> H.R.5408 would tie up a shrunken mediation agency with many small required cases and leave less help for strikes that threaten defense, health care and power, so Morgan's wish for a secure, stable country that protects its people gives a reason to oppose it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO has not shown that the bill would weaken national protection, and its path to a settled first contract addresses a source of labor unrest, so the PRO has not grounded opposition in Morgan's value.</p></td></tr>
