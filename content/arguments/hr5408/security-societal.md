**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

The bill adds a path to an imposed first union contract. After 90 days of talks, either side may request mediation by the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS). After 30 days from that request without a deal, “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. Both periods can be extended by agreement: “or such additional period as the parties may agree upon” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. The panel’s decision is “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a></p></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><span class="lbl">Point 1</span> It adds compulsory work to an agency that protects essential services and already limits its caseload.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Requiring first-contract mediation at either side’s request adds demand for FMCS help. Former NLRB chair Lauren McFerran writes: “This mandatory requirement would presumably significantly increase the demand for the FMCS’s services, as opposed to the current, voluntary system.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20251211030125/https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[5]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> FMCS would also have to fill arbitration panel seats when the parties fail to choose members: “the Service shall designate any members not selected by the individual or labor organization or by the employer.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The agency’s April 2026 budget reports limits on the groups of workers it serves: “FMCS limited its involvement to facilities that provide health care services with bargaining units of 250 or more members, and to bargaining units of 1,000 members or more for all other industries in which it has jurisdiction.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029145606/https://fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2027-Congressional-Budget.pdf" title="Michelle Batts-Dunkins, “Fiscal Year 2027 Congressional Budget Submission”, 2026-04-03">[21]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill provides no funding for more staff. The National Restaurant Association warns: “The FLCA also assumes a significant expansion in federal mediation capacity that has not been funded.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029145207/https://edworkforce.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=413396" title="House Education and Workforce Committee, press release, June 9, 2026">[22]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> FMCS describes its role: “Our mediators work tirelessly to minimize and prevent disruptions affecting utilities, health care, public safety and security.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201051/https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FMCS-PAR-FY2025-version-2.pdf" title="Davis Anna, “Fiscal Year 2025 Performance and Accountability Report”, 2025-11-14 (transmittal; PDF posted February 2026)">[23]</a></li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> New compulsory cases compete for the resources used to prevent disruptions to power and health care. That risks weakening the state’s ability to keep essential services running and protect citizens, giving [NAME] a reason to oppose the added burden.</li></ul></td><td><p><span class="lbl">Response</span> The PRO does not establish that the new duties would raise the risk of losing urgent mediation.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Health-care mediation already carries a legal duty, including first-contract disputes after notice: “the Service shall promptly communicate with the parties and use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to bring them to agreement.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260703004621/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="“29 U.S.C. § 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition">[20]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS) already handles first-contract cases: it “mediates collective bargaining negotiations for initial contract negotiations” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029201051/https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FMCS-PAR-FY2025-version-2.pdf" title="Davis Anna, “Fiscal Year 2025 Performance and Accountability Report”, 2025-11-14 (transmittal; PDF posted February 2026)">[23]</a>. The relevant burden is the extra work created by the new duty, beyond this existing work.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing FMCS policy offers mediation in disputes “which imperil or threaten to imperil the national health or safety.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029192609/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title29-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title29-vol4-part1403.pdf" title="“29 CFR Part 1403 — Functions and Duties”, 2025-07-01 (annual Code of Federal Regulations edition; source rule dated 1967-07-06)">[24]</a> The bill does not remove that policy or the health-care duty.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The added panel appointments depend on disputes remaining unsettled. Referral follows only when “the Service is not able to bring the parties to agreement by conciliation” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. In Canadian first-contract arbitration, Johnson finds that “the threat of having a first agreement imposed creates an incentive for the parties to reach agreement on their own.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20221212230802/https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages”, 2008-12 (date printed on the paper)">[4]</a> The bill creates that incentive through an award “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029203943/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li class="so"><span class="lbl">So</span> The PRO needs a link from the extra work to a greater risk that urgent disputes will lose help. Existing duties, the urgent-service policy, and settlement incentives make that a separate claim needing support.</li></ul></td></tr>
