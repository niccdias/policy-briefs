title: "H.R.5408 × Universalism – Tolerance"
---

**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Universalism – Tolerance. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to be tolerant toward all kinds of people and groups
- to listen to and understand people who are different to them
- to accept people even when they disagree with them

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor H.R.5408?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Morgan's value of tolerance gives Morgan a reason to favor H.R.5408, because the bill makes employers and newly unionized workers hear each other out, accept each other, and settle their disagreements peacefully.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO gives Morgan no tolerance-based reason to favor H.R.5408 because it treats securing a contract as proof that people who disagree are heard and accepted.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> The bill sets deadlines for a new union's first contract. If talks stall, it adds mediation (a neutral helper guides the talks) and then arbitration (a panel sets binding terms): “This bill establishes mandatory deadlines for parties negotiating an initial collective bargaining agreement (CBA) and provides for mediation and arbitration to finalize CBAs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, June 9, 2026">[1]</a></p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill makes the two sides actually talk, with a neutral helper, so neither side can ignore the other.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under current law, an employer who stalls risks little: “if they fail to do so, the remedy is to remind them of the need to bargain in good faith” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-faster-labor-contracts-act-should-be-just-the-starting-point-for-labor-law-reform/" title="Center for American Progress, “The Faster Labor Contracts Act Should Be Just the Starting Point for Labor Law Reform - Center for American Progress”, July 7, 2026">[2]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Today, federal help with contract talks is “completely free, completely voluntary” <a href="https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[3]</a>. So either side can turn it down.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires talks to start within 10 days of the union's written request. Both sides “shall make every reasonable effort to conclude and sign a collective bargaining agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> After 90 days without a deal, both sides “must participate in FMCS mediation at either party’s request” <a href="https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[3]</a>. FMCS is the federal mediation service. The bill tells it to “use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to secure an agreement.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In this kind of mediation, “a neutral third party comes in to help facilitate the bargaining process between parties that do not have a mature bargaining relationship.” <a href="https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The arbitration backstop also pushes the sides to work out their own deal. A study of Canada's first-contract arbitration laws found that “the threat of having a first agreement imposed creates an incentive for the parties to reach agreement on their own” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Neither group can refuse to hear the other, and a neutral person helps two groups who distrust each other understand each other. Morgan values listening to and understanding people who are different. The bill builds that into the law for bosses and workers, whichever side Morgan agrees with.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The bill's pressure to settle applies even when both sides have listened in good faith and still disagree.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing law already requires both sides “to meet at reasonable times and confer in good faith” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="United States Congress; U.S. Government Publishing Office, GovInfo, “29 U.S.C. § 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code)”, 2024 edition; section last amended July 26, 1974">[6]</a>. It also says that this duty “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title29/html/USCODE-2024-title29-chap7-subchapII-sec158.htm" title="United States Congress; U.S. Government Publishing Office, GovInfo, “29 U.S.C. § 158 — Unfair labor practices (2024 U.S. Code)”, 2024 edition; section last amended July 26, 1974">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> If mediation fails after 30 days, unless both sides extend that period, “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. This trigger requires no finding that either side refused to listen or bargained in bad faith.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The mediator's task is “to secure an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. The PRO's study explains the incentive to settle through “the threat of having a first agreement imposed” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The added rules press people toward a contract even when their disagreement is sincere. Taking part in mediation or settling to avoid an imposed contract does not establish understanding or acceptance. Accepting people leaves room to reject their demands.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill makes employers who oppose a union still accept and deal with the group their workers chose.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Today, stalling is a way to get rid of a group the employer dislikes. The bill's findings say: “The employers can use those delays to sap labor organization resolve and secure more favorable terms for the employer.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The teachers' union AFT reports employers who “turn their energies toward wearing down a new union by refusing to negotiate a first contract.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="American Federation of Teachers; Randi Weingarten, President, AFT., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[7]</a> Such delays “are meant to send a message to workers that their voice and their vote do not matter” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="American Federation of Teachers; Randi Weingarten, President, AFT., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The stalling often works: “a third of newly organized bargaining units still do not have a first contract after three years.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[8]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill blocks this. The employer's duty to bargain “shall continue absent decertification of the representative following an election” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. So the employer cannot walk away on its own. Only a worker vote to remove the union ends the duty.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> While talks go on, the employer must “maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. So it cannot change pay or hours on its own in the meantime.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill makes an employer deal with a group it may dislike, instead of waiting for the group to fall apart. Morgan values accepting people even when disagreeing with them. The bill writes that acceptance into workplace law.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The added recognition rule can keep a union in place after workers reject it, so it cannot simply be equated with accepting dissenting people.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Current law ordinarily protects a union's election win for a year. The Supreme Court held that its official certification “must be honored for a "reasonable" period, ordinarily "one year," in the absence of "unusual circumstances."” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/348/96/" title="U.S. Supreme Court (opinion by Justice Frankfurter); text via Justia U.S. Supreme Court Center, “Brooks v. Labor Board, 348 U.S. 96 (1954)”, December 6, 1954">[9]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Outside protected periods, current law permits an employer to stop recognizing a union “if it can show through objective evidence that the union has lost majority support” <a href="https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dc-circuit/1616152.html" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit (opinion by Circuit Judge Rogers); text via FindLaw, “SFO Good-Nite Inn, LLC v. National Labor Relations Board, Nos. 11-1295, 11-1325”, November 20, 2012">[10]</a>. That means workers' support, not union membership or the employer's dislike.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> An employer cannot supply that proof through “a petition that it unlawfully assisted, supported, or otherwise unlawfully encouraged” <a href="https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dc-circuit/1616152.html" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit (opinion by Circuit Judge Rogers); text via FindLaw, “SFO Good-Nite Inn, LLC v. National Labor Relations Board, Nos. 11-1295, 11-1325”, November 20, 2012">[10]</a>. Existing law already bars using such pressure to justify dropping the union.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires continued bargaining “absent decertification of the representative following an election” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. Decertification means voting the union out.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The employer must keep recognizing the union until an election removes it, even where an independent worker majority's rejection would allow recognition to end under current law. The added rule closes that route for workers who change their minds. Keeping their rejected representative in place does not establish acceptance of those workers. Keeping pay and hours steady does not answer that objection either.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> When the two sides still disagree, the bill settles the dispute through a balanced panel instead of a bitter fight.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> If 30 days of mediation fail, a three-person panel decides. It has “one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel would “hear from both parties” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[8]</a>. It must weigh the employer's “financial status and prospects” and “the employees' ability to sustain themselves, their families, and their dependents on the wages and benefits they earn from the employer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Without such a path, first-contract strikes are fights over whether the union is accepted at all. They “fundamentally concern union recognition and can be very bitter and protracted disputes.” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In Canada, similar laws “reduced work stoppages on first-contract negotiations in the private sector by at least 50 percent.” <a href="https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-faster-labor-contracts-act-should-be-just-the-starting-point-for-labor-law-reform/" title="Center for American Progress, “The Faster Labor Contracts Act Should Be Just the Starting Point for Labor Law Reform - Center for American Progress”, July 7, 2026">[2]</a> (Work stoppages are strikes and lockouts.)</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> Critics say the panel “imposes a contract on employers and employees for at least 2 years without their consent” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Walberg, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[11]</a>. But in Canada, first-contract arbitration “is not accessed frequently and it is even rarer for a first contract (in whole or in part) to be imposed” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>. When a panel does rule, each side has picked one member. The two sides can still change the terms by “written consent of the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Groups that still disagree end up with shared rules they can both live with, not a long fight over whether one side should exist. Morgan values accepting people despite disagreement. The bill gives a peaceful way to do that.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> A panel can settle terms without either side accepting them, and the Canadian strike figures do not establish mutual acceptance.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill makes a panel majority's decision binding without requiring workers to approve it: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Choosing panel members and weighing both sides' needs do not let either side reject the result. A side seeking a change can be blocked by the other side for two years. The panel's power to overrule continued disagreement does not show that the groups have learned to accept one another.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study identifies Manitoba's arbitration system as based on elapsed time, without proof of failed good-faith bargaining: “There is no need to provide evidence of dysfunctional bargaining” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>. Its reported share of imposed contracts per new union certification was “7.5 percent in Manitoba” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>, against an overall figure of “1.4 percent” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> A low rate across Canada's different systems cannot establish how rarely this bill's time-based rule would impose terms.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study could not separate the strike effects of the different systems: “There is not enough variation across Canadian jurisdictions in the use of different types of FCA to permit the analysis to estimate the affect of each type of FCA on incidence.” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a> FCA means first-contract arbitration; incidence means how often strikes or lockouts occur.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Once arbitration was sought, the Canadian rules required that “any work stoppage in progress must stop” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>. H.R.5408's referral rule, quoted above, adds no matching requirement to end strikes or lockouts.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study found “no statistically significant impact on the duration of first agreement work stoppages” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University; Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008">[5]</a>. It therefore supplies no clear evidence that strikes which did occur became shorter.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO cannot turn fewer strikes under varied Canadian rules into proof that this bill would make opposing groups accept one another. Strike counts and binding terms do not establish the listening, understanding, or acceptance Morgan values.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The bill makes opposing groups at work listen to each other, accept each other, and settle disputes peacefully, so Morgan's value of tolerance gives Morgan a reason to favor it.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO has not connected the bill's added contract rules to acceptance or understanding of people who disagree, so it gives Morgan no tolerance-based reason to favor H.R.5408.</p></td></tr>

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408?

<table class="pc">
<tr><th>PRO</th><th>CON</th></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Universalism–Tolerance gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408 because its binding first-contract process weakens room for disagreement, workers’ voice, and mutual understanding.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO shows that the bill takes away each side's power to block a first contract, but tolerance is about how people treat those who differ, not about who holds a veto.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> After 90 days of first-contract talks, either side may request federal mediation—help reaching an agreement. If no agreement follows within 30 days of that request, unless both sides allow more time, “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. That panel decides the terms, which are “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a></p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> The bill subjects honest disagreements to forced settlement.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Current law requires honest bargaining but preserves each side’s choice: “such obligation does not compel either party to agree to a proposal or require the making of a concession” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/397/99/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “H. K. Porter Co., Inc. v. NLRB, 397 U.S. 99 (1970)”, 1970-03-02 (decision)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill’s trigger is failure to agree, without any finding of dishonest bargaining: “the parties have failed to reach an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Talks can take longer because workplaces differ: “The time it takes to reach first contracts varies significantly depending on factors like the industry, employer size, workplace complexity, negotiator experience, and the level of cooperation or contentiousness between negotiators.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[13]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Accepting people who disagree means leaving room for sincere differences between workers and employers. The bill allows a binding order even when neither side has bargained dishonestly.</li><li><strong>Response:</strong> Extra time requires agreement: “such additional period as the parties may agree upon” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. One side cannot secure extra time on its own.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Ending a deadlock with a neutral decision, after both sides are heard, does not reject anyone who disagrees.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Before any panel, a federal mediator must “use its best efforts, by mediation and conciliation, to secure an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel then meets to “hear from both parties” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel must weigh how workplaces differ, including “the size and type of the employer's operations and business” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a> and “the employees' cost of living” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under current law, an honest deadlock is not left alone. It is settled by pressure. Each side may “rely ultimately on its economic strength to try to secure what it cannot obtain through bargaining” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/397/99/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “H. K. Porter Co., Inc. v. NLRB, 397 U.S. 99 (1970)”, 1970-03-02 (decision)">[12]</a>. The law does not promise “that strikes and lockouts will never result from a bargaining impasse” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/397/99/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “H. K. Porter Co., Inc. v. NLRB, 397 U.S. 99 (1970)”, 1970-03-02 (decision)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In Canada, first-contract arbitration “reduces first agreement work stoppage incidence by at least 50 percent” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University / Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008 (working paper 2009-01)">[5]</a>. A work stoppage is a strike or lockout.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The process starts only if a side asks: “either party may notify the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service that a dispute exists, and may request mediation” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. Two sides that both want more time can keep talking. And the rule that extra time needs both sides' consent also stops one side from stalling alone.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill swaps a test of who can hold out longer for a hearing where both sides speak. The PRO shows lost leverage, not lost acceptance of people.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> The bill lets a panel bind workers to terms they would vote down.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Union members usually approve contracts under their unions’ own rules: “nearly all union constitutions and bylaws require union members’ ratification of collective bargaining agreements before they are signed and enforced.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[13]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel’s decision does not depend on that approval. As Representative Foxx warned, “workers themselves may be bound by contracts they never approved.” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="Foxx, floor speech, Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[14]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The union’s appointee can be outvoted: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. The employer’s appointee and the neutral member can impose terms over that member’s objection.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers can support a union yet reject particular terms. Bypassing their approval removes a way to make negotiators listen to those different views. Morgan’s concern for people who disagree gives a reason to preserve that voice.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Losing a final vote does not leave dissenting workers unheard, and the vote the PRO defends never shielded dissenters.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Majority rule already binds workers who disagree: “the representative designated by the majority of the employees represents the minority as well” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/397/99/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “H. K. Porter Co., Inc. v. NLRB, 397 U.S. 99 (1970)”, 1970-03-02 (decision)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Federal law does not require a contract vote. Unions “are not normally required to let their members participate in negotiations or to ratify contracts” <a href="https://digitalcommons.law.umaryland.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1225&amp;context=jbtl" title="Melanie Stallings Williams and Dennis A. Halcoussis; Journal of Business &amp; Technology Law, vol. 9, no. 2, p. 213 (University of Maryland Francis King Carey School of Law, DigitalCommons@UM Carey Law), “Unions and Democracy: When Do Nonmembers Have Voting Rights?”, 2014">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Workers who stay out of the union can be shut out of the vote. Under past court rulings, “a union could exclude agency fee payers from voting on their employment contracts” <a href="https://digitalcommons.law.umaryland.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1225&amp;context=jbtl" title="Melanie Stallings Williams and Dennis A. Halcoussis; Journal of Business &amp; Technology Law, vol. 9, no. 2, p. 213 (University of Maryland Francis King Carey School of Law, DigitalCommons@UM Carey Law), “Unions and Democracy: When Do Nonmembers Have Voting Rights?”, 2014">[15]</a>. Agency fee payers are nonmembers who pay for representation.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Each side picks one panel member, and the third is “mutually agreed to by the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. The employer's member can be outvoted just as the union's can.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The union must still “serve the interests of all members without hostility or discrimination toward any” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/735/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “Communications Workers of America v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735 (1988)”, 1988-06-29 (decision)">[16]</a>. The bill makes no exception for arbitration.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The panel must weigh “the employees' ability to sustain themselves, their families, and their dependents on the wages and benefits they earn from the employer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Workers can still press for changes, since the decision binds “unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Today's vote lets a majority bind a minority and can leave nonmembers out. A panel that must weigh workers' needs, used only after talks and mediation fail, takes from dissenters no acceptance they had.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> The bill can reward holding out instead of working to understand the other side.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Building understanding takes sustained dialogue. Former manufacturing executive Joe Eddy describes “sitting down together with my employees, talking through challenges and building trust over time.” <a href="https://www.heraldstaronline.com/opinion/local-columns/2025/10/guest-column-government-should-stay-out-of-contract-negotiations/" title="The Herald Star, “Guest column/Government should stay out of contract negotiations - The Herald Star”, October 18, 2025">[17]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Labor lawyer Richard Lehr warns that the bill weakens the reason to compromise: “After all, the union knows it will get a contract so why not take a chance on the terms the arbitration panel may decide.” <a href="https://www.worklaw.com/blog/the-fastor-labor-contracts-act-here-we-go-again" title="Richard Lehr, Lehr Middlebrooks Vreeland &amp; Thompson, P.C. / Worklaw Network, “The Faster Labor Contracts Act - &quot;Here We Go Again&quot;”, 2026-06-05">[18]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> A negotiator expecting better terms from the panel has less reason to win the other side over. Morgan’s wish to listen to and understand different people gives a reason to oppose that incentive.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The record shows that the threat of arbitration pushes both sides to settle; it is current law that rewards holding out.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Lehr offers only his view. A study of Canadian provinces with first-contract arbitration (FCA) found that “FCA is not accessed frequently and it is even rarer for a first contract (in whole or in part) to be imposed” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University / Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008 (working paper 2009-01)">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The study concludes that FCA “encourages collective bargaining by creating an incentive for the parties to bargain in good faith and negotiate an agreement” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University / Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008 (working paper 2009-01)">[5]</a>. Collective bargaining means talks between the union and the employer.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> In Quebec, where panels imposed first contracts that were later renewed, both sides agreed “that the climate has improved over time and that they have learned to speak to one another and to come to agreements” <a href="https://isidore.science/document/10.7202/050308ar" title="Jean Sexton; Relations industrielles / Industrial Relations, vol. 42 no. 2 (Consortium Érudit), record hosted by isidore.science, “L&#x27;arbitrage de première convention collective au Québec: 1978-1984 (First Contract Arbitration in Quebec: 1978-1984)”, 1987">[19]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Lehr himself says the union would “take a chance on the terms the arbitration panel may decide” <a href="https://www.worklaw.com/blog/the-fastor-labor-contracts-act-here-we-go-again" title="Richard Lehr, Lehr Middlebrooks Vreeland &amp; Thompson, P.C. / Worklaw Network, “The Faster Labor Contracts Act - &quot;Here We Go Again&quot;”, 2026-06-05">[18]</a>. The panel must weigh “the employer's financial status and prospects” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. Neither side knows what it will get, so both have a reason to settle.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Current law already pays off for stalling. In the PRO's own Supreme Court case, a delay of over eight years came “chiefly because of the skill of the company's negotiators in taking advantage of every opportunity for delay” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/397/99/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “H. K. Porter Co., Inc. v. NLRB, 397 U.S. 99 (1970)”, 1970-03-02 (decision)">[12]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Employers “slow-walk the bargaining process because there are no penalties for doing so” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[8]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires the sitting down that Eddy praises. Both sides must meet and “make every reasonable effort to conclude and sign a collective bargaining agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO's worry about incentives is a guess that the record contradicts. The bill replaces a system that rewards stalling with one that pushes both sides toward their own deal.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 4:</strong> The bill could impose financial support on workers who reject the union.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Where state law permits it, a contract can require nonmembers to pay for union representation to keep their jobs. The Supreme Court describes payments required “as a condition of continued employment, whether or not the employees become union members.” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/735/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “Communications Workers of America v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735 (1988)”, 1988-06-29 (decision)">[16]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The required support concerns representation: “collective bargaining, contract administration, and grievance adjustment” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/735/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “Communications Workers of America v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735 (1988)”, 1988-06-29 (decision)">[16]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill adds a way to impose such payments without a negotiated agreement. A critic of the Senate companion warns it “would empower bureaucrats to impose a forced-dues regime” <a href="https://nrtwc.org/renegade-gop-politicians-favor-more-forced-dues/" title="Right To Work Staff / National Right to Work Committee, “Renegade GOP Politicians Favor More Forced Dues”, 2025-05-22">[20]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan’s acceptance of people who disagree gives a reason to resist giving a panel power to make dissenting workers’ jobs depend on paying an organization they reject.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The bill says nothing about union fees, and the PRO's fee harm rests on guesses that other critics of the bill reject.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill lists what the panel's decision “shall be based on” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>: the employer's finances and size, living costs, workers' ability to support their families, and pay at similar employers. Union fees are not on the list.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO's evidence is an advocacy group's warning <a href="https://nrtwc.org/renegade-gop-politicians-favor-more-forced-dues/" title="Right To Work Staff / National Right to Work Committee, “Renegade GOP Politicians Favor More Forced Dues”, 2025-05-22">[20]</a> and a lawyer's open question: “will the panel include that in a first contract?” <a href="https://www.worklaw.com/blog/the-fastor-labor-contracts-act-here-we-go-again" title="Richard Lehr, Lehr Middlebrooks Vreeland &amp; Thompson, P.C. / Worklaw Network, “The Faster Labor Contracts Act - &quot;Here We Go Again&quot;”, 2026-06-05">[18]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Heritage, which opposes the bill, argues the reverse. It says terms set by a government arbitrator “could potentially extend those same right-to-work protections to all private-sector employees across the U.S.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[13]</a>. Right-to-work rules bar contracts that require payments to a union.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Where fees are allowed, workers “could not be compelled to join unions but could be required to pay agency fees” <a href="https://digitalcommons.law.umaryland.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1225&amp;context=jbtl" title="Melanie Stallings Williams and Dennis A. Halcoussis; Journal of Business &amp; Technology Law, vol. 9, no. 2, p. 213 (University of Maryland Francis King Carey School of Law, DigitalCommons@UM Carey Law), “Unions and Democracy: When Do Nonmembers Have Voting Rights?”, 2014">[15]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The charge covers “only those fees necessary to finance collective bargaining activities” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/735/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “Communications Workers of America v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735 (1988)”, 1988-06-29 (decision)">[16]</a>. Its basis is that “those who enjoy union-negotiated benefits contribute to their cost” <a href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/735/" title="Supreme Court of the United States; text hosted by Justia, “Communications Workers of America v. Beck, 487 U.S. 735 (1988)”, 1988-06-29 (decision)">[16]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO has not shown that the bill would add fees at all. A capped charge for a service one receives does not require joining or agreeing with the union, so it rejects no one.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><p><strong>New point:</strong> The PRO leaves out that the bill makes an employer that would rather ignore a new union sit down and listen to it.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under current law, “There is little to discourage employers from bargaining in bad faith” <a href="https://lcerpa.org/public/papers/LCERPA_2009-01.pdf" title="Susan Johnson, Associate Professor, Department of Economics, Wilfrid Laurier University / Laurier Centre for Economic Research &amp; Policy Analysis (LCERPA), “First Contract Arbitration: Effects on Bargaining and Work Stoppages (LCERPA Economic Research Paper 2009-01; listed online as &quot;First Contract Arbitration: Lessons from Canada&quot;)”, December 2008 (working paper 2009-01)">[5]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The AFT reports that “many employers simply refuse to bargain a first contract” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="American Federation of Teachers; Randi Weingarten, President, AFT., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>. Long delays and refusals, it says, “are meant to send a message to workers that their voice and their vote do not matter” <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-06-09/html/CREC-2026-06-09-pt1-PgH4029.htm" title="American Federation of Teachers; Randi Weingarten, President, AFT., letter inserted in the Congressional Record, June 9, 2026">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires both sides to “meet and begin bargaining collectively” within 10 days of a request <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[4]</a>. Either side can then call in a mediator.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> That mediation service exists to “help workers, unions, and employers foster collaborative labor management relationships” <a href="https://tcf.org/content/commentary/trumps-war-on-the-labor-peacemakers/" title="The Century Foundation, analysis, March 31, 2025">[3]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Morgan's value calls for listening to people who are different. The bill's main effect makes reluctant employers listen to workers they oppose. That points Morgan's value toward the bill, not against it.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Protecting honest disagreement, workers’ voice, mutual understanding, and dissenters’ choices gives Morgan a reason to oppose H.R.5408.</p></td><td><p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> The PRO shows that the bill limits each side's veto over a first contract, not that it leaves anyone less heard or less accepted, so Universalism–Tolerance gives Morgan no reason to oppose H.R.5408.</p></td></tr>
