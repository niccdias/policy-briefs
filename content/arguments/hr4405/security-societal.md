**Bill:** [H.R.4405](https://web.archive.org/web/20261002112301/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405)), the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on November 18, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, November 18, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country is secure and stable. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to have a strong state that can defend its citizens. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] that their country protect itself against all threats. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?

**Reference:** Read "their country" as the United States.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.4405?

**Bill description:** H.R.4405 requires the U.S. Attorney General to publicly release all unclassified Department of Justice records about Jeffrey Epstein, including flight logs, names of government officials, and records of his death in custody. It may withhold victims' personal information, active investigations, and properly classified national-security material.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Judged as of November 18, 2025, the bill would add a duty to publish covered Justice Department records within 30 days, subject to its listed exceptions: “Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment of this Act” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>; “make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format all unclassified records, documents, communications, and investigative materials in the possession of the Department of Justice” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>. Existing public-records law allowed withholding law-enforcement information whose release “could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion of personal privacy” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260923012059/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title5/html/USCODE-2024-title5-partI-chap5-subchapII-sec552.htm" title="“5 U.S.C. §552 — Public information; agency rules, opinions, orders, records, and proceedings (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 2016-06-30">[2]</a>. The bill expressly bars withholding for “embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>.</p></div>
