**Bill:** [H.R.4405](https://web.archive.org/web/20261002112301/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405)), the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on November 18, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, November 18, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by an average American: ordinary personal health and safety risks in U.S. circumstances, without assuming a specially exposed person.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.4405?

**Bill description:** H.R.4405 requires the U.S. Attorney General to publicly release all unclassified Department of Justice records about Jeffrey Epstein, including flight logs, names of government officials, and records of his death in custody. It may withhold victims' personal information, active investigations, and properly classified national-security material.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on November 18, 2025, the bill adds a duty to publish the Department of Justice’s (DOJ’s) covered Epstein files within 30 days, subject to its listed exceptions: “Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the Attorney General shall, subject to subsection (b), make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format all unclassified records, documents, communications, and investigative materials in the possession of the Department of Justice” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>.</p><ul><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> Existing public records law allowed some internal government records to be withheld: “inter-agency or intra-agency memorandums or letters that would not be available by law to a party other than an agency in litigation with the agency” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260923012059/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title5/html/USCODE-2024-title5-partI-chap5-subchapII-sec552.htm" title="“5 U.S.C. § 552, Public information; agency rules, opinions, orders, records, and proceedings (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition; relevant FOIA provisions last amended 2016-06-30">[2]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> DOJ had also decided that “no further disclosure would be appropriate or warranted.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261023195024/https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1407001/dl" title="U.S. Department of Justice, “Department of Justice and Federal Bureau of Investigation memorandum on Jeffrey Epstein investigative holdings”, 2025-07-06 (publication date identified in evidence/opinion_tracey_newsletter_concealing.txt)">[3]</a></li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> The bill bars withholding for “embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>.</li><li class="because"><span class="lbl">Because</span> It permits withholding portions that “would jeopardize an active federal investigation or ongoing prosecution, provided that such withholding is narrowly tailored and temporary” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>.</li></ul></div>
