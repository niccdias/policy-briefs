**Bill:** [H.R.4405](https://web.archive.org/web/20261002112301/https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405) ([live page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4405)), the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on November 18, 2025, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Engrossed in House, November 18, 2025. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to honor the traditional practices of their culture. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.4405?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference. Tradition here concerns preserving, observing or passing down identifiable inherited customs, religious practices and ways of life, valued for their continuity with the past. Religious endorsement of a general moral goal alone does not establish that connection.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.4405?

**Bill description:** H.R.4405 requires the U.S. Attorney General to publicly release all unclassified Department of Justice records about Jeffrey Epstein, including flight logs, names of government officials, and records of his death in custody. It may withhold victims' personal information, active investigations, and properly classified national-security material.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> If enacted on November 18, 2025, the bill would require the Justice Department (DOJ) to “make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format all unclassified records, documents, communications, and investigative materials in the possession of the Department of Justice” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a> concerning the listed Epstein matters, subject to specified exceptions. The deadline is “Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment of this Act” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>. This adds a broad, timed legal duty to earlier disclosures: a November 18 statement reported that “the Committee has released more than 65,000 pages of records” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029142725/http://fedorchak.house.gov/media/press-releases/fedorchak-votes-transparency-and-justice-epstein-victims" title="Fedorchak, press release, November 18, 2025">[2]</a>, while DOJ’s July memo had said “no further disclosure would be appropriate or warranted” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261023195024/https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1407001/dl" title="U.S. Department of Justice and Federal Bureau of Investigation, “U.S. Department of Justice / Federal Bureau of Investigation memorandum on Jeffrey Epstein”, 2025-07-06 (release date; no date printed on the memorandum; release date identified in evidence/opinion_tracey_newsletter_concealing.txt)">[3]</a>.</p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose H.R.4405?

**Bill description:** H.R.4405 requires the U.S. Attorney General to publicly release all unclassified Department of Justice records about Jeffrey Epstein, including flight logs, names of government officials, and records of his death in custody. It may withhold victims' personal information, active investigations, and properly classified national-security material.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Judged as of November 18, 2025, the bill would require the Justice Department to “make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format all unclassified records, documents, communications, and investigative materials” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a> about Epstein and Maxwell within “30 days after the date of enactment of this Act” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>. Subject to its listed exceptions, it bars withholding for “embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[1]</a>.</p></div>
