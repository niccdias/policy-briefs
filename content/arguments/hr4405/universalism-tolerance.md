## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.4405?

**Bill description:** H.R.4405 requires the U.S. Attorney General to publicly release all unclassified Department of Justice records about Jeffrey Epstein, including flight logs, names of government officials, and records of his death in custody. It may withhold victims' personal information, active investigations, and properly classified national-security material.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> As of November 18, 2025, the bill would add a deadline for the Justice Department to publish its Epstein and Maxwell files. Existing freedom-of-information law allowed keeping law enforcement information private when release “could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion of personal privacy” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260923012059/https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title5/html/USCODE-2024-title5-partI-chap5-subchapII-sec552.htm" title="United States Congress; Office of the Law Revision Counsel / U.S. Government Publishing Office, “5 U.S.C. 552 — Public information; agency rules, opinions, orders, records, and proceedings (2024 edition)”, 2024 edition; section last amended 2016-06-30">[1]</a>. The bill requires the department to “make publicly available in a searchable and downloadable format all unclassified records, documents, communications, and investigative materials” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[2]</a>, with a deadline of “Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment of this Act” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029202828/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr4405/BILLS-119hr4405eh.htm" title="H.R.4405, bill text, November 18, 2025">[2]</a> and specified withholding exceptions.</p></div>
