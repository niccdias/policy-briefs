<p><strong>S.2 · the Secure America Act</strong></p><p><span data-release-part="119_S_2:description">The bill would give about $70 billion more to federal agencies, in addition to money they already have. The money could be used until September 30, 2029. It would go mainly to two agencies in the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Customs and Border Protection (CBP) guards the borders and includes the Border Patrol. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) arrests, holds and deports (“removes”) noncitizens. Some money would go to Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), the part of ICE that investigates crimes, for work other than immigration and customs enforcement. The bill would pay for officers and staff, detention and deportation operations, border technology, and other enforcement work.</span></p><p><strong>Comparison:</strong> <span data-release-part="119_S_2:comparison">If this bill took effect on June 9, 2026, versus law in force then</span></p><details><summary>Bill version and evidence cutoff</summary><p>Senate-engrossed · 2026-06-05. Evidence cutoff: 2026-07-17.</p><a href="https://niccdias.github.io/policy-briefs/arguments/s2/universalism-nature">Bill and sources</a></details>
<h2>Tradition</h2><ul data-release-part="S2_TR:value"><li>to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking</li><li>to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion</li><li>to honor the traditional practices of their culture</li></ul><p><span data-release-part="S2_TR:reference">Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference. Tradition here concerns preserving, observing or passing down identifiable inherited customs, religious practices and ways of life, valued for their continuity with the past. Religious endorsement of a general moral goal alone does not establish that connection.</span></p>
