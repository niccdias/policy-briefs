**Comparison:** If this bill took effect on June 9, 2026, versus law in force then. **Bill text:** Senate-engrossed, June 5, 2026. **Evidence cutoff:** July 17, 2026.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.2?
2. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.2?
3. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to honor the traditional practices of their culture. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose S.2?

**Reference:** Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference.

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor S.2?

**Bill description:** S.2 provides about $70 billion through 2029 for immigration enforcement and border security. It increases the number of Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers; expands resources for detention and deportation operations; invests in border security technology; and supports other federal immigration enforcement activities.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> S.2 adds funds through September 2029 to what the law in force on June 9, 2026, already provided: “In addition to amounts otherwise available” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a>. The Congressional Research Service describes their uses: “This bill provides funding to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) through FY2029 for immigration enforcement, border security, and related activities.” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260705212432/https://www.govinfo.gov/bulkdata/BILLSUM/119/s/BILLSUM-119s2.xml" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, June 9, 2026">[2]</a></p></div>

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to oppose S.2?

**Bill description:** S.2 provides about $70 billion through 2029 for immigration enforcement and border security. It increases the number of Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers; expands resources for detention and deportation operations; invests in border security technology; and supports other federal immigration enforcement activities.

<div class="argument-main-effect"><p><span class="lbl">Main effect</span> Compared with the law in force on June 9, 2026, S.2 adds resources for the existing immigration arrest, detention and deportation system. Section 202 gives Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) “$31,075,000,000, to remain available until September 30, 2029” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a>, including “operations and maintenance for its immigration enforcement functions” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a> and “transportation costs and related costs associated with alien departure or removal operations” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a>. ICE’s enforcement work includes “the identification, arrest, detention, and removal of noncitizens” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029220230/https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R49032/R49032.2.pdf" title="Congressional Research Service, report R49032, July 14, 2026">[17]</a>. Section 201 gives Customs and Border Protection “$13,020,000,000, to remain available until September 30, 2029” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a> “in order to carry out immigration enforcement activities” <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261029205704/https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s2/BILLS-119s2es.htm" title="S.2, bill text, June 5, 2026">[1]</a>.
