> [!danger] Arguments against
> - [[claims/victim_limits|The bill lets the Attorney General withhold victims' information only within limited categories.]] <span class="tag dis">Disputed: D3 (partly settled)</span>
>   > [!quote]- Who says this (1)
>   > - *Document inserted into the Record by Johnson of Louisiana, House floor, November 18, 2025 (author unknown)*: “While H.R. 4405 permits the Attorney General to withhold “personally identifiable information of [Epstein's] victims” and “personal and medical files and similar files,” this authority is limited to disclosures that “constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy” [[sources/insert_floor_03_008_01#q3|↗ source]]
>   
>   > [!info]- What the record shows (partly settled)
>   > Bill text: “The Attorney general may withhold or redact the segregable portions of records that-- (A) contain personally identifiable information of victims or victims' personal and medical files and similar files the disclosure of which would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy;” [[sources/bill_text#q1|↗ source]]
>   >
>   > The bill permits withholding or redacting parts of records on five specific grounds, including victims' identifying information and personal and medical files, which fits Position 2's claim of narrow redactions. Position 1 holds in part because the power is optional and a privacy test may narrow it; the wording cuts against Position 3, but DOJ's January and February letters give no count of the whole records it withheld.
