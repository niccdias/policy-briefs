**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** Each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [NAME].

1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid disease and protect their health. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to be personally safe and secure. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
1. Imagine a person named [NAME]. It's very important to [NAME] to avoid anything dangerous. Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

## Does this value give [NAME] a reason to favor H.R.5408?

<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> The bill creates a path to a first labor contract that both sides must follow: “This bill establishes mandatory deadlines for parties negotiating an initial collective bargaining agreement (CBA) and provides for mediation and arbitration to finalize CBAs.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408" title="Congressional Research Service, bill summary, June 9, 2026">[1]</a> If talks and mediation fail, a three-person panel settles the dispute: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a> This changes the law in force on June 9, 2026, which “does not compel either party to agree to a proposal” <a href="https://www.littler.com/files/littler_on_collective_bargaining.pdf" title="Blomberg, Angela, “Littler on Collective Bargaining”, 2024 edition">[3]</a>. The bill also requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> Faster first contracts bring enforceable workplace safety rules within reach sooner.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> First contracts secure safety rules that workers can enforce through complaints. A study of 16 contracts from 2010–2014 found: “Most contracts detail the employer’s responsibility to provide and maintain protective clothing and equipment.” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4880255/?pdf=0" title="Jenn Hagedorn, Claudia Alexandra Paras, Howard Greenwich, Amy Hagopian, “The Role of Labor Unions in Creating Working Conditions That Promote Public Health”, 2016-04-14 (published online; June 2016 issue)">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Unions also help workers report hazards without retaliation: “They bargain to improve safety practices, protect their members against retaliation for reporting a violation, or make complaints on members’ behalf about hazards” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Labor-Unions-And-The-Middle-Class.pdf" title="U.S. DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY, “Labor Unions and the Middle Class”, August 2023 (released 2023-08-28)">[6]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The bill’s binding route to a first contract helps workers secure and enforce these safeguards sooner. Protection from hazards serves [NAME]’s aim to avoid injury and illness at work.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Safety clauses in successful bargains do not establish which added safeguards the bill brings forward in disputes that would otherwise remain unsettled.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The first-contract study collected agreements already reached, including contracts “in units where the first contract was reached” <a href="https://research.upjohn.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1168&amp;context=up_press&amp;filename=4&amp;type=additional" title="“Significant Victories: An Analysis of Union First Contracts”, 2006 (chapter 5, Justice on the Job, pp. 87–114)">[4]</a>. It explains that “the quality of the first contract that a union achieves is a direct product of their power” <a href="https://research.upjohn.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1168&amp;context=up_press&amp;filename=4&amp;type=additional" title="“Significant Victories: An Analysis of Union First Contracts”, 2006 (chapter 5, Justice on the Job, pp. 87–114)">[4]</a>. Its clause counts measure the results of successful bargaining, not the terms produced by changing the settlement process.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The 16-contract study includes “restatements of existing city, state, and federal laws” <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4880255/?pdf=0" title="Jenn Hagedorn, Claudia Alexandra Paras, Howard Greenwich, Amy Hagopian, “The Role of Labor Unions in Creating Working Conditions That Promote Public Health”, 2016-04-14 (published online; June 2016 issue)">[5]</a>. For equipment required by federal safety rules, employers are already “responsible for providing at no cost to their employees” <a href="https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/federalregister/2007-11-15-0" title="“Employer Payment for Personal Protective Equipment; Final Rule”, 2007-11-15">[7]</a>, subject to the rule’s exceptions. An equipment clause therefore need not create a new equipment duty.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Contract language and its use are separate. The first-contract study says, “we have not gathered data on the operation and effectiveness of the local unions” <a href="https://research.upjohn.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1168&amp;context=up_press&amp;filename=4&amp;type=additional" title="“Significant Victories: An Analysis of Union First Contracts”, 2006 (chapter 5, Justice on the Job, pp. 87–114)">[4]</a>. It does not measure whether those clauses changed hazards or how well workers could enforce them.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The missing link is whether this bill brings added safety rights or stronger enforcement forward. Existing equipment duties, clause counts, and unions’ general reporting work do not establish that change.</li></ul></td></tr>

Preserving those protections serves [NAME]’s personal health and safety.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> No sound rebuttal to this point is supported by the sources.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing law allows an employer to change terms after a genuine deadlock: “When parties reach impasse, the employer may implement its final offer.” <a href="https://www.littler.com/files/littler_on_collective_bargaining.pdf" title="Blomberg, Angela, “Littler on Collective Bargaining”, 2024 edition">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill instead requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. <tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> Earlier contract-backed health benefits make medical care easier to afford.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> First contracts secure health coverage. The presence of an insurance clause does not measure newly gained coverage or a reduction in workers’ medical bills.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Treasury’s health-insurance finding follows workers “before and after they join a union” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Labor-Unions-And-The-Middle-Class.pdf" title="U.S. DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY, “Labor Unions and the Middle Class”, August 2023 (released 2023-08-28)">[6]</a>. It does not compare earlier and later first contracts for workers whose union already exists.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Treasury’s concrete estimate of added employer contributions at newly unionized firms concerns “employees’ pension plans” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Labor-Unions-And-The-Middle-Class.pdf" title="U.S. DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY, “Labor Unions and the Middle Class”, August 2023 (released 2023-08-28)">[6]</a>. That estimate does not measure health premiums or treatment costs.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The PRO treats evidence about insurance clauses and joining a union as evidence of medical savings from this bill’s faster process. The needed comparison is missing.</li></ul></td></tr>

NLRB, 200 F.3d 436”, Decided and filed December 8, 1999; rehearing en banc denied February 17, 2000">[33]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> At that point, “an employer does not violate the Act by making unilateral changes” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/200/436/521476/" title="“Loral Defense Systems–Akron and Aircraft Braking Systems Corp. v. NLRB, 200 F.3d 436”, Decided and filed December 8, 1999; rehearing en banc denied February 17, 2000">[33]</a> that are “consistent with the offers the Union has rejected.” <a href="https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/200/436/521476/" title="“Loral Defense Systems–Akron and Aircraft Braking Systems Corp. v. NLRB, 200 F.3d 436”, Decided and filed December 8, 1999; rehearing en banc denied February 17, 2000">[33]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> A vote that blocks a binding agreement need not block the working conditions that threaten health. The PRO must connect the lost approval vote to lost protection against those conditions; its examples and medical evidence do not establish that connection.</li></ul></td></tr>
