**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Survey question:** each respondent saw one of these three versions, with a randomly assigned name in place of [name]. The arguments call this person Morgan.

1. Imagine a person named [name]. It's very important to [name] that the weak and vulnerable in society be protected. Does this value give [name] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
1. Imagine a person named [name]. It's very important to [name] that every person in the world have equal opportunities in life. Does this value give [name] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?
1. Imagine a person named [name]. It's very important to [name] that everyone be treated justly, even people they don't know. Does this value give [name] a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?

Answer options: A reason to favor H.R.5408 / A reason to oppose H.R.5408 / Both / Neither

## Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor H.R.5408?

<tr><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> Universalism – Concern gives Morgan a reason to favor H.R.5408 because it protects workers with less power and widens access to contracts that support just treatment and equal opportunities.</p></td><td><p><strong>Claim:</strong> The PRO does not establish that the bill's first-contract settlement process would deliver the wider protection, fair treatment and equal opportunities claimed in points 4–14.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> The bill adds deadlines, mediation and binding arbitration for a first union contract. Mediation means outside help to reach agreement. Arbitration means a panel decides the terms: “A majority of the 3-person arbitration panel shall render a decision settling the dispute and such decision shall be binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years, unless amended during such period by written consent of the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a> Under the law in force on June 9, 2026, good-faith talks were required, but “it does not impose any deadline or mandate that the parties achieve a collective bargaining agreement.” <a href="https://www.heritage.org/government-regulation/report/labor-law-reform-part-1-diagnosing-the-issues-exploring-current" title="Heritage Foundation, “Labor Law Reform Part 1: Diagnosing the Issues, Exploring Current Proposals | The Heritage Foundation”, October 9, 2025">[2]</a> The bill adds this first-contract process: - <strong>Because:</strong> Talks must start within 10 days of a written request, unless both sides agree to more time: “Not later than 10 days after receiving a written request for collective bargaining from an individual or labor organization that has been newly recognized or certified as a representative as provided under section 9(a), or within such further period as the parties agree upon” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>. - <strong>Because:</strong> Either side can seek mediation after 90 days without agreement, unless both sides extend that period: “If after the expiration of the 90-day period beginning on the date on which bargaining is commenced, or such additional period as the parties may agree upon, the parties have failed to reach an agreement, either party may notify the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service that a dispute exists, and may request mediation.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a> - <strong>Because:</strong> After 30 days from that request without agreement, the mediation service must refer the dispute to a panel, unless both sides extend the time: “If after the expiration of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the request for mediation is made under subparagraph (B), or such additional period as the parties may agree upon, the Service is not able to bring the parties to agreement by conciliation, the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</p></td><td><p><strong>Main effect:</strong> Not disputed.</p></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 1:</strong> It gives workers with less power a way past an employer's use of delay.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Treasury's research review explains the power gap: “employers typically have the upper hand in the relationship with their employees, even in the absence of anti-competitive practices.” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Labor-Unions-And-The-Middle-Class.pdf" title="U.S. DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY, “Labor Unions and the Middle Class”, August 2023">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Congress identifies how delay deepens that gap: “The employers can use those delays to sap labor organization resolve and secure more favorable terms for the employer.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The process above lets either side seek a binding settlement. Neither side can block the panel merely by refusing to choose its members: “if the individual or labor organization or the employer fail to do so, the Service shall designate any members not selected by the individual or labor organization or by the employer.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Both sides have a place on the panel: “with one member selected by the individual or labor organization, one member selected by the employer, and one neutral member mutually agreed to by the parties.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers gain a settlement route beyond persuading the stronger party to agree. Making their right to bargain usable protects people with less power and supports just treatment.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO has not shown that workers with little bargaining power could use the new settlement route given the mediation agency's documented service limits.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill depends on the Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service (FMCS): “the Service shall refer the dispute to a 3-person arbitration panel” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> FMCS's April 3, 2026 budget submission says it had limited mediation to health-care groups with at least 250 represented workers and “bargaining units of 1,000 members or more for all other industries in which it has jurisdiction” <a href="https://www.fmcs.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2027-Congressional-Budget.pdf" title="Michelle Batts-Dunkins, “Fiscal Year 2027 Congressional Budget Submission”, April 3, 2026">[4]</a>.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Its proposed service form, published May 15, still says of first-contract cases: “These cases will be assigned to a mediator if they meet the current criteria/metrics for case assignment.” <a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/15/2026-09783/service-request-form" title="Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service; Federal Register, “Service Request Form, 91 FR 27942, Document 2026-09783”, May 15, 2026 (signed May 13, 2026)">[5]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A supporter of the bill identifies the practical condition: “Without a robust, government-supported mediation and arbitration system, the Faster Labor Contracts Act would not be able to live up to its promise.” <a href="https://www.epi.org/blog/the-missing-piece-in-the-senate-committee-hearing-on-the-challenges-facing-newly-unionized-workers/" title="Economic Policy Institute, “The missing piece in the Senate committee hearing on the challenges facing newly unionized workers | Economic Policy Institute”, October 23, 2025">[6]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> Preventing an employer from blocking appointments does not resolve this service gap. The PRO needs a working route for the workers it says will gain protection; the new legal duty alone does not establish that capacity.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 2:</strong> It requires a worker election before the employer's duty to bargain ends.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing rules allow withdrawal of union recognition after a contract expires when evidence shows lost majority support and the union has not requested an election in time: “If no such petition is timely filed, the employer may rely on the disaffection evidence to affect withdrawal. That evidence—assuming it establishes actual loss of majority status—will be dispositive of the union’s lack of majority status at the time of actual withdrawal; and the withdrawal of recognition will be lawful assuming no other grounds exist to find it unlawful.” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-july-1-5-2019" title="National Labor Relations Board, “Summary of NLRB Decisions for Week of July 1 - 5, 2019”, Week of July 1–5, 2019; Johnson Controls decision dated July 3, 2019">[7]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires that “an employer's duty to collectively bargain shall continue absent decertification of the representative following an election conducted pursuant to section 9” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers retain collective representation until an election removes it. Requiring a vote before ending that protection gives workers a fairer process, serving Morgan's concern for just treatment.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> The PRO does not show why requiring continued recognition when a union does not use an existing election route gives workers a fairer process.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Unions newly approved by the federal labor board already receive a protected first year: “An employer must bargain in good faith during these first 12 months and cannot withdraw recognition of the union, even if the union allegedly loses majority support through no fault of the employer.” <a href="https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dc-circuit/115888928.html" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit; opinion by Senior Circuit Judge Harry T. Edwards; reproduced by FindLaw, “J.G. Kern Enterprises, Inc. v. National Labor Relations Board, No. 22-1287”, March 1, 2024">[8]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO's source concerns withdrawal near an existing contract's expiry. It requires evidence that “establishes actual loss of majority status” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-july-1-5-2019" title="National Labor Relations Board, “Summary of NLRB Decisions for Week of July 1 - 5, 2019”, Week of July 1–5, 2019; Johnson Controls decision dated July 3, 2019">[7]</a>. It also gives the union an election route: “the incumbent union may file, within 45 days from the date of that announcement, an election petition” <a href="https://www.nlrb.gov/cases-decisions/weekly-summaries-decisions/summary-of-nlrb-decisions-for-week-of-july-1-5-2019" title="National Labor Relations Board, “Summary of NLRB Decisions for Week of July 1 - 5, 2019”, Week of July 1–5, 2019; Johnson Controls decision dated July 3, 2019">[7]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The comparison already includes protection for newly certified workers and a vote when an incumbent union requests one. The PRO does not explain why compulsory recognition when that election route goes unused treats workers' expressed wishes more fairly.</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><p><strong>Point 3:</strong> It protects existing pay and conditions while workers seek an agreement, including after a genuine deadlock.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing law lets an employer impose previously offered terms after good-faith talks reach a deadlock: “the employer may unilaterally change terms and conditions of employ, so long as these changes are consistent with offers that the union has rejected.” <a href="https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/22/04/212188P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit; Judge Loken, “National Labor Relations Board v. Noah’s Ark Processors, LLC, No. 21-2188”, 2022-04-22">[9]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill instead requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Workers gain protection against cuts while seeking a contract. This protects people whose households cannot absorb lower pay or worse hours.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> No supported rebuttal to this point survives review; the draft challenges protection after settlement, which the PRO does not claim here.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> The court source explains that, after a genuine bargaining deadlock, “the employer may unilaterally change terms and conditions of employ, so long as these changes are consistent with offers that the union has rejected.” <a href="https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/22/04/212188P.pdf" title="U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit; Judge Loken, “National Labor Relations Board v. Noah’s Ark Processors, LLC, No. 21-2188”, 2022-04-22">[9]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill instead requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Existing bans on changes during bad-faith bargaining do not answer the PRO's point about a genuine deadlock. <tr><td><p><strong>Point 4:</strong> It strengthens workers' route to pay that supports their families and opens life opportunities.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Treasury's research review finds a wage gain from union membership: “Unions raise the wages of their members by 10 to 15 percent.” <a href="https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Labor-Unions-And-The-Middle-Class.pdf" title="U.S. DEPARTMENT OF THE TREASURY, “Labor Unions and the Middle Class”, August 2023">[3]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill requires any panel decision to consider “the employees' cost of living” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a> and “the employees' ability to sustain themselves, their families, and their dependents on the wages and benefits they earn from the employer” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[1]</a>.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> The binding-contract route gives workers stronger access to wage settlements, with basic living needs expressly counted. Workers retain the route to vote a union out.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The PRO’s own legal account calls an election “the preferred method for determining employees’ representational preferences.” <a href="https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/nlrb-streamlines-process-for-employers-to-withdraw-union-recognition-1" title="“NLRB Streamlines Process For Employers To Withdraw Union Recognition”, 2019-07-09">[31]</a></li><li><strong>Because:</strong> A signature is not always a worker’s last word. Describing workers who signed conflicting petitions, labor lawyers explain that a secret ballot allows a final choice under anonymity, “insulating them from retaliation from whichever side they fail to support.” <a href="https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/board-ends-unions-gotcha-strategy-to-counter-anticipatory-withdrawal-of-recognition/" title="“Board Ends Union’s “Gotcha” Strategy To Counter Anticipatory Withdrawal Of Recognition”, 2019-07-31">[33]</a></li><li><strong>So:</strong> The election requirement checks majority wishes through a private choice by workers, rather than leaving the employer to act on signatures. Just treatment includes protecting that choice for every worker. The PRO shows that employer withdrawal can be quicker, but speed alone does not establish that verifying the majority’s wishes is unfair.</li></ul></td></tr>
