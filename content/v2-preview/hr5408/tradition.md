**Bill:** [H.R.5408](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5408), the Faster Labor Contracts Act. Judged as of June 9, 2026, when Congress last voted on it.

**Value:** Tradition. A person who holds this value finds these things very important:

- to maintain traditional values and ways of thinking
- to follow their family's customs or the customs of a religion
- to honor the traditional practices of their culture

*Read this value as held by a Christian traditionalist in the United States, with the religious right as the cultural reference.*

**Survey question:** “Imagine a person named Morgan. It's very important to Morgan [statement]. Does this value give Morgan a reason to favor or oppose H.R.5408?” Each respondent saw one of the statements above.

The study cannot establish the claimed advance in Christian peacemaking.</li></ul></td></tr>

The bill removes their collective vote as a way to keep shared health coverage in line with inherited Christian teaching.</li></ul></td><td><p><strong>Response:</strong> Losing a contract vote does not by itself establish the claimed increase in faith-conflicting insurance payments.</p><ul><li><strong>Because:</strong> Under existing law, a genuine deadlock after good-faith bargaining can already lead to employers making “unilateral changes by imposing their last best, final offer” <a href="https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/23/23-60132-CV0.pdf" title="“Thryv, Inc. v. National Labor Relations Board, No. 23-60132”, 2024-05-24">[41]</a>. Rejecting a proposed contract does not give workers final control over every workplace term.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> The bill also requires employers “to maintain current wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment pending an agreement” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a>. Its effect on coverage must be compared with the existing route through employer-imposed terms after a genuine deadlock.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> AFT’s resolution says it “will advocate for transgender-inclusive healthcare” <a href="https://www.aft.org/resolution/bargaining-lgbtq-equity-health-benefits-and-working-conditions" title="“Bargaining LGBTQ Equity for Health Benefits and Working Conditions”, 2017 (resolution year stated in source)">[39]</a>. Advocacy is a bargaining demand; it does not decide the panel’s award.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> Existing religious-freedom law also governs federal compulsion. The Congressional Research Service explains that “all prospective federal action is subject to RFRA's provisions” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11490" title="“The Religious Freedom Restoration Act: A Primer”, 2020-04-03">[42]</a>. RFRA means the Religious Freedom Restoration Act.</li><li><strong>Because:</strong> For federal action that places a substantial burden on religious practice, “the action is valid only if the government shows that the burden is (1) in furtherance of a compelling governmental interest and (2) the least restrictive means of furthering that interest.” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11490" title="“The Religious Freedom Restoration Act: A Primer”, 2020-04-03">[42]</a> The government must justify the burden and show why a less restrictive approach would not meet its need.</li><li><strong>So:</strong> Making an award “binding upon the parties for a period of 2 years” <a href="https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr5408/BILLS-119hr5408eh.htm" title="H.R.5408, bill text, June 9, 2026">[2]</a> does not by itself override that religious-freedom test. The PRO has not shown how replacing the vote with this process creates added exposure to forbidden payments under the law already in force.</li></ul></td></tr>
