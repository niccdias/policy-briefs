# S.2 guided-decision package: editorial review

Author: Opus editorial author. Built only from the frozen `input/source.json`. No outside source was read and no fact was checked anew. `author.py` (with `author_lib.py`) is the maintained generator. It copies every citation link from the frozen field it names. Separately from the runtime build, the generator checks every reader-visible quotation and every locator as an exact substring of its named field.

## Shape

- 5 contexts, not asked:
  - Section 102, Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) money and its child-exploitation set-aside (C18, C19).
  - Sections 101 and 103, Border Patrol duties other than immigration, aircraft, boats and equipment (C20–C22, C29).
  - Section 201, CBP immigration enforcement staff (C23).
  - Section 202, ICE uses (C17, C24–C28, C30).
  - Money available until 2029 (C14).

  Each context's scope limits are qualification blocks shown beside every decision that relies on that context.
- 90 causal claims, 31 value links and 223 blocks. `coverage.json` places all 98 arguments, all 71 responses (every one retained) and all 92 qualifications.

## Causal claims

Each prediction about what the bill would do is asked. Three shared roots carry most of the enforcement and investigation arguments:

- Added immigration enforcement beyond the law in force (Sections 201 and 202 only; not the investigation, rescue, fire-warning or drug-inspection money).
- Added HSI time for criminal investigations.
- Public-facing tactics continuing.

A fourth root, less yearly leverage over conditions and conduct, is independent of all three. The root for added immigration enforcement carries the "money already in law" limits from the context notes: the 2025 budget law amounts, the $62 billion not yet committed, the 11,000 hires, "base operations", and the June 9 offset plans. A No on it sets aside 46 decisions. The root for added HSI time carries the shared staff-reassignment challenge (C01, C31–C35) once. A No on it sets aside 17.

## Consolidation and splitting

Value links are merged with OR routes when arguments make the same, precisely scoped value connection in the same value and direction. Each route keeps its own causal claim, evidence, challenges and limits. A merged value page carries only a route-neutral bridge block, with two exceptions. First, a provision route whose evidence is statutory, such as the release limit. Second, a challenge that applies to every route. Where an accepted route is added work or capability rather than a realized result, the bridge says so explicitly, so that the value question asks about that added work, not about results no one was asked to accept. Merged wording lists the alternatives and states that each needs its own claim. Thirteen value links have more than one route:

- **Nature:**
  - Seven routes protect wild land, water and wildlife: wild-area crossings, towers, invasive species, wildfire, fire warnings, fishing and oil spills.
  - Two support conservation work.
  - Six oppose damage: flights, facility wildlife harm, facility air pollution, sewer, off-road travel and forest crews.
- **Concern:**
  - Eight protection routes, covering child rescue, guardianship, trafficking, victims, scams, terrorism, cyber and violent offenders.
  - Three life-and-health routes.
  - Four justice routes.
  - Seven harm routes, including the yearly-leverage claim and the raid-disruption claim.
  - Freedom has two routes: detaining people who pose no threat, or the release limit as a provision.
- **Tolerance:**
  - Persecution has three routes.
  - Groups has two: race-based stops or blocked minority worship.
  - Neighbors has two: the claim, or the provision.
  - Dissent has two: force or intimidation of critics.
- **Personal Security:**
  - Health: three routes.
  - Safety: six routes; the gang route asks whether HSI or local-partner work would stop gang violence.
  - Harm to safety: three routes.
  - Harm to health: five routes.
- **Societal Security:**
  - Borders: four routes (ready workforce, detection, response, identity checks).
  - Threats: twelve routes.
  - Protection: six routes, including the yearly-leverage claim.
  - Stability: seven routes.
- **Tradition:** worship, congregations, ministry and pastoral care share one church-practice link.

Some links were kept separate because the value connection differs:

- **Tradition:** family transmission of faith (TR_oppose_01) stays separate from church practice; it rests on the family as the setting for teaching. Persecution abroad (TR_oppose_07) is a loss of freedom to practise at all, not disruption of an established local practice.
- **Concern:** fair review of officers through cameras (UNC_support_11), worker safety gear (UNC_support_13) and justice for atrocities abroad (UNC_support_05) each judge a different object of concern.
- **Tolerance:** victims (UNT_support_03–04) stays separate from persecution, everyday contact across differences under 287(g) (UNT_oppose_03) and hearing people facing deportation (UNT_oppose_04).
- **Personal and Societal Security:** these links stay separate for support and oppose, and for one's own health versus safety.

Splits made because one part needed a claim the other did not:

- **SES_support_02:** the ledger has two paths (surveillance; aircraft and boats). Detection and response are now separate OR claims, so a reader who accepts one is not forced to reject both.
- **SES_oppose_08:** "Both bear directly on the strong, secure country". Weaker essential services, a local claim, and a smaller national economy and public finances, a national claim, are now separate OR routes. A No on the national step therefore keeps the essential-services route. The C13 general-link limit sits at the national claim.
- **SEP_support_06:** the ledger's second path, Section 203 support for local crime prevention, "without HSI staff", is its own claim. It no longer passes through added HSI time.
- **SES_oppose_07:** the KFF avoidance route, which is local in the ledger, is its own fear claim, alongside the shared raids claim.

## Sensitive choices for main

- **Provision versus prediction:**
  - **Prediction claims:**
    - TR_oppose_02 and TR_oppose_04, plus SES_oppose_07's KFF route, have local (empty) ledger paths. Each still predicts an effect of added enforcement: fear at church, interrupted ministries, avoided work and school. So each is asked as a causal claim that depends on added enforcement, not left resting on the provision. This means a No on added enforcement sets them aside.
    - SES_support_01, the ready workforce, and SES_oppose_11, added debt pressure, are asked as root claims rather than read off the statute. Both depend on predicted effects.
  - **Provision routes:**
    - UNC_oppose_01's release limit is a provision route.
    - So are UNT_oppose_02's neighbors and UNT_oppose_04's hearing for people facing deportation.
    - The two yearly-leverage routes (UNC_oppose_04, SES_oppose_01) are no longer provision routes; see S2-AUDIT-01 below.
- **National versus local:**
  - SES_oppose_08 is split as above.
  - The Societal Security stability link otherwise mixes community-level claims (raids, avoided daily life, sewer capacity, care avoidance) with one national claim, debt pressure. Each is its own route.
  - The rescues claim was widened from "near the border" to rescues "or other emergency help" so that SES_support_14's emergency support fits. Every link that uses it now carries the same inclusive wording (S2-AUDIT-03).
- **Scope of shared claims:**
  - The critics claim now covers observers (SES_oppose_06's database threat).
  - SES_support_04's two ledger paths reduce to the scanner claim, because the weaker path needs only that claim.
  - SES_support_06 cites gang cases as well as terrorism, although its ledger path names only C31. Both HSI lines are kept as alternatives.
- **Fixes and reassignments:**
  - The clergy-access response's documented fixes (Broadview, Whipple, Roybal) are a challenge at the clergy-access claim. They are a limit on prediction, not a reason to drop it.
  - The responses to unsafe returns and congregation closure stay at their claims, with their partial-answer clauses intact.
  - All HSI responses keep "does not show that the money cannot reach this work". The reassignment evidence is judged once at the shared HSI-time root, and each line's own response sits at its line.
- **Dates:** legal and program facts are as dated in the source. No later updating was done.

## Checks run

- **Runtime builder:** `runtime/build.mjs` `buildBill` built the package without error. Its checks cover HTML, source-ref locators and their citation pairs, coverage, parity and paths. It does not by itself audit every reader-visible quotation; that audit is the generator's, below, as in the common procedure.
- **Path counts:**
  - All Yes: 121 decisions (127 judgments); before the audit batch, 114 (120).
  - All causal No: 32 decisions, with 89 set aside; before, 36 with 78 set aside. The yearly-leverage and raid-disruption reasons no longer survive an all-No traversal.
  - Single No on added immigration enforcement: sets aside 46 (before, 40). The six added are the flights, facility-operation, facility-wildlife, facility-air and forest-crew claims, and the raid-disruption claim.
  - Single No on HSI time: sets aside 17 (before, 15).
  - Single No on scanners, or on drug supply: sets aside 3 each (the fentanyl claim, the opioid-family claim and the Tradition link).
  - Single No on yearly leverage: sets aside nothing; both value links stay reachable through their other routes.
  - Single No on wild-area crossings: sets aside 3.
- **Generator self-checks:**
  - Unique prefixed IDs, no cycles, and every block used.
  - Routes require only causal nodes.
  - Argument reciprocity holds.
  - Every retained response and qualification is bound to a decision that shows its block.
  - Every locator and reader-visible quotation is found in its source field.
  - Each qualification row credits only blocks shown on decisions that carry one of that row's arguments.
- **Route check:**
  - Every argument on a value link has at least one route whose claims all carry that argument (or an empty route).
  - Every causal route on a value link is carried by at least one of the link's arguments.
  - Arguments on a claim are propagated to every claim it requires.
- **Manual pass on OR pages:** each always-shown block on every multi-route value page was read. The only non-bridge blocks are:
  - statutory provision evidence for the release limit;
  - the ICE scope;
  - the freedom and neighbors value challenges, which apply to every route.

  No route-specific factual evidence sits on a merged page. Bridges that join work or capability routes with result routes say explicitly which routes accepted only added work or capability.

## Audit corrections (S2-AUDIT-01 to 07)

- **01, yearly leverage:** a new root claim, "Less yearly leverage over conditions and conduct", asks whether S.2's money through 2029 would further weaken Congress's yearly lever compared with the 2025 law. The full baseline sits at that claim: $45 billion and $29.85 billion through fiscal year 2029, $62 billion uncommitted, and the June 9 offsets. Both leverage routes (Concern harms; Societal Security protection) now require it. It does not pass through the tactics or detention-conditions claims. Funding duration stays as context.
- **02, work versus results:** each HSI line was reviewed against its argument's own conclusion.
  - Where the argument's value step rests on a realized result, that result is now asked as its own claim:
    - trafficking victims found and brought to safety (UNC_support_03, SEP_support_05);
    - perpetrators of atrocities held responsible (UNC_support_05, UNT_support_01);
    - less gang violence, by HSI or local-partner routes, kept independent (SEP_support_06).
  - Two single-use lines now carry their result in the claim itself: pills taken off sale (SEP_support_02); criminal money seized (SES_support_09). The persecutor-case claim also includes removal or accountability.
  - Where the argument's value step is the added capability (SES_support_06, 07, 10–13; UNC_support_04, 08, 09; UNT_support_02), the link and bridge now say "more investigative work" and state that the accepted step is added work, not crimes stopped.
  - The oil-spill route now reads as added occasional ability to help coordinate cleanup, not better-contained spills.
  - No HSI parent was broadened, and no mechanism was made to depend on another.
- **03, rescues:** both the Personal Security safety link and the Societal Security threats link, with their subjects and bridge, now say "rescues or other emergency help", matching the claim.
- **04, drugs:** two narrower claims now follow scanners + supply. "Less fentanyl reaching people" gates the Personal Security health route. "Less opioid abuse separating children from parents" gates the Tradition link. The TR response's challenge to the detection-to-abuse step sits at the opioid claim. The Tradition link keeps only the family-faith premise. The broad Concern route still uses the general supply claim.
- **05, raids:** a new claim, "Added community disruption from those raids", follows the large-raids claim. It holds the Minnesota harms and the winding-down response. The large-raids claim keeps the C37 evidence about limits to the reported change. Both value links now require the disruption claim. The KFF fear route stays independent and depends only on added immigration enforcement.
- **06, omissions restored:**
  - The reporting challenge now includes the null victimization result for non-Latino Whites and Blacks.
  - The care-supply limit now includes KFF's 2025–2026 health-care employment figures and the lack of local staffing series.
  - The scanner challenge now includes the inspector general's acquisition-policy and planning findings.
  - The camera limit now says the AP accounts were seen only in search extracts.
  - Coverage rows credit these locations.
- **07, immigration-scoped root:** the root claim is now "Added immigration enforcement beyond the law in force", limited to Sections 201 and 202. The flights, facility-operation and forest-crew claims now require it; the facility wildlife and air claims follow through facility operation. Off-road travel, sewer capacity, drones, surveillance, wild-area patrols, towers and land officers were left as they were, for the reasons the ledger gives.
- **Generator change:** qualification rows now credit only blocks shown on decisions that carry one of the row's own arguments. This removed credits for the shared "money already in law" context note at decisions on other arguments' paths. No block text changed for this.
- **08, surveillance baseline:** the shared "money already in law" note (SESCTX_LIM) shown at the surveillance, sewer and debt claims now also includes two points: about $62 billion of ICE's 2025 money was still uncommitted at the end of May 2026, and unspent money does not by itself show that S.2 adds nothing. Both now carry locators to UNC_oppose_09's note. UNC_oppose_09's qualification row, credited at the surveillance claim, is therefore shown in full there. The surveillance claim still does not depend on added immigration enforcement; no other node or block changed.

## Limits

Which claims became questions, where limits sit and which value links were merged are editorial judgments for main and the independent verifier. Structural and quotation checks do not establish that any inference is valid or any prediction true. Human comprehension, reading time and workload were not tested.
